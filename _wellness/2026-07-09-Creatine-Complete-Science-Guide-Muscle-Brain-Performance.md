Creatine is the single most researched sports supplement in history, with over 1,000 peer-reviewed studies behind it. And unlike most supplements on store shelves, the science is remarkably consistent: it works. Not just for bodybuilders — for athletes, older adults, and even your brain.

## What Is Creatine?

Creatine is a **naturally occurring compound** made from three amino acids — arginine, glycine, and methionine. Your liver, kidneys, and pancreas produce roughly 1–2 grams per day. You also get small amounts from red meat and fish.

The problem? Dietary and endogenous creatine only fills about 60–80% of your muscle creatine stores. Supplementation saturates those stores, and that's where the benefits begin.

---

## How Creatine Works: The Science

### The ATP-PCr Energy System
Your muscles run on ATP (adenosine triphosphate). During intense effort — a heavy squat, a sprint, a maximal jump — ATP is depleted in 1–3 seconds. Creatine phosphate **donates a phosphate group** to regenerate ATP almost instantly, extending your capacity for explosive effort.

More creatine in muscle = more phosphocreatine = faster ATP regeneration = more reps, more power, better performance.

### Cell Volumization
Creatine pulls water into muscle cells, increasing cell volume. This isn't just cosmetic — **cell swelling is an anabolic signal** that triggers protein synthesis pathways, including mTOR.

### Myosin Heavy Chain Expression
Long-term supplementation has been shown to shift fiber-type expression toward Type II (fast-twitch) fibers — the ones responsible for strength and power.

## Proven Benefits

### 1. Increased Strength and Power
A 2003 meta-analysis in the *Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research* analyzing 22 studies found creatine supplementation increased maximum strength by **8%** and weightlifting performance by **14%** compared to placebo.

### 2. Greater Muscle Mass
Creatine consistently produces more lean mass gains than resistance training alone. A systematic review of 250 studies found an average **2.2 kg greater lean mass** gain with creatine over training alone.

### 3. Enhanced High-Intensity Performance
Repeated sprint ability, cycling power output, and high-intensity interval performance all improve by 5–15% with creatine loading. The benefit is most pronounced in efforts lasting **5–150 seconds**.

### 4. Faster Recovery
Creatine reduces markers of muscle damage (creatine kinase, lactate dehydrogenase) and decreases **delayed onset muscle soreness** (DOMS) after intense training, allowing harder training with less downtime.

### 5. Cognitive Benefits
Emerging research is revealing creatine as a potent **nootropic**:
- A 2003 Aust/NZ *Proceedings of the Royal Society* study found 5g/day for 6 weeks improved **working memory and intelligence** in vegetarians by 20–50%
- Creatine supplementation reduces **mental fatigue** under sleep deprivation
- Recent research links creatine to reduced risk of **depression and anxiety**
- During traumatic brain injury, creatine reduces cognitive damage in animal models

The brain uses enormous amounts of ATP. Creatine buffers that energy system just as it does in muscle.

### 6. Benefits for Older Adults
Creatine is becoming recognized as a key supplement for **healthy aging**:
- Combats sarcopenia (age-related muscle loss) when combined with resistance training
- Improves bone mineral density
- Reduces fall risk through increased power and strength
- Enhances cognition in the elderly population

## Dosing Protocols

### Loading Phase (Optional)
- **20g per day for 5–7 days**, split into 4 doses of 5g
- Saturates muscle stores rapidly
- May cause temporary water retention and GI discomfort

### Maintenance (Standard)
- **3–5g per day**, taken daily
- Reaches full saturation in ~28 days without loading
- No cycling required — consistent daily use is optimal

### When to Take It
Timing matters less than consistency. However, research suggests **post-workout** creatine may be marginally superior for muscle and strength gains. A 2013 *Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition* study found post-workout creatine led to greater lean mass and strength than pre-workout.

Practically: take it whenever you'll actually remember to take it.

## Which Form to Buy?

**Creatine monohydrate** remains the gold standard. It is:
- The most studied form by far
- The cheapest per serving
- Equally effective as all marketed alternatives (ethyl ester, HCl, buffered, etc.)

A 2012 *Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition* study compared creatine monohydrate to HCl directly and found **no significant difference** in muscle creatine uptake or body composition. Despite higher price tags, alternative forms offer no proven advantage.

## Safety: Addressing the Myths

### "Creatine damages kidneys"
This myth comes from creatine elevating serum **creatinine**, a kidney filtration marker. But creatinine from creatine supplementation is not the same as creatinine from kidney dysfunction. Multiple long-term studies in healthy individuals show no adverse kidney effects at standard doses. Even 5 years of continuous use shows no kidney damage in healthy people.

**Caution:** If you have pre-existing kidney disease, consult a doctor before supplementing.

### "You need to cycle it"
No evidence supports cycling creatine. It doesn't downregulate natural production meaningfully, and daily use is safe long-term.

### "It causes hair loss"
Based on a single 2009 study in rugby players where DHT (a hair loss hormone) rose 56% after loading. The study used hair as a proxy, not actual hair loss outcomes, and has never been replicated. The evidence is insufficient to conclude creatine causes hair loss in practice.

## Who Benefits Most?

**Most benefit:**
- Strength/power athletes (weightlifting, sprinting, football, wrestling)
- Vegetarians and vegans (lower dietary creatine intake)
- Older adults (sarcopenia prevention, cognitive protection)
- Sleep-deprived individuals (cognitive protection)

**Less benefit:**
- Elite endurance athletes (minimal benefit for sustained aerobic effort >3 min)
- Already-maximized dietary creatine consumers (rare)

---

## Practical Tips

- Mix with warm water or juice (improves dissolution)
- Carbohydrates enhance creatine uptake (insulin-mediated transport)
- Combine with **β-alanine** for additive performance benefits in high-intensity training
- Don't worry about proprietary blends — pure creatine monohydrate powder is ideal

---

## Bottom Line

Creatine is the closest thing to a sure bet in the supplement world. For strength, power, muscle mass, recovery, and increasingly brain health — the evidence is stronger than almost any other supplement. It's safe, cheap, and it works.

If you lift weights and don't take creatine, you're leaving proven gains on the table. And if you're over 50, it may be the most impactful daily supplement you can take.

**Dose:** 3–5g creatine monohydrate daily. Every day. That's it.
