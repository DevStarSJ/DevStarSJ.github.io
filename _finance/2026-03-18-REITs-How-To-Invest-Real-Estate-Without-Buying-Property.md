Real estate has historically been one of the best wealth-building assets in existence. But buying a rental property requires hundreds of thousands of dollars, a good credit score, dealing with tenants, maintenance, vacancies, and endless headaches. What if you could invest in real estate with $100 and earn dividends every quarter — without owning a single brick?

That's exactly what Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) offer.

---

## What Is a REIT?

A Real Estate Investment Trust (REIT) is a company that:

1. **Owns** income-producing real estate (apartments, offices, shopping centers, hospitals, cell towers, etc.)
2. **Must distribute at least 90% of taxable income** as dividends to shareholders
3. **Trades on stock exchanges** like regular stocks (for publicly traded REITs)

In exchange for the 90% dividend distribution requirement, REITs pay no corporate income tax. This makes them tax-efficient vehicles for passing real estate income to investors.

---

## Types of REITs

### Equity REITs (Most Common)
Own and operate income-producing real estate. Revenue comes primarily from rents.

**Subcategories:**
- **Residential:** Apartment complexes, single-family rentals
- **Commercial:** Office buildings, retail malls
- **Industrial:** Warehouses, distribution centers (booming with e-commerce)
- **Healthcare:** Hospitals, senior housing, medical office buildings
- **Infrastructure:** Cell towers, data centers, pipelines
- **Specialty:** Self-storage, casinos, farmland

### Mortgage REITs (mREITs)
Don't own properties — instead, they invest in mortgages and mortgage-backed securities. Higher yields but more interest-rate sensitive and volatile.

### Hybrid REITs
Combine both equity and mortgage strategies.

---

## Why REITs Belong in Your Portfolio

### 1. Passive Income (High Dividends)
REITs are legally required to distribute 90%+ of income. Average REIT dividend yields are **3–6%** (some specialty REITs yield 7–10%), far above the S&P 500's ~1.3% average.

### 2. Real Estate Exposure Without the Hassle
No tenants. No toilets. No vacancies. No property management. The REIT management team handles everything.

### 3. Portfolio Diversification
Real estate often moves differently than stocks and bonds. Adding REITs to a traditional 60/40 portfolio has historically improved risk-adjusted returns.

### 4. Inflation Hedge
Real estate rents and property values tend to rise with inflation. Many REITs have inflation-linked lease structures.

### 5. Liquidity
Unlike actual real estate, you can sell REIT shares in seconds. No 6-month closing process, no real estate agent fees.

### 6. Accessible to All Investors
You can invest in real estate with as little as $1 through fractional shares or REIT ETFs.

## Tax Considerations for REITs

REIT dividends are taxed differently than qualified stock dividends:

- **Most REIT dividends are "ordinary dividends"** — taxed as ordinary income (not at the lower qualified dividend rate)
- **Section 199A deduction** — allows individuals to deduct up to 20% of qualified REIT dividends, partially offsetting the higher tax rate
- **Best accounts for REITs:** Tax-advantaged accounts (IRA, 401k) are ideal because they defer or eliminate taxes on dividends

**Example:**
- REIT dividend income: $5,000
- 20% Section 199A deduction: -$1,000
- Taxable REIT income: $4,000
- If in 22% bracket: Tax owed = $880 (vs. $1,100 without deduction)

## REIT Red Flags to Watch

### High Debt Levels
REITs use leverage to buy properties. Watch the debt-to-equity ratio — above 2x can be concerning, especially when interest rates rise.

### Payout Ratio Above 100%
If a REIT is paying more in dividends than it earns, the dividend is unsustainable. Check "Funds From Operations" (FFO) payout ratio, not earnings per share.

### Occupancy Rate Declining
Low or declining occupancy in a REIT's properties signals trouble. Look for REITs with 90%+ occupancy rates.

### Concentration Risk
A REIT that depends on one tenant for 40%+ of revenue is risky. Diversified tenant bases are safer.

## Getting Started with REIT Investing

1. **Open a brokerage account** (Fidelity, Vanguard, Schwab all offer REITs and REIT ETFs)
2. **Start with VNQ** — broad US REIT exposure, low-cost, highly liquid
3. **Add sector-specific REITs** as you learn more (industrial, data centers, healthcare)
4. **Enable dividend reinvestment** — let dividends buy more shares automatically
5. **Hold in tax-advantaged accounts** when possible (IRA, 401k) to defer dividend taxes

REITs represent one of the most accessible forms of passive real estate income ever created. Start small, diversify, and let compounding and rental income work for you.
