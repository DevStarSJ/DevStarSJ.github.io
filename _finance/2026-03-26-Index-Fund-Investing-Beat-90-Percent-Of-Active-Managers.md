## What Is an Index Fund?

An index fund is a type of mutual fund or ETF that tracks a specific market index — like the **S&P 500**, the **Total Stock Market**, or the **MSCI World**. Instead of a manager picking individual stocks, the fund simply holds every stock in the index in proportion to its market value.

The result? **You own a tiny slice of hundreds or thousands of companies with a single investment.**

## Why Index Funds Beat Active Managers

Here's the dirty secret about actively managed funds: over a 15-year period, **more than 90% of active fund managers fail to beat their benchmark index** (according to S&P's SPIVA report). The reasons are straightforward:

- **Higher fees eat your returns.** Active funds charge 0.5–2% annually. Index funds often charge just 0.03–0.20%.
- **Markets are hard to predict consistently.** Even professionals get it wrong more than they get it right.
- **Tax drag.** Frequent trading creates capital gains tax events that compound the disadvantage.

The math is brutal: a 1% fee difference on a $100,000 portfolio over 30 years costs you **over $100,000 in lost growth** (assuming 7% average returns).

## The Core Index Fund Strategy

You don't need a complex portfolio. Many investors build serious wealth with just **two or three funds**:

### Option 1: The One-Fund Portfolio
- **VT** (Vanguard Total World Stock ETF) — owns ~9,000 companies across 50+ countries
- Set it and forget it. Done.

### Option 2: The Two-Fund Portfolio
- **VTI** (Vanguard Total Stock Market) — US stocks (80%)
- **VXUS** (Vanguard Total International) — international stocks (20%)

### Option 3: The Three-Fund Portfolio
- **VTI** — US stocks (60%)
- **VXUS** — International stocks (30%)
- **BND** (Vanguard Total Bond Market) — bonds (10%)

Adjust allocations based on your age and risk tolerance. The younger you are, the heavier you can go on stocks.

## Dollar-Cost Averaging: Your Superpower

Rather than trying to "time the market" (which even professionals fail at), use **dollar-cost averaging (DCA)**: invest a fixed dollar amount at regular intervals — weekly, bi-weekly, or monthly — regardless of market conditions.

**Why it works:**
- You buy more shares when prices are low
- You buy fewer shares when prices are high
- Over time, you automatically average a favorable cost

Even if markets crash 40% next year, DCA investors see it as a sale — buying more shares at discount prices.

## Common Index Fund Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Selling during downturns** — Markets always recover. Panic selling locks in losses.
2. **Over-diversifying with too many funds** — Three funds is plenty. Twenty funds is noise.
3. **Ignoring expense ratios** — Even 0.5% vs 0.03% makes a massive long-term difference.
4. **Waiting for the "right time"** — The best time to invest was yesterday. The second best is today.
5. **Checking your portfolio daily** — Check quarterly. More frequent checking leads to emotional decisions.

## The Simple Truth

Index fund investing isn't sexy. It won't make you rich overnight. But it is one of the **most reliable, evidence-backed methods for building long-term wealth** ever discovered.

The strategy: pick a low-cost index fund, invest consistently every month, never stop, and don't panic when markets drop. That's it.
