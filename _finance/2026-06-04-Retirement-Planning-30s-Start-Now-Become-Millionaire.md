Here's a number that will change how you think about retirement: **$1,000 invested at age 30 becomes roughly $7,600 by age 65** (at 7% average annual return). Wait until 40, and that same $1,000 grows to only $3,870.

Your 30s aren't too early. They're the exact right time.

## Why Your 30s Are the Power Decade

Most people in their 30s are:
- Earning more than they did in their 20s
- (Hopefully) past the worst debt accumulation
- 30+ years away from retirement — enormous time for compounding

The problem: life is expensive in your 30s. Mortgage, kids, lifestyle upgrades. Retirement feels abstract and distant.

**The trap:** Waiting until you "can afford it." By then, you've lost years of the most powerful compounding.

## How Much Should You Have Saved by 35?

Common benchmarks:
- By 30: 1× your annual salary saved
- By 35: 2× your annual salary
- By 40: 3× your annual salary

If you earn $75,000 and are 35 with $50,000 saved — you're roughly on track.
If you're at $10,000 — you have catching up to do, but it's absolutely not too late.

## The Two Accounts You Must Use

### 1. 401(k) / 403(b) — Employer Plan

**Always capture the full employer match first.** This is free money.

Example:
- Your salary: $80,000
- Company matches 100% up to 4%
- If you don't contribute at least 4%, you're leaving $3,200 on the table every year

After capturing the match, you can invest up to **$23,000/year** (2024 limit) pre-tax.

**Traditional 401k:** Tax deduction now, taxed in retirement
**Roth 401k:** No deduction now, tax-free in retirement

### 2. Roth IRA — The Crown Jewel

Contribute up to **$7,000/year** (2024, under 50).

Why it's special:
- **Tax-free growth** — dividends, capital gains, all tax-free
- **Tax-free withdrawals** in retirement
- **Flexible** — can withdraw contributions (not earnings) anytime penalty-free
- **No RMDs** (Required Minimum Distributions)

Income limits: You can contribute fully if income is under $146,000 (single) / $230,000 (married) in 2024.

Over the limit? Use the **Backdoor Roth IRA** strategy.

## What to Invest In

For most people in their 30s, **target-date funds or a simple three-fund portfolio** works great.

### Option 1: Target-Date Fund (Easiest)
Pick a fund dated near your retirement year: e.g., Vanguard Target Retirement 2055 (VFFVX)
- Automatically diversified: US stocks, international stocks, bonds
- Automatically becomes more conservative as you age
- Set it and forget it

### Option 2: Three-Fund Portfolio
| Fund | Allocation | Example (Vanguard) |
|------|-----------|-------------------|
| US Total Market | 60% | VTSAX / VTI |
| International | 30% | VXUS |
| Bonds | 10% | BND |

Adjust bond % to your comfort level. In your 30s, you can be 90-100% stocks.

## The Math That Will Motivate You

**Scenario A: Start at 30**
- Invest $500/month from age 30 to 65
- At 7% return: **$1,068,048**

**Scenario B: Start at 40**
- Invest $500/month from age 40 to 65
- At 7% return: **$486,428**

Same monthly investment. $581,000 difference. Just from starting 10 years earlier.

## Building Your 30s Retirement Plan

### Priority Order

1. ✅ Capture full employer 401k match
2. ✅ Pay off high-interest debt (>7% rate)
3. ✅ Max Roth IRA ($7,000/year)
4. ✅ Increase 401k contributions beyond the match
5. ✅ Taxable brokerage account (if more to invest)

### How Much to Contribute

A useful guideline: **save 15% of gross income for retirement**, including employer match.

On $80,000:
- 15% = $12,000/year = $1,000/month

If that's too much right now, start with 6% (to get the match) and increase by 1% every time you get a raise.

## Special 30s Considerations

**Have kids?** Set up their 529 college savings account, but **don't sacrifice your retirement**. You can borrow for college; you can't borrow for retirement.

**Variable income?** Use Roth IRA for flexible contributions throughout the year. Contribute when you have extra cash.

**Self-employed?** You have access to even better accounts:
- SEP-IRA: Contribute up to 25% of net income (max $69,000 in 2024)
- Solo 401k: $23,000 employee + up to $46,000 employer contributions

## Your Next Steps

1. Check your 401k — are you getting the full match?
2. Open a Roth IRA if you don't have one (Fidelity, Vanguard, or Schwab)
3. Set up automatic contributions
4. Pick a target-date fund or three-fund portfolio
5. Ignore the market noise and don't touch it for 30 years

The best retirement plan is boring. Automate, diversify, wait. That's it.
