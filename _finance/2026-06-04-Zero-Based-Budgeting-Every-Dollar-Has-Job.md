Most budgets fail not because people spend too much, but because they never decide *where* the money goes in the first place. Zero-based budgeting (ZBB) fixes that at the root.

## What Is Zero-Based Budgeting?

Zero-based budgeting means your income minus your expenses equals **zero** — not because you spend everything, but because you *assign* every dollar a role:

- Bills → assigned
- Groceries → assigned
- Savings → assigned
- Investments → assigned
- Fun money → assigned

The goal isn't zero savings. It's zero **untracked** dollars.

## Why Traditional Budgets Fail

Most people budget like this:
1. Get paid
2. Pay bills
3. Spend the rest "carefully"
4. Wonder where the money went

ZBB inverts that logic. You allocate first, spend second.

## How to Set Up Zero-Based Budget in 5 Steps

### Step 1: Write Down Your Monthly Take-Home Income

Include salary, side income, freelance — everything that hits your account. Use **conservative estimates** if income varies.

### Step 2: List Every Expected Expense

Fixed costs:
- Rent/mortgage
- Car payment
- Insurance
- Subscriptions

Variable costs:
- Groceries
- Gas
- Dining out
- Clothing

### Step 3: Add Savings & Investment Goals

Treat these like bills:
- Emergency fund contribution
- Retirement (401k, IRA)
- Vacation fund
- Big purchase sinking fund

### Step 4: Subtract Until You Hit Zero

Income – (All Expenses + Savings) = $0

If you have leftover money, put it somewhere (extra savings, debt payoff, etc.). If you're in the negative, cut something.

### Step 5: Track During the Month

Check weekly. Adjust categories as real spending happens. The budget is a living document, not a one-time event.

## Common ZBB Mistakes

**Forgetting irregular expenses:** Car registration, annual subscriptions, holiday gifts. Create a sinking fund for these.

**Making it too rigid:** Life happens. Budget for flexibility — a "misc" category is fine.

**Not doing a mid-month check-in:** The budget only works if you look at it.

## ZBB vs. 50/30/20 Budget

| | Zero-Based | 50/30/20 |
|-|-----------|---------|
| Control level | High | Medium |
| Time required | More | Less |
| Best for | Debt payoff, saving goals | Simplicity |
| Flexibility | High (you set categories) | Preset percentages |

Neither is "better" — ZBB is more work but gives more insight.

## The Psychology Behind Why It Works

When you name your money, you own your choices. Spending $80 on dinner hits different when you *chose* to put $80 in "dining out" rather than watching money disappear mysteriously.

ZBB makes spending **intentional**. That's the secret.

## Real-World Example

Monthly take-home: **$4,000**

Every dollar has a job. Zero left over, zero unaccounted for.

## Start This Month

You don't need a perfect system. Open a spreadsheet, list your income, list your expenses, and assign every dollar. Do it imperfectly. Adjust next month. Keep going.

The best budget is the one you actually use.
