## What Is the Stock Market (Really)?

When you buy a stock, you buy a small piece of ownership in a company. If the company grows and becomes more valuable, your piece is worth more. If it pays profits to shareholders, you get a cut — called a **dividend**.

The **stock market** is simply a marketplace where buyers and sellers trade these ownership pieces.

**Major U.S. stock exchanges:**
- **NYSE** (New York Stock Exchange) — older, more traditional companies
- **NASDAQ** — tech-heavy, growth-oriented companies

**Key indexes** (collections of stocks that track overall market health):
- **S&P 500** — 500 largest U.S. companies; the most-watched benchmark
- **Dow Jones** — 30 major blue-chip companies
- **NASDAQ Composite** — all NASDAQ-listed companies

## Why Invest in Stocks?

**Historical returns (S&P 500, long-term average):** ~10% per year

Compare that to:
- Savings account: 0.5–5% (varies)
- Bonds: 3–5%
- Real estate: 7–9%

Over long periods, the stock market has beaten inflation and every alternative for most investors.

**The power of compounding:**
- $10,000 invested at 10%/year
- After 10 years: $25,937
- After 20 years: $67,275
- After 30 years: $174,494

That's your money making money, without you doing anything.

## How to Get Started: Step by Step

### Step 1: Open a Brokerage Account
You need an account to buy stocks. Top options for beginners:
- **Fidelity** — no minimums, excellent research tools, great for beginners
- **Schwab** — similar to Fidelity, strong customer service
- **Vanguard** — best for index fund investors long-term
- **Robinhood** — simple interface, but fewer tools

**Look for:** No account minimums, no trading commissions, user-friendly interface.

### Step 2: Fund the Account
Start with whatever you can. Even $500 is a real start. Many brokerages allow fractional shares — you can buy $50 of Amazon stock without needing the full share price.

### Step 3: Choose What to Buy

This is where most beginners get stuck. Here's a simple framework.

### Option A: Index Funds / ETFs (Best for Most People)

An **index fund** buys a little of every stock in an index. One purchase = instant diversification.

**Why index funds win:**
- Low fees (0.03–0.20% annually vs. 1%+ for actively managed funds)
- Outperform 90%+ of actively managed funds over 10+ years
- No research required
- Warren Buffett's recommendation for the average investor

**Top beginner ETFs:**
- **VTI** — Total U.S. stock market (all sizes)
- **VOO** — S&P 500 only (top 500 companies)
- **VT** — Total world market (U.S. + international)
- **SCHB** — Total U.S. market (Schwab version, similar to VTI)

**Simple starter portfolio:**
- 80% VTI (or VOO)
- 20% VXUS (international)

### Option B: Individual Stocks

Buying specific companies (Apple, Google, Tesla) is higher risk and higher potential reward. Requires research into:
- Revenue growth
- Profit margins
- Competitive position
- Valuation (P/E ratio, etc.)

**Beginner recommendation:** Stick to index funds for 80%+ of your portfolio. Individual stocks for experimentation only.

## Core Investing Principles

### 1. Time in the Market Beats Timing the Market
Nobody consistently predicts short-term market movements — not professionals, not algorithms. Investors who try to "time" the market typically buy high (when optimistic) and sell low (when scared).

**The solution:** Invest consistently, regardless of market conditions.

### 2. Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA)
Invest a fixed amount on a regular schedule — $200/month regardless of price. When prices are low, you buy more shares. When prices are high, you buy fewer. Averages out over time.

### 3. Diversification
Don't put all your eggs in one basket. Spread risk across:
- Different companies
- Different sectors (tech, healthcare, financials, energy)
- Different geographies (U.S. + international)

Index funds handle this automatically.

### 4. Don't Panic Sell
Markets drop. Regularly. A 10-20% correction is normal and expected every few years. Investors who sell during downturns lock in losses. Investors who hold (or buy more) recover and profit.

## Biggest Mistakes Beginners Make

❌ **Waiting for the "perfect" time** — there is no perfect time. Start now.

❌ **Picking individual stocks without research** — most people underperform the index.

❌ **Checking the portfolio daily** — leads to emotional decisions. Check quarterly.

❌ **Panic selling during corrections** — the crash is when the opportunity is.

❌ **Not reinvesting dividends** — DRIP (Dividend Reinvestment Plans) accelerate compounding.

❌ **Ignoring tax-advantaged accounts** — always maximize 401(k) match and IRA before taxable investing.

---

## Tax-Advantaged Accounts First

Before investing in a regular brokerage account, use these:

1. **401(k) with employer match** — 100% return on matched contributions (free money)
2. **Roth IRA or Traditional IRA** — tax-free or tax-deferred growth ($7,000/year limit)
3. **HSA** — triple tax advantage for healthcare expenses
4. **Regular brokerage account** — no special tax treatment, but unlimited contributions

## Realistic Expectations

- **Short-term (1-3 years):** Could be up or down 20-30%
- **Medium-term (5-10 years):** Likely up, but not guaranteed
- **Long-term (20+ years):** Historically, positive in every 20-year period in U.S. market history

Stocks are for money you don't need for at least 5 years. For shorter time horizons, use savings accounts or bonds.
