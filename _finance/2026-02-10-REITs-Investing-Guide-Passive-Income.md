Want to invest in real estate but don't have hundreds of thousands of dollars for a down payment? Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) offer a way to own a piece of commercial real estate—from shopping malls to data centers—with as little as the cost of a single share.

## What Are REITs?

REITs are companies that own, operate, or finance income-producing real estate. They were created by Congress in 1960 to give everyday investors access to large-scale, income-producing real estate.

Think of REITs as mutual funds for real estate. When you buy shares of a REIT, you're buying a small piece of a diversified real estate portfolio managed by professionals.

### Key Requirements for REITs

To qualify as a REIT, a company must:

- **Invest at least 75%** of assets in real estate
- **Derive at least 75%** of income from rents or mortgage interest
- **Pay at least 90%** of taxable income as dividends
- Have at least 100 shareholders
- Have no more than 50% of shares held by five or fewer individuals

The 90% dividend requirement is why REITs are popular among income investors—they're essentially required to pay out most of their profits.

## Types of REITs

### 1. Equity REITs

The most common type, equity REITs own and operate income-producing properties.

**Property sectors include:**
- **Residential**: Apartment complexes, single-family rentals
- **Retail**: Shopping centers, malls
- **Office**: Commercial office buildings
- **Industrial**: Warehouses, distribution centers
- **Healthcare**: Hospitals, medical offices, senior housing
- **Data Centers**: Facilities housing servers and tech infrastructure
- **Cell Towers**: Wireless communication infrastructure

### 2. Mortgage REITs (mREITs)

Instead of owning properties, mREITs provide financing for real estate by purchasing mortgages or mortgage-backed securities.

**Characteristics:**
- Higher dividend yields (often 8-12%)
- More sensitive to interest rate changes
- Higher risk profile

### 3. Hybrid REITs

These combine both equity and mortgage REIT strategies, owning properties while also holding mortgages.

## Benefits of REIT Investing

### 1. Accessibility
- Start with as little as one share
- No property management headaches
- Professional management

### 2. Liquidity
- Buy and sell on major exchanges
- Much more liquid than physical property
- Easy to adjust position size

### 3. Diversification
- Exposure to real estate sector
- Geographic diversification
- Property type diversification

### 4. Income Generation
- High dividend yields (typically 3-6% for equity REITs)
- Quarterly or monthly distributions
- Historically reliable income stream

### 5. Inflation Hedge
- Real estate values often rise with inflation
- Rent increases protect against purchasing power erosion

## Risks to Consider

### 1. Interest Rate Sensitivity
When interest rates rise:
- REIT prices often fall
- Borrowing costs increase
- Dividend yields become less attractive vs. bonds

### 2. Economic Cycles
- Retail REITs suffer during recessions
- Office REITs impacted by remote work trends
- Economic downturns reduce occupancy rates

### 3. Sector-Specific Risks
- **Retail**: E-commerce competition
- **Office**: Work-from-home trends
- **Healthcare**: Regulatory changes

### 4. Tax Considerations
REIT dividends are typically taxed as ordinary income, not at the lower qualified dividend rate.

**Tax-efficient strategies:**
- Hold REITs in tax-advantaged accounts (IRA, 401k)
- Consider the 20% pass-through deduction (Section 199A)

## How to Evaluate REITs

### Key Metrics

**1. Funds From Operations (FFO)**
The REIT equivalent of earnings. FFO adds depreciation back to net income since real estate typically appreciates.

**2. Adjusted Funds From Operations (AFFO)**
FFO minus capital expenditures. Shows true cash available for dividends.

**3. Price-to-FFO Ratio**
Similar to P/E ratio. Compare to sector averages:
- Below 15: Potentially undervalued
- 15-20: Fairly valued
- Above 20: Potentially overvalued

**4. Dividend Yield**
Current annual dividend ÷ share price. Compare to:
- 10-year Treasury yield
- Sector average
- Historical yield for the REIT

**5. Payout Ratio**
Dividend ÷ FFO. Look for:
- Below 80%: Sustainable dividend
- 80-90%: Watch carefully
- Above 90%: Dividend at risk

**6. Occupancy Rate**
Percentage of rentable space that's occupied. Higher is better.

**7. Debt-to-EBITDA**
Measures leverage. Generally:
- Below 5x: Conservative
- 5-7x: Moderate
- Above 7x: Aggressive

## Ways to Invest in REITs

### 1. Individual REIT Stocks

**Pros:**
- Choose specific sectors
- Potentially higher returns
- Direct ownership

**Cons:**
- Requires research
- Less diversification
- Higher risk

### 2. REIT ETFs

Popular options:
- **VNQ** (Vanguard Real Estate ETF)
- **SCHH** (Schwab US REIT ETF)
- **IYR** (iShares US Real Estate ETF)

**Pros:**
- Instant diversification
- Low expense ratios
- Easy to buy/sell

### 3. REIT Mutual Funds

**Pros:**
- Professional management
- Automatic reinvestment
- Diversification

**Cons:**
- Higher expense ratios
- Minimum investment requirements

### 4. Private/Non-Traded REITs

**Pros:**
- Potentially higher returns
- Less market volatility

**Cons:**
- Illiquid (difficult to sell)
- Higher fees
- Less transparency

## Building a REIT Portfolio

### Beginner Strategy

Start with a diversified REIT ETF:
1. Open a brokerage account
2. Invest in VNQ or similar broad REIT ETF
3. Set up automatic monthly investments
4. Reinvest dividends

### Intermediate Strategy

Build a diversified individual REIT portfolio:
- 3-5 REITs across different sectors
- Mix of growth and income focused
- Geographic diversification

### Sample Sector Allocation

- **30%** Industrial/Logistics
- **20%** Residential
- **20%** Data Centers/Technology
- **15%** Healthcare
- **15%** Diversified/Other

## The Bottom Line

REITs offer an accessible way to add real estate exposure to your portfolio without the complexity of direct property ownership. Whether you're seeking income, diversification, or both, REITs can play a valuable role in a well-rounded investment strategy.

**Key takeaways:**
- REITs must pay 90% of income as dividends
- Different REIT types offer different risk/reward profiles
- Use tax-advantaged accounts when possible
- Start with diversified ETFs if you're new to REITs
