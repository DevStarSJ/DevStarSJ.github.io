# Roth IRA vs Traditional IRA: Which One Actually Makes You Richer?

Choosing between a Roth IRA and a Traditional IRA is one of the most consequential financial decisions you'll ever make — and most people pick wrong.

The difference in lifetime wealth can exceed **$500,000** depending on when you invest, your tax rates, and how long your money grows. This guide breaks down exactly which account wins in your situation.

## The Core Difference (In Plain English)

| | Traditional IRA | Roth IRA |
|---|---|---|
| **Tax break** | Now (deductible contributions) | Later (tax-free withdrawals) |
| **Contributions** | Pre-tax | After-tax |
| **Growth** | Tax-deferred | Tax-free |
| **Withdrawals** | Taxed as income | Tax-free |
| **RMDs** | Required at 73 | None |

Both have a **$7,000/year contribution limit** in 2026 ($8,000 if 50+).

---

## The Real Question: When Do You Pay Taxes?

The entire debate comes down to one question: **Will your tax rate be higher now or in retirement?**

### Choose Traditional IRA if:
- You're in the **22%+ tax bracket** now
- You expect to be in a **lower bracket** in retirement
- You need the tax deduction now to invest more
- You have significant other income sources already

### Choose Roth IRA if:
- You're in the **10–22% bracket** now
- You expect **higher income or higher taxes** in retirement
- You want **no required minimum distributions**
- You're **under 40** (decades of tax-free growth ahead)
- You want flexibility — Roth contributions can be withdrawn penalty-free anytime

---

## The Math: A Real Comparison

Let's say you're 30 years old, invest $6,500/year, earn 8% annually, and retire at 65.

**Traditional IRA:**
- You save 22% on contributions = $1,430/year in taxes saved
- At 65: ~$1.16 million
- Pay 22% in retirement taxes = **$905,000 net**

**Roth IRA:**
- No upfront deduction, but:
- At 65: ~$1.16 million
- Zero taxes owed = **$1,160,000 net**

> Roth wins by ~**$255,000** if tax rates stay the same — and by even more if taxes rise.

## The Roth Conversion Ladder (Advanced Move)

If you have a Traditional IRA and want Roth benefits, you can convert funds over time:

1. Convert Traditional IRA money to Roth each year in low-income years
2. Pay income tax on converted amount
3. After 5 years, withdraw converted funds penalty-free
4. Access retirement funds early (before 59½) without the 10% penalty

This is especially powerful in **early retirement** or **gap years** with low income.

---

## Income Limits to Know (2026)

**Roth IRA Phase-Out:**
- Single: $150,000–$165,000
- Married Filing Jointly: $236,000–$246,000

**Traditional IRA Deductibility Phase-Out** (if covered by workplace plan):
- Single: $79,000–$89,000
- Married Filing Jointly: $126,000–$146,000

> Over the income limit for Roth? Use the **Backdoor Roth IRA** — contribute to Traditional IRA then immediately convert to Roth.

---

## 5 Situations Where Roth Always Wins

1. **You're young (under 35)** — decades of tax-free compounding
2. **You're in a low tax bracket** — pay low taxes now, none later
3. **Tax rates are rising** — lock in today's rates
4. **You want no RMDs** — Traditional forces withdrawals at 73
5. **You want estate planning flexibility** — Roth passes to heirs tax-free

---

## The Verdict

**Most people under 40 should lean Roth.** Tax-free compounding over 30+ years beats an upfront deduction almost every time — especially with today's historically moderate tax rates that could rise.

**High earners in peak earning years** often benefit from Traditional IRA's immediate tax relief, then convert strategically in retirement.

**Best strategy:** Max both if you can. If not, match your current tax bracket reality against your expected retirement income.
