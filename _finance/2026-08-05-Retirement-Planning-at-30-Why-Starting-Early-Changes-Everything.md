Most people think about retirement when it's getting close. The math says that's a costly mistake. Starting at 30 instead of 40 doesn't just give you 10 more years — through the power of compounding, it can **double or triple your final retirement balance**.

## Why Starting at 30 Matters So Much

This single table explains everything:

| Start Age | Monthly Investment | Return | Balance at 65 |
|-----------|-------------------|--------|---------------|
| 25 | $500 | 7% | **$1,359,000** |
| 30 | $500 | 7% | **$949,000** |
| 35 | $500 | 7% | **$651,000** |
| 40 | $500 | 7% | **$437,000** |

Same $500/month. Same 7% return. Just different start ages. The person who starts at 30 ends up with **2.2x more money** than the one who starts at 40. This is the magic of compounding — time is more powerful than contribution amount.

## The Foundation: Understanding Your Retirement Accounts

### 401(k) — Your Employer-Sponsored Plan

A 401(k) reduces your taxable income now. You invest pre-tax dollars; the money grows tax-deferred; you pay taxes when you withdraw in retirement.

**2026 contribution limits:**
- Employee contribution: up to **$23,500/year**
- If employer matches: always contribute at least enough to get the full match — it's **free money**

**Example employer match:**
"We match 50% of your contributions up to 6% of salary."
If you earn $70,000 and contribute 6% ($4,200), your employer adds $2,100.
That's a **50% instant return on those dollars** before any market gains.

### Roth IRA — Tax-Free Growth Forever

With a Roth IRA, you invest after-tax dollars now. But all future growth and withdrawals are **completely tax-free** — even the gains.

**2026 limits:**
- Contribution: up to **$7,000/year** ($8,000 if 50+)
- Income phase-out starts at $150,000 single / $236,000 married

**Why Roth at 30?** You're likely in a lower tax bracket now than you'll be at peak earnings — or in retirement. Pay lower taxes now, enjoy tax-free withdrawals for 30+ years.

## The Optimal 30s Retirement Strategy

### Priority Order for Retirement Savings

1. **401(k) to employer match** — Always do this first (free money)
2. **Max out Roth IRA** ($7,000/year = $583/month)
3. **Max out 401(k)** ($23,500/year = $1,958/month)
4. **Taxable brokerage account** — any additional savings
5. **HSA** if available (triple tax advantage)

### Asset Allocation in Your 30s

At 30, you have 35 years for your money to grow. You can afford more risk — which historically means more reward.

**Aggressive growth portfolio (age 30-40):**
- 90% stocks
- 10% bonds

**Simple implementation:**
- 70% US Total Market (VTI)
- 20% International (VXUS)
- 10% Bonds (BND)

## How Much Should You Save?

The classic guideline: **save 15% of gross income** for retirement. But that's a minimum — if you're starting late or want early retirement, aim for 20–25%.

**Reality check for 30-year-olds:**

If you earn $70,000:
- 15% = $10,500/year = $875/month
- With a 5% match: You contribute $6,000, employer adds $3,500, total = $9,500/year from the account

At this rate, starting at 30, you're on track for a solid retirement by 65.

## The 4% Rule: How Much Is Enough?

The "4% rule" says you can safely withdraw 4% of your portfolio in year one of retirement, then adjust for inflation — and your money will last 30 years.

**Retirement target = Annual spending ÷ 0.04**

| Annual Spending | Portfolio Needed |
|----------------|----------------|
| $40,000 | $1,000,000 |
| $60,000 | $1,500,000 |
| $80,000 | $2,000,000 |
| $100,000 | $2,500,000 |

Calculate your target and work backward to know what monthly contribution you need.

## Investing Mistakes to Avoid in Your 30s

**1. Not investing because you have debt**
High-interest debt (credit cards) — pay off first. But low-interest debt (student loans, mortgage) — invest in parallel. The math often favors investing.

**2. Being too conservative**
Many 30-somethings keep retirement funds in low-return "safe" investments out of fear. Over 35 years, this costs fortunes. Own more stocks.

**3. Withdrawing from 401(k) early**
Early withdrawals incur a 10% penalty plus income taxes. A $10,000 withdrawal can cost $3,000-$4,000 in penalties and taxes. Leave it alone.

**4. Ignoring fees**
A 1% annual fee difference over 35 years can reduce your final balance by **20-25%**. Choose low-cost index funds.
