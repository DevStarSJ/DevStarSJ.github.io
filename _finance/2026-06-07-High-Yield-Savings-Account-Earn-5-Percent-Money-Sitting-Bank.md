## What Is a High-Yield Savings Account?

A high-yield savings account (HYSA) is a type of savings account — typically offered by online banks — that pays a significantly higher interest rate than traditional brick-and-mortar banks. They are FDIC-insured (up to $250,000), meaning your money is just as safe as in any major bank.

The reason online banks can offer higher rates is simple: they have no physical branches, so their operating costs are dramatically lower. They pass those savings on to you as higher interest.

## How Much Can You Actually Earn?

Let's run the numbers. Say you keep $20,000 in savings:

| Account Type | APY | Annual Interest |
|---|---|---|
| Traditional bank | 0.01% | $2 |
| National average | 0.46% | $92 |
| High-yield savings | 4.75% | $950 |

That's nearly **$1,000/year in passive interest** — just for moving your money to the right account. No investing, no risk.

Over 5 years (with interest compounding), that $20,000 grows to approximately **$25,175** at 4.75% vs. $20,092 at 0.46%.

## HYSA vs. Other Savings Options

**HYSA vs. Regular Savings Account:**
- HYSAs win hands-down on interest. The only downside is transfers can take 1–2 business days.

**HYSA vs. CDs (Certificates of Deposit):**
- CDs may offer slightly higher rates but *lock up* your money for 6–24 months. HYSAs keep your money liquid.

**HYSA vs. Money Market Accounts:**
- Very similar. Money market accounts sometimes offer debit cards. HYSAs often edge them out on APY.

**HYSA vs. I-Bonds:**
- I-Bonds are inflation-indexed and great for long-term savings, but have a $10,000/year limit and a 1-year lock-up period. Use both if you can.

## Who Should Use a High-Yield Savings Account?

Pretty much everyone — but HYSAs are *especially* valuable for:

1. **Emergency fund holders** — Your 3–6 months of expenses should be earning 4%+, not rotting at 0.01%
2. **Short-term savers** — Saving for a vacation, down payment, or wedding in the next 1–3 years? HYSA is perfect.
3. **Risk-averse savers** — Not ready to invest? At least earn real interest on your cash.
4. **People waiting to deploy capital** — Holding cash before investing? Park it in a HYSA.

## How to Switch to a HYSA

1. **Pick your account** — Compare rates on Bankrate or NerdWallet
2. **Open online** — Takes about 10 minutes; need SSN, government ID, and a funding account
3. **Transfer your savings** — Link your current bank and initiate a transfer
4. **Set up direct deposit (optional)** — Some banks like SoFi boost your APY with direct deposit
5. **Watch the interest roll in** — Interest typically posts monthly

> **Note:** Keep a small buffer in your primary checking account to avoid overdrafts during transfers.
