## What Is Dividend Investing?

When you own shares of a profitable company, that company may distribute a portion of its profits to shareholders as **dividends** — cash payments made regularly (usually quarterly or monthly).

**The math of dividend income:**

If you own $200,000 in stocks with an average 6% dividend yield:
- $200,000 × 6% = $12,000/year = **$1,000/month**

No working. No selling. Just passive income.

## The 3 Types of Dividend Stocks

### 1. Dividend Aristocrats (Low Risk, Lower Yield)
Companies that have increased dividends for 25+ consecutive years.

**Examples:**
- **Johnson & Johnson (JNJ)** — 61 years of consecutive increases
- **Coca-Cola (KO)** — 62 years of increases
- **Procter & Gamble (PG)** — 67 years of increases
- **Realty Income (O)** — Monthly dividends, 30 years of increases

**Typical yield:** 2–4%
**Best for:** Safety and long-term growth

---

### 2. High-Dividend Stocks (Higher Income, More Risk)
These pay more now but have less room for error.

**Examples:**
- **Altria (MO)** — ~8% yield (tobacco)
- **AT&T (T)** — ~7% yield (telecom)
- **Verizon (VZ)** — ~6.5% yield (telecom)
- **LyondellBasell (LYB)** — ~7% yield (chemicals)

**Typical yield:** 5–10%
**Best for:** Income generation with moderate risk

---

### 3. REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts)
REITs own income-producing real estate and must distribute 90% of taxable income as dividends.

**Examples:**
- **Realty Income (O)** — Monthly dividends, ~5.5% yield
- **AGNC Investment (AGNC)** — ~12% yield (mortgage REIT, high risk)
- **Agree Realty (ADC)** — ~4.5% yield, grocery-anchored properties
- **Prologis (PLD)** — ~3% yield, logistics warehouses

**Typical yield:** 3–12%
**Best for:** Real estate exposure without owning property

## Dividend Reinvestment: The Secret Accelerant

The key to building faster is **DRIP** — Dividend Reinvestment Plan.

Instead of taking dividends as cash, you reinvest them to buy more shares, which generate more dividends, which buy more shares...

**Example: $10,000 in Realty Income (O) at 5.5% yield**

*Without DRIP (taking dividends as cash):*
- Year 1: $550 in dividends
- Year 10: $550/year (assuming no price change)
- Total after 10 years: ~$15,500 (price appreciation) + $5,500 cash dividends

*With DRIP:*
- Year 1: 550 reinvested → now own slightly more shares
- Each quarter: more shares = more dividends = even more shares
- Year 10: ~$17,100 worth of shares paying ~$940/year in dividends

The compounding effect adds approximately 20–30% more wealth over 10 years.

## Dividend Safety: How to Avoid Dividend Cuts

A high yield means nothing if the company cuts it. Watch these warning signs:

**⚠️ Danger signs:**
- Payout ratio over 90% (less room for cuts if earnings drop)
- Revenue declining for 2+ consecutive quarters
- Debt/equity ratio rising rapidly
- Dividend yield suspiciously higher than peers (market is pricing in a cut)
- Company losing money despite paying dividends

**✅ Safety signs:**
- Payout ratio 40–70%
- Growing free cash flow
- Consistent revenue growth
- Long history of dividend increases
- Low debt levels

**Tool to check dividend safety:** Simply Safe Dividends (simplysfedividends.com) — scores each dividend 0–100.

## Taxes on Dividend Income

Not all dividends are taxed equally:

**Qualified dividends** (most US stock dividends held 60+ days):
- 0% tax if your taxable income is under $47,026 (single) or $94,050 (married)
- 15% for most middle-income earners
- 20% for high earners

**Ordinary dividends** (REITs, some international stocks, bond funds):
- Taxed as regular income (10–37% depending on your bracket)

**Strategy:** Hold REIT dividends in a Roth IRA to avoid ordinary income tax on them.

## Common Mistakes

**❌ Chasing yield only**
A 15% yield often signals a dividend cut ahead. Quality matters more than current yield.

**❌ Concentrating in one sector**
All energy stocks, all REITs = sector risk. Spread across utilities, consumer staples, healthcare, tech, real estate.

**❌ Not using tax-advantaged accounts**
Dividend income in a taxable account gets taxed every year. Use Roth IRA for REITs and high-yield stocks first.

**❌ Panic selling during downturns**
When stocks drop 30%, dividend yields go up (lower price, same dividend). This is opportunity, not crisis.

**❌ Ignoring dividend growth**
A company growing its dividend 7%/year doubles it in 10 years. Dividend growth beats starting yield over time.
