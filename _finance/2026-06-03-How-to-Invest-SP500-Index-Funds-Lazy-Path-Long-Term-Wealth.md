Here's a fact that should change how you think about investing: **Over any 20-year period in history, the S&P 500 has never lost money.** And over those same periods, it has beaten approximately 90% of actively managed funds.

You don't need to be smart to beat most professional investors. You just need to be boring.

---

## What Is the S&P 500?

The S&P 500 is an index of **500 of the largest publicly traded companies** in the United States, including Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, Nvidia, Berkshire Hathaway, and 495 others.

When you invest in an S&P 500 index fund, you're effectively buying a tiny piece of all 500 companies at once — the most diversified single investment available to retail investors.

## Index Funds vs ETFs: What's the Difference?

Both track the S&P 500. The difference is in how you buy:

| | Index Fund (Mutual Fund) | ETF |
|---|---|---|
| How to buy | Once per day at NAV | Trades like stock, any time |
| Minimum | Often $0-3,000 | Price of 1 share (~$500-600 for SPY) |
| Expense ratio | 0.01-0.03% | 0.03-0.20% |
| Best for | Automatic investing | Brokerage accounts |

## The Psychological Challenge

The strategy is simple. The psychology is hard.

**You will experience:**
- Market drops of 30–40% (happened in 2008, 2020, 2022)
- News headlines screaming to "sell everything"
- Temptation to time the market
- Peer pressure toward crypto/meme stocks

**The correct response:** Do nothing. Or if you have more cash, buy more.

Every major market crash in history has eventually been followed by new all-time highs. The people who panicked and sold locked in their losses. The people who held (or bought more) got rich.

## Dollar-Cost Averaging: Your Best Friend

Instead of trying to time the market, invest the same amount every month regardless of price:

| Month | Investment | Price | Shares Bought |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jan | $500 | $500 | 1.0 |
| Feb | $500 | $400 | 1.25 |
| Mar | $500 | $600 | 0.83 |
| **Total** | **$1,500** | avg $483 | **3.08** |

By investing consistently, you automatically buy *more shares* when prices are low — without any skill required.
