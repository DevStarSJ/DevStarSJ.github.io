# Index Fund Investing: The Simple Path to Building Wealth

Warren Buffett famously bet $1 million that an S&P 500 index fund would outperform hedge funds over 10 years—and won. Index fund investing isn't just for beginners; it's a strategy endorsed by the world's most successful investors.

## What Are Index Funds?

An index fund is a type of mutual fund or ETF designed to track a specific market index, like the S&P 500 or Total Stock Market. Instead of trying to beat the market, index funds aim to match market returns.

### Key Characteristics

- **Passive management**: No active stock picking
- **Low fees**: Expense ratios often under 0.10%
- **Broad diversification**: Own hundreds or thousands of stocks
- **Tax efficiency**: Lower turnover means fewer taxable events

## Why Index Funds Win

### The Numbers Don't Lie

Over 15-year periods, approximately 90% of actively managed funds underperform their benchmark index. Here's why:

**Cost advantage:**
- Average active fund: 0.70-1.00% expense ratio
- Average index fund: 0.03-0.20% expense ratio

Over 30 years, a 1% fee difference on $100,000 costs you over $150,000 in lost returns.

### Academic Evidence

Nobel Prize-winning research supports passive investing:
- Markets are largely efficient
- Professional managers can't consistently beat the market
- Costs are the best predictor of future returns

## Types of Index Funds

### Stock Index Funds

**Total U.S. Stock Market**
- Covers entire U.S. market
- 3,000+ companies
- Examples: VTI, FSKAX, SWTSX

**S&P 500**
- 500 largest U.S. companies
- Most popular benchmark
- Examples: VOO, SPY, IVV

**International Developed**
- European, Asian, Australian markets
- Diversification beyond U.S.
- Examples: VXUS, IXUS, EFA

**Emerging Markets**
- China, India, Brazil, etc.
- Higher growth potential, more volatility
- Examples: VWO, IEMG, EEM

### Bond Index Funds

**Total Bond Market**
- Government and corporate bonds
- Stability and income
- Examples: BND, AGG, FXNAX

**International Bonds**
- Global fixed income
- Currency diversification
- Examples: BNDX, IAGG

## Building Your Index Fund Portfolio

### The Three-Fund Portfolio

A simple, complete portfolio:

1. **U.S. Total Stock Market (60-70%)**
   - VTI, FSKAX, or SWTSX

2. **International Stock Market (20-30%)**
   - VXUS, FTIHX, or SWISX

3. **Total Bond Market (10-30%)**
   - BND, FXNAX, or SWAGX

### Sample Allocations by Age

**Age 25 (Aggressive)**
- 70% U.S. stocks
- 20% International stocks
- 10% Bonds

**Age 40 (Moderate)**
- 55% U.S. stocks
- 20% International stocks
- 25% Bonds

**Age 60 (Conservative)**
- 40% U.S. stocks
- 15% International stocks
- 45% Bonds

## Index Funds vs. ETFs

### Mutual Funds
- Buy at end-of-day NAV
- May have minimum investments
- Automatic investment options
- Better for 401(k) accounts

### ETFs
- Trade throughout the day
- No minimums (buy single shares)
- Slightly more tax-efficient
- Better for taxable accounts

**Best approach**: Use whichever is available and cheapest in your accounts.

## How to Invest in Index Funds

### Step 1: Choose Your Platform

**Brokerage Options:**
- Vanguard (index fund pioneer)
- Fidelity (zero-fee index funds)
- Schwab (low minimums)
- M1 Finance (automated portfolios)

### Step 2: Open Accounts

Priority order:
1. 401(k) up to employer match
2. Roth IRA or Traditional IRA
3. Taxable brokerage account

### Step 3: Select Your Funds

Look for:
- Expense ratio under 0.20%
- High assets under management
- Tracking error close to zero
- No transaction fees

### Step 4: Automate Contributions

Set up automatic investments:
- Weekly or monthly contributions
- Dollar-cost averaging
- Reinvest dividends automatically

## Common Index Fund Mistakes

### 1. Checking Too Often
- Daily monitoring leads to emotional decisions
- Check quarterly at most

### 2. Chasing Performance
- Don't switch funds based on recent returns
- Stay the course

### 3. Over-Diversifying
- 3-5 funds is plenty
- Too many funds adds complexity without benefit

### 4. Ignoring International
- Don't be 100% U.S.
- Global diversification reduces risk

### 5. Wrong Asset Location
- Keep bonds in tax-advantaged accounts
- Hold stocks in taxable accounts when possible

## Rebalancing Your Portfolio

### When to Rebalance

- Once or twice per year, OR
- When allocation drifts 5%+ from target

### How to Rebalance

**Option 1**: Sell and buy (taxable implications)
**Option 2**: Direct new contributions to underweight assets
**Option 3**: Use dividends to rebalance
