## What Are Dividends?

Dividends are cash payments companies make to shareholders, typically every quarter. When a company earns more profit than it needs to reinvest in growth, it returns some of that excess to its owners — you, if you own shares.

**Example:**
- You own 500 shares of a company at $50/share ($25,000 invested)
- The company pays a $1.50 annual dividend per share
- You receive $750/year — just for holding the stock

This is **completely separate from stock price gains**. Even if the stock price barely moves, you're still receiving income.

---

## Why Dividend Investing Stands Out

### 1. Income Without Selling
Traditional investing requires you to sell shares to access value. Dividend investing sends you cash while you keep the shares — which may continue to grow in value.

### 2. Compounding Through DRIP
**Dividend Reinvestment Plans (DRIP)** automatically reinvest your dividends to buy more shares. More shares → more dividends → even more shares. This creates a powerful compounding snowball effect over decades.

### 3. Inflation Defense via Dividend Growth
The best dividend stocks are **Dividend Growers** — companies that raise their dividend every year. These companies tend to be resilient businesses with pricing power, and their growing payments help offset inflation.

**Dividend Aristocrats:** S&P 500 companies that have raised dividends for 25+ consecutive years. Examples include Coca-Cola (60+ years), Johnson & Johnson, and Procter & Gamble.

## Key Metrics to Evaluate Dividend Stocks

### Dividend Yield
`Annual Dividend ÷ Stock Price × 100`

A 3% yield means you receive $3/year per $100 invested. **Beware of extremely high yields (8%+)** — they often signal a company in trouble that may cut its dividend.

**Sweet spot:** 2.5%–5% yield from a stable, growing business

### Dividend Payout Ratio
`Dividends Paid ÷ Earnings × 100`

Shows what percentage of earnings is paid as dividends. A ratio of 40–60% is generally healthy. Over 80% may mean the dividend is at risk.

### Dividend Growth Rate
How much has the dividend grown per year over 5–10 years? Aim for 5%+ annual growth, which means your income doubles roughly every 14 years.

## Building a Dividend Portfolio

### Start with Dividend ETFs
If you're new, start with **dividend-focused ETFs** rather than individual stocks:
- **VYM** (Vanguard High Dividend Yield) — broad exposure, low fees
- **SCHD** (Schwab US Dividend Equity) — quality focus, excellent track record
- **DGRO** (iShares Core Dividend Growth) — dividend growth focus

These instant diversification tools let you collect dividends across dozens or hundreds of companies with a single purchase.

### Diversify Across Sectors
Strong dividend sectors include:
- **Utilities** — stable, regulated cash flows
- **Consumer Staples** — people always need food and household goods
- **Healthcare** — aging populations drive steady demand
- **Financials** — banks and insurers with regular payouts
- **REITs** — required by law to pay 90% of income as dividends

## The Math of Dividend Income

**Goal: $2,000/month in dividend income**
- Required: $24,000/year in dividends
- At 4% average yield: $600,000 portfolio

That sounds like a lot — but with DRIP over 20–25 years of working life, $1,000/month invested at 7% total return gets you there.

The earlier you start, the less you need to contribute. Time is the most powerful variable.

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**1. Chasing yield** — A 12% yield usually means a dividend cut is coming. Research why it's so high before buying.

**2. Ignoring diversification** — Don't put 30% of your dividend portfolio in one company or sector.

**3. Forgetting about taxes** — Qualified dividends are taxed at lower capital gains rates. Non-qualified (REITs, some foreign stocks) are taxed as ordinary income. Account placement matters.

**4. Stopping contributions during downturns** — Bear markets are actually great for dividend investors; you buy more shares at lower prices, increasing your yield on cost.

## The Passive Income Flywheel

```
Invest → Receive Dividends → Reinvest → More Shares → More Dividends
```

This flywheel, once set in motion, becomes increasingly powerful. After 20+ years, many investors find their dividends alone exceed their original monthly contributions.

## Getting Started This Week

1. Open a brokerage account (Fidelity, Schwab, or Vanguard are excellent)
2. Buy one dividend ETF (SCHD or VYM are good starting points)
3. Enable DRIP — reinvest dividends automatically
4. Set a monthly automatic investment amount
5. Review annually; otherwise, ignore short-term price noise

## Bottom Line

Dividend investing transforms you from a passive saver into a business owner who gets paid regularly. The income isn't glamorous at first — but with patience and consistency, it becomes a financial foundation that makes everything else easier.

The best dividend portfolio is one you build quietly and forget to check.
