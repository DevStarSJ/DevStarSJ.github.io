The single most important decision in investing isn't which stock to pick — it's how you divide your money between different types of assets. Get asset allocation right, and individual security selection becomes almost secondary.

## What Is Asset Allocation?

Asset allocation is how you divide your investment portfolio among different asset classes:
- **Stocks (equities)**: Ownership stakes in companies — higher risk, higher long-term return
- **Bonds (fixed income)**: Loans to governments/corporations — lower risk, lower return, stability
- **Real estate (REITs)**: Property exposure without ownership headaches
- **Cash/equivalents**: Money market, T-bills — safest, lowest return
- **Alternative assets**: Commodities, international markets, private equity

The mix you choose determines roughly **90% of your portfolio's long-term performance** — more than any individual investment decision.

## Why Age Matters in Asset Allocation

Your ideal allocation shifts throughout life because **time horizon is everything**:

- More time = can afford to ride out market downturns = more stocks
- Less time = can't afford a crash right before you need the money = more bonds

**The classic rule**: Subtract your age from 110 (or 120 for aggressive investors) to get your stock percentage.
- Age 30: 110 − 30 = **80% stocks**
- Age 50: 110 − 50 = **60% stocks**
- Age 65: 110 − 65 = **45% stocks**

This is a starting point — not a law. Adjust based on your risk tolerance, goals, and income stability.

## Asset Allocation by Life Stage

### 🌱 20s: Aggressive Growth (80–100% Stocks)

You have 40+ years before retirement. Short-term volatility is irrelevant — what matters is capturing decades of compound growth.

**Sample Portfolio:**
- 70% Total Stock Market (U.S.)
- 20% International Stocks
- 10% Bonds

**Key focus:** Maximize contributions, not optimization. Even a "suboptimal" portfolio at 100% stocks beats a perfectly allocated portfolio with tiny contributions.

**Don't:** Move to bonds out of fear after a market drop. A 30% decline in your 20s is an *opportunity*, not a disaster.

### 💼 30s: Growth with a Foundation (70–80% Stocks)

You may now have a mortgage, kids, life insurance needs. Time horizon is still long, but complexity is growing.

**Sample Portfolio:**
- 60% U.S. Stocks
- 20% International Stocks
- 10% Bonds
- 10% REITs

**New priorities:**
- Maximize 401(k) to capture full employer match
- Open a Roth IRA if income permits
- Ensure adequate life/disability insurance

### ⚖️ 40s: Balanced Growth (60–70% Stocks)

Peak earning years for most people. Time to accelerate savings while gradually de-risking.

**Sample Portfolio:**
- 50% U.S. Stocks
- 20% International Stocks
- 20% Bonds
- 10% REITs

**Watch for:**
- "Sequence of returns risk" becomes more relevant as retirement approaches
- College funding if you have children
- Increasing life insurance if income significantly grew

### 🏁 50s: Conservative Growth (50–60% Stocks)

The decade before retirement is when protection starts to matter more. A major market crash at 58 with no recovery time is a serious risk.

**Sample Portfolio:**
- 40% U.S. Stocks
- 15% International Stocks
- 35% Bonds
- 10% Cash/Short-term

**Key moves:**
- "Catch-up contributions" — IRS allows extra contributions for 50+
- Consider target-date funds to automate the glide path
- Review Social Security strategy (when to claim?)

### 🌅 60s+: Income & Preservation (30–50% Stocks)

In retirement, you're drawing down — not accumulating. The focus shifts to:
1. Not running out of money (longevity risk)
2. Maintaining purchasing power (inflation risk)
3. Volatility management (sequence of returns risk)

**Sample Portfolio (Early Retirement):**
- 40% U.S. Stocks
- 10% International Stocks
- 35% Bonds
- 15% Cash/CDs

**The 4% Rule**: Research suggests withdrawing 4% of your portfolio annually provides ~30 years of sustainability. With current interest rates, some advisors suggest 3.5%.

## Beyond Age: Other Factors That Matter

### Risk Tolerance
A 35-year-old who panic-sells at every 10% drop should NOT have 80% stocks. Be honest with yourself.

**Test**: If your portfolio dropped 40% tomorrow (like 2008), would you:
A) Buy more (aggressive — higher stock allocation)
B) Stay the course (moderate — standard allocation)
C) Panic sell (conservative — lower stock allocation)

### Income Stability
- Teacher/government employee with pension? More risk tolerance.
- Commission-only salesperson? More conservative allocation.
- Entrepreneur with variable income? Extra cash buffer, slightly lower equity exposure.

### Other Assets
Own real estate? Your overall wealth is already "allocated" there — consider reducing REITs in your portfolio. Have a pension? That functions like a bond — can hold more stocks.

## Common Asset Allocation Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Too Conservative Too Early**
Being 60% bonds at age 35 out of fear of market risk guarantees you'll underperform inflation over 30 years.

**Mistake 2: Too Aggressive Too Late**
Being 90% stocks at age 62 means a bear market in year 1 of retirement could permanently impair your lifestyle.

**Mistake 3: Not Rebalancing**
If stocks surge, your 70/30 becomes 85/15. Rebalance annually (or when any asset class drifts 5+ percentage points from target).

**Mistake 4: Ignoring International Exposure**
The U.S. has been dominant for 15 years — that won't necessarily continue. 20–30% international exposure reduces home-country risk.

**Mistake 5: Forgetting Inflation**
Bonds feel safe but lose purchasing power during high inflation. A portfolio of 100% bonds in 2022 lost real value despite "safety."

## Simple Allocation for Beginners

Don't want to think about this too hard? Use a **Target-Date Fund**:
- Pick your expected retirement year: "Vanguard Target Retirement 2055 Fund"
- It automatically starts aggressive and gradually becomes conservative
- One fund, total diversification, auto-rebalancing
- Expense ratios around 0.10–0.15%

It's not the most optimized approach, but it's correct, automatic, and far better than paralysis.

## The Bottom Line

Asset allocation isn't about finding the "perfect" portfolio. It's about finding an allocation you can *stick with* through bull markets, bear markets, recessions, and booms — while slowly shifting it as your life changes.

Start somewhere. Stay disciplined. Rebalance periodically. Time does the rest.
