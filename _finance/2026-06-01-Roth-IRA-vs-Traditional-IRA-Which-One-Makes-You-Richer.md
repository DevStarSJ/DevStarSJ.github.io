Choosing between a Roth IRA and a Traditional IRA is one of the most important retirement decisions you'll ever make. The wrong choice could mean paying far more taxes than necessary — potentially costing you **$50,000 or more** over your lifetime.

Let's break it down clearly.

---

## The Core Difference: When You Pay Taxes

![Two paths representing Roth and Traditional IRA tax strategies](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1611974789855-9c2a0a7236a3?w=1000&auto=format&fit=crop)

*Photo by Towfiqu barbhuiya on Unsplash*

| | **Roth IRA** | **Traditional IRA** |
|---|---|---|
| Contributions | After-tax (no deduction) | Pre-tax (tax deductible) |
| Growth | Tax-free | Tax-deferred |
| Withdrawals | Tax-free | Taxed as income |
| RMDs | None | Required at age 73 |
| 2026 Contribution Limit | $7,000 ($8,000 if 50+) | $7,000 ($8,000 if 50+) |

---

## When Roth IRA Wins

**Choose Roth if you expect to be in a HIGHER tax bracket in retirement.**

This is the typical case for:
- **Young professionals** early in their career (low income now, high income later)
- **High earners** who expect income to grow significantly
- **Anyone under 40** who has decades of tax-free compounding ahead
- People who want **no required minimum distributions (RMDs)**
- Those who want to **leave tax-free wealth to heirs**

### The Roth Math:

Invest $7,000/year at 7% for 30 years:
- **Total invested:** $210,000
- **Account value:** ~$700,000
- **Tax at withdrawal:** $0 ✅

---

## When Traditional IRA Wins

**Choose Traditional if you're in a HIGH tax bracket NOW and expect lower taxes in retirement.**

This makes sense for:
- **Peak earning years** (50s with high salary)
- **High-income earners** wanting immediate tax relief
- People who expect to be in a **lower bracket** after retiring
- Those who will have **lower expenses** in retirement

### The Traditional Math:

At 35% tax bracket, $7,000 contribution saves you **$2,450 in taxes today**. If you invest that savings and retire in a 22% bracket, you come out ahead.

---

## The Income Limits (2026)

**Roth IRA phase-out:**
- Single: $150,000 – $165,000
- Married filing jointly: $236,000 – $246,000

**Traditional IRA deductibility phase-out (if covered by workplace plan):**
- Single: $79,000 – $89,000
- Married: $126,000 – $146,000

> 💡 **Backdoor Roth:** If you earn too much for Roth, contribute to a Traditional IRA (non-deductible) and convert it. This is legal and widely used by high earners.

---

## The Honest Answer: Do Both

If possible, consider this strategy:
1. **Max out your 401(k) to get the employer match** (free money first)
2. **Contribute to Roth IRA** if under the income limit
3. **Add to Traditional IRA** if you want the deduction

For most people in their 20s and 30s, **Roth wins** because of:
- Decades of tax-free growth
- Tax flexibility in retirement
- No RMD obligations

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Roth IRA = pay taxes now, withdraw tax-free**
- **Traditional IRA = deduct now, pay taxes later**
- Your current vs. future tax bracket is the deciding factor
- When in doubt, **diversify across both** for tax flexibility
- Start early — time in the market matters more than which account you choose

The best IRA is the one you actually contribute to consistently. Open one today.
