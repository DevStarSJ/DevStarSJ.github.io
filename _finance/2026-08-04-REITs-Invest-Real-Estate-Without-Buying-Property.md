Owning real estate is one of the most proven wealth-building strategies in history. There's just one problem: most people can't afford it.

A rental property requires a down payment of $50,000–$200,000+. It needs maintenance. It needs tenants. It needs your time.

REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts) solve all of this. They let you invest in real estate with as little as a single share — collecting rental income without ever unclogging a drain.

## What Is a REIT?

A Real Estate Investment Trust is a company that owns, operates, or finances income-producing real estate. By law, REITs must:

1. **Invest at least 75%** of total assets in real estate
2. **Derive at least 75%** of gross income from real estate rents, mortgages, or property sales
3. **Distribute at least 90%** of taxable income to shareholders as dividends

That last rule is the key. REITs are legally required to pay out most of their income. This is why they're one of the highest-yielding asset classes available to retail investors.

## How REITs Work

Imagine a shopping mall that generates $50 million in rent annually. Instead of one billionaire owning the whole thing, a REIT lets millions of investors own fractional shares of that same mall — and collect proportional dividend income.

You buy REIT shares just like stocks, through any brokerage account. The REIT collects rent from tenants, takes a management cut, and distributes the rest to shareholders.

## Types of REITs

### Equity REITs (Most Common)
Own and operate income-producing properties. They make money from rents.

**Examples of sectors:**
- **Residential:** Apartment complexes, single-family rentals
- **Commercial:** Office buildings, retail centers
- **Industrial:** Warehouses, distribution centers (huge demand from e-commerce)
- **Healthcare:** Hospitals, medical offices, senior living
- **Data Centers:** Server farms (one of the fastest-growing REIT sectors)
- **Cell Towers:** Infrastructure REITs (e.g., American Tower)
- **Self-Storage:** Storage unit facilities

### Mortgage REITs (mREITs)
Don't own property — instead, they finance real estate by holding mortgages or mortgage-backed securities. They earn income from the spread between short-term borrowing rates and long-term mortgage rates. Higher risk, often higher yield.

### Hybrid REITs
Combination of equity and mortgage REITs.

### Public vs. Private REITs
- **Publicly traded REITs:** Listed on stock exchanges, fully liquid, easy to buy/sell
- **Non-traded public REITs:** Registered with SEC but not exchange-listed, less liquid
- **Private REITs:** Not registered, accredited investors only, illiquid

For most investors: stick with publicly traded REITs.

## Why Invest in REITs?

### 1. Passive Income
REITs often yield 3–8%+ annually in dividends. For passive income seekers, this is a core holding.

### 2. Diversification
Real estate behaves differently from stocks and bonds. Adding REITs to a portfolio can reduce overall volatility.

### 3. Inflation Hedge
Rents and property values tend to rise with inflation. REITs provide a natural hedge against purchasing power erosion.

### 4. Accessibility
You can invest in institutional-quality real estate (skyscrapers, logistics parks) with $50.

### 5. Liquidity
Unlike physical property, you can sell REIT shares instantly during market hours.

### 6. Professional Management
Expert teams manage the properties. You don't deal with tenants, repairs, or vacancies.

## REIT Risks to Understand

**Interest Rate Risk**
REITs are sensitive to interest rate changes. When rates rise, REITs often decline because:
- Borrowing costs increase (hurts profits)
- High-yield bonds become more competitive (reduces REIT's yield advantage)

**Sector-Specific Risk**
Office REITs struggled after remote work trends. Retail REITs were hammered by e-commerce. Understand what you own.

**Leverage**
REITs use significant debt to acquire properties. High leverage amplifies both gains and losses.

**Dividend Cuts**
If cash flow drops (vacancies, rent defaults), REITs can cut dividends. Always research the payout ratio and occupancy rates.

**Tax Treatment**
REIT dividends are typically taxed as ordinary income (not the lower qualified dividend rate). Hold REITs in tax-advantaged accounts (IRA, 401k) when possible.

## Key Metrics for Evaluating REITs

### Funds From Operations (FFO)
The standard profitability measure for REITs. Like earnings per share for regular stocks, but adjusted for real estate depreciation. Use FFO (not EPS) to evaluate REITs.

### Price-to-FFO
Like P/E ratio for REITs. Lower = potentially undervalued.

### Dividend Yield
Annual dividend divided by current share price. Compare within the same sector.

### Payout Ratio
Dividends paid as a percentage of FFO. A ratio over 90–100% may be unsustainable.

### Occupancy Rate
What percentage of properties are occupied? Higher is better. Watch for trends.

### Debt-to-Equity Ratio
How leveraged is the REIT? Higher debt = higher risk.

## How to Invest in REITs

### Option 1: Individual REIT Stocks
Buy shares of specific REITs through any brokerage. Requires research to select quality companies.

**Examples of well-known publicly traded REITs (for research purposes):**
- Realty Income (O) — Monthly dividends, diversified commercial
- Prologis (PLD) — Industrial/logistics
- American Tower (AMT) — Cell tower infrastructure
- Welltower (WELL) — Healthcare
- Simon Property Group (SPG) — Premium retail

### Option 2: REIT ETFs/Index Funds
Instant diversification across dozens of REITs. Lower individual risk, easier management.

**Popular REIT ETFs:**
- VNQ (Vanguard Real Estate ETF) — Most popular, low cost
- SCHH (Schwab US REIT ETF) — Very low expense ratio
- RQI, RNP — Closed-end fund options for income focus

### Option 3: REIT Mutual Funds
Professionally managed, similar to ETFs but actively managed. Higher fees.

## Sample REIT Allocation Strategy

For a diversified income portfolio:

| Allocation | Purpose |
|---|---|
| 5–10% of portfolio | Core diversification, inflation hedge |
| REIT ETF (e.g., VNQ) | Broad diversification |
| 1–2 sector-specific REITs | Higher conviction picks |

Don't over-concentrate. REITs should complement your overall portfolio, not dominate it.

## REIT vs. Owning Rental Property

| Factor | REIT | Rental Property |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum investment | ~$50 | $50,000–$200,000+ |
| Liquidity | Same-day sale | Months to sell |
| Management | Professional | You (or property manager) |
| Control | None | Full |
| Tax complexity | Moderate | High |
| Income | Quarterly/monthly dividends | Monthly rent |
| Diversification | Instant | Single property |

REITs win on accessibility and ease. Direct ownership wins on control and tax optimization. Many investors do both.
