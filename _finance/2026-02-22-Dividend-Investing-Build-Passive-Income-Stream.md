## What Are Dividends?

When a company earns profit, it can:
1. Reinvest into the business (growth)
2. Buy back its own shares
3. **Pay dividends to shareholders**

Dividends are cash payments made to shareholders, typically quarterly. When you own a dividend-paying stock, you receive a portion of the company's profits simply for holding the shares.

**Key terms:**
- **Dividend yield:** Annual dividend / stock price (e.g., $4 dividend on $100 stock = 4% yield)
- **Dividend per share (DPS):** Dollar amount paid per share per period
- **Ex-dividend date:** Date by which you must own shares to receive the next dividend
- **Payout ratio:** % of earnings paid as dividends (lower = more sustainable)
- **DRIP:** Dividend Reinvestment Plan — automatically reinvest dividends to buy more shares

## How to Evaluate Dividend Stocks

### The 5 Key Metrics

**1. Dividend Yield (target: 2-6%)**
Too low (<1.5%): minimal income
Sweet spot: 3-5%
Too high (>7%): often unsustainable — may signal trouble

The "yield trap": A 10% yield that gets cut becomes worthless income.

**2. Payout Ratio (target: <75% for stocks, <90% for REITs)**
- Payout ratio = Dividends / Earnings
- 40% payout: Very safe, room to grow
- 60-70% payout: Acceptable, monitor
- 90%+: Risky, may cut if earnings dip

**3. Dividend Growth Rate (target: 5%+ annually)**
A stock that grows its dividend 7% annually doubles the payout in 10 years.

| Starting Yield | Annual Growth | Yield-on-Cost in 10 Years |
|----------------|---------------|--------------------------|
| 3% | 5% | 4.9% |
| 3% | 7% | 5.9% |
| 3% | 10% | 7.8% |

This is why dividend growth investors often accept lower starting yields.

**4. Dividend History**
- 10+ years without cuts: Strong signal
- 25+ years (Dividend Aristocrat): Excellent
- Cut history: Red flag — research why

**5. Free Cash Flow Coverage**
- FCF payout ratio = Dividends / Free Cash Flow
- Should be <80% for safety
- FCF is more reliable than earnings for dividend safety

### Red Flags to Avoid

- 🚩 Yield suddenly jumped much higher (often means price crashed)
- 🚩 Payout ratio > 100% (paying more than earned)
- 🚩 Declining revenue for multiple years
- 🚩 Heavy debt load (interest eats into dividend safety)
- 🚩 Dividend growth stopped while earnings grew (management warning sign)

### Creating Monthly Income from Quarterly Payers

Most U.S. stocks pay quarterly, but you can structure your portfolio to receive checks every month:

**Month 1 (January, April, July, October):**
Companies paying in these months: JNJ, PG, ABT, MMM

**Month 2 (February, May, August, November):**
Companies: KO, PEP, GIS, MCD

**Month 3 (March, June, September, December):**
Companies: T, VZ, O (monthly), MAIN (monthly)

Combine quarterly payers across all three cycles + add monthly payers (REITs like O, BDCs like MAIN) for true monthly income.

## The Power of DRIP (Dividend Reinvestment)

DRIP automatically reinvests your dividends to buy more shares. This creates a powerful compounding effect.

**Example: $50,000 in SCHD at 3.5% yield**

| Approach | After 20 Years |
|----------|---------------|
| Spend dividends | $189,000 (stock growth only) |
| DRIP dividends | $318,000 (29% more shares) |

During accumulation phase (pre-retirement): Always DRIP.
During income phase (retirement): Take cash dividends as income.

## Tax Considerations for Dividend Investors

### Qualified vs. Ordinary Dividends

**Qualified dividends** (taxed at capital gains rates — 0%, 15%, or 20%):
- Most U.S. stock dividends
- Requirements: held 60+ days, from U.S. corporation or qualified foreign

**Ordinary dividends** (taxed as regular income — up to 37%):
- REITs (most of the dividend)
- BDCs
- Money market dividends
- Short-term holdings

**Tax-efficient placement:**
| Account Type | Best Holdings |
|-------------|---------------|
| Roth IRA | REITs, BDCs (high ordinary income) |
| 401(k) | Same — shelter from taxes |
| Taxable brokerage | Qualified dividend stocks |

Keep high-yield, ordinary-income payers in tax-sheltered accounts to avoid the tax drag.

## Common Dividend Investing Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Yield chasing**
Picking stocks purely for highest yield often leads to dividend cuts and capital loss.

**Mistake 2: Ignoring total return**
A 6% dividend stock that declines 6% annually has a 0% total return. Growth matters.

**Mistake 3: Underdiversification**
10 positions in 2 sectors isn't diversification. Aim for 20+ positions across 5+ sectors or use ETFs.

**Mistake 4: Tax misplacement**
Holding REITs in taxable accounts turns 90%+ of returns into ordinary income. Use retirement accounts.

**Mistake 5: Selling during volatility**
Dividend investors should care most about the income stream, not the daily price. Price drops during downturns can be buying opportunities (higher yield).

**Mistake 6: Neglecting dividend growth**
A 2% yield growing 10% annually is worth more long-term than a static 5% yield. Model both.
