# Covered Call Options: Earn Passive Income from Stocks You Already Own

What if your stock portfolio paid you a monthly income — even when the market goes sideways? That's exactly what **covered calls** do. It's one of the most popular income-generating strategies used by professional investors, and it's accessible to individual investors too.

![Stock trading on multiple screens](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1611974789855-9c2a0a7236a3?w=800)
*Photo by [Maxim Hopman](https://unsplash.com/@nampoh) on Unsplash*

Covered calls won't make you rich overnight, but they can add **2-5% annual income** on top of your existing stock returns — essentially a dividend you create yourself.

## What Is a Covered Call?

A covered call is an options strategy where you:
1. **Own at least 100 shares** of a stock
2. **Sell a call option** on those shares, giving someone else the right to buy them at a set price by a set date

In return for selling that right, you collect an **immediate cash premium** — that's your income.

You're "covered" because you already own the shares, so if the option buyer exercises their right, you simply hand over shares you already own.

## The Basic Mechanics

Let's say you own 100 shares of **Apple (AAPL)** currently trading at $200.

You sell 1 call option:
- **Strike price:** $210 (the price at which you'd sell your shares)
- **Expiration:** 30 days from now
- **Premium received:** $3.00 per share = **$300 immediate cash**

### Three Possible Outcomes:

**Scenario 1: Apple stays below $210 at expiration**
- Option expires worthless
- You keep your 100 shares
- You keep the $300 premium
- **Result: $300 profit, still own the stock ✅**

**Scenario 2: Apple rises above $210 at expiration**
- Option buyer exercises their right to buy at $210
- You sell your 100 shares at $210 (even if the stock is at $220)
- You keep the $300 premium
- **Result: You made $10/share gain + $300 premium, but missed upside above $210 ⚠️**

**Scenario 3: Apple falls significantly**
- Option expires worthless (good)
- You keep the $300 premium (reduces your loss)
- But your shares are worth less
- **Result: Premium cushions the loss, but you still hold declining stock ❌**

## Understanding Premium Income

The premium you collect depends on:

1. **Time to expiration** — More time = more premium. A 60-day option pays more than a 30-day option.
2. **Distance from current price** — Options closer to the current stock price (at-the-money) pay more premium.
3. **Implied volatility** — More volatile stocks pay higher premiums. NVDA options pay more than JNJ options.

### Typical Annual Yields from Covered Calls:

| Stock Type | Monthly Premium | Annualized Yield |
|------------|----------------|------------------|
| Conservative (JNJ, KO) | 0.5-1% | 6-12% |
| Moderate (AAPL, MSFT) | 1-2% | 12-24% |
| High volatility (NVDA, TSLA) | 2-4% | 24-48% |

*Note: Higher yields come with higher risk of shares being called away.*

## The Covered Call Math: Monthly Income Example

Own 100 shares of AAPL at $200 = **$20,000 position**

Sell monthly covered calls at $215 strike:
- Monthly premium: $2.50/share = **$250/month**
- Annual premium income: **$3,000**
- Yield on investment: **15% additional income** on top of stock performance

Over 12 months with the stock never rising above $215:
- Stock position value: $20,000 (unchanged)
- Premium collected: $3,000
- **Total return: 15% from premiums alone**

## Best Stocks for Covered Calls

### Ideal candidates have:
- ✅ **You own 100+ shares already**
- ✅ **Liquid options market** (high open interest, tight spreads)
- ✅ **Moderate-to-high implied volatility**
- ✅ **Stocks you're comfortable holding** (if called away, you're okay)
- ❌ **Earnings announcements** within expiration period (avoid)

### Popular covered call stocks in 2026:
- **Apple (AAPL)** — Liquid, consistent premiums
- **Microsoft (MSFT)** — Steady, good premium
- **Nvidia (NVDA)** — High premiums, but volatile
- **Tesla (TSLA)** — Very high premiums, very volatile

## Step-by-Step: How to Sell a Covered Call

### Step 1: Ensure you own 100+ shares
Each options contract represents 100 shares. You need at least 100 shares per contract.

### Step 2: Choose your strike price
- **At-the-money (ATM):** Strike = current price. Highest premium, highest chance of being called away.
- **Out-of-the-money (OTM):** Strike > current price. Lower premium, more room for stock appreciation.
- **Rule of thumb:** For income + holding stock, choose 5-10% above current price.

### Step 3: Choose expiration date
- **Weekly (7 days):** Low premium, repeat frequently
- **Monthly (30 days):** Sweet spot for most investors
- **Quarterly (90 days):** Higher premium, less flexibility

### Step 4: Place the sell order
In your brokerage (Fidelity, Schwab, TD Ameritrade):
- Go to Options chain
- Select expiration date
- Find your target strike price
- Select "Sell to Open" → "Covered Call"
- Review the premium and confirm

### Step 5: Manage or let expire
- If stock rises toward strike: Consider buying the option back (closing the position)
- If stock stays flat: Let it expire worthless and repeat next month
- If stock rises above strike: Shares get called away; repeat with new position

## Risks to Understand

### Risk 1: Capped upside
If your stock rockets 30% but your strike is 5% away, you miss 25% of the gain. Covered calls limit your upside.

### Risk 2: Stock can still fall
The premium reduces but doesn't eliminate downside risk. If AAPL drops 40%, your $250 premium doesn't help much.

### Risk 3: Tax consequences
In taxable accounts, premiums are typically taxed as short-term capital gains. Selling calls on long-term positions can affect their long-term status. Consult a tax professional.

### Risk 4: Assignment at inconvenient times
If the stock rises above your strike before expiration, you may be assigned early (especially around dividend dates). Monitor positions around dividends.

## Advanced Strategy: The Wheel Strategy

The **Wheel Strategy** extends covered calls into a full income system:

1. **Sell cash-secured puts** on a stock you want to own
2. If assigned, you buy the shares at your strike price
3. **Sell covered calls** on those shares
4. If called away, repeat from step 1

This creates a continuous income cycle — premium income whether you own the stock or not.

## Covered Calls in Retirement Accounts

You can sell covered calls in IRAs (traditional and Roth), which is a significant advantage:
- No immediate tax on premium income
- In a Roth IRA: all premium income grows tax-free

The IRS allows covered calls in IRAs as they're considered "Level 1" options — the lowest risk category. Ask your broker to enable options trading in your IRA.

## Key Takeaways

- Covered calls generate monthly income from stocks you already own
- You sell the right for someone to buy your shares at a set price
- Collect premium immediately — even if the option expires worthless
- Best for stocks you're comfortable selling at the strike price
- Typical yields: 12-24% additional annual income on moderate-volatility stocks
- The Wheel Strategy creates continuous income cycles
- Works in taxable accounts and IRAs

Covered calls transform a passive buy-and-hold portfolio into an active income engine. If you own stocks and aren't selling covered calls, you're leaving money on the table every single month.
