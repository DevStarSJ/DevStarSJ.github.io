Your credit score is a three-digit number that follows you through every major financial decision — your mortgage rate, car loan, apartment application, even some job applications. Most people don't know how it's calculated. Here's everything you need to know to understand and improve yours.

## What Is a Credit Score?

A credit score is a numerical summary of your creditworthiness — how likely you are to repay debt. The most widely used system is the **FICO Score**, which ranges from 300 to 850.

| Score Range | Rating | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| 800-850 | Exceptional | Best rates available |
| 740-799 | Very Good | Better than average rates |
| 670-739 | Good | Near-average rates |
| 580-669 | Fair | Higher interest rates |
| 300-579 | Poor | Difficulty getting approved |

## The 5 Factors That Make Up Your Score

Understanding these factors is the key to improving your score strategically.

### 1. Payment History (35%)
The single biggest factor. Every on-time payment helps. Every late payment hurts — and stays on your report for 7 years.

**Rule:** Never miss a payment. Set up autopay for at least the minimum on every account.

### 2. Credit Utilization (30%)
How much of your available credit you're using. If you have $10,000 in total credit limits and you're carrying $3,000 in balances, your utilization is 30%.

**Rule:** Keep utilization below 30% (ideally below 10%) for the best score impact. Paying down balances is the fastest way to boost your score.

### 3. Length of Credit History (15%)
How long you've had credit. Older accounts help more.

**Rule:** Don't close old credit card accounts unless they have annual fees you can't justify. Even unused accounts help your average age.

### 4. Credit Mix (10%)
Lenders want to see you can handle different types of credit: revolving (credit cards) and installment (loans, mortgage).

**Rule:** Don't open loans just to diversify. But if you have only credit cards, a small personal loan that you pay back reliably can help.

### 5. New Credit Inquiries (10%)
Every time you apply for new credit, a "hard inquiry" appears. Too many inquiries in a short window signals financial stress.

**Rule:** Don't apply for multiple new cards or loans in a short period. Space out applications by 6+ months.

## How To Check Your Score for Free

- **AnnualCreditReport.com** — free reports from all three bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion) every 12 months
- **Credit Karma** — free ongoing monitoring (Equifax + TransUnion)
- **Your credit card** — many issuers (Chase, Capital One, Discover) show your FICO score in the app

## How To Improve Your Score: Ranked by Impact

### Immediate Impact (weeks)

**1. Pay down credit card balances**
If you're at 50%+ utilization, paying down to below 30% can add 30-50 points quickly. Below 10% can add another 20-30 points.

**2. Dispute errors on your credit report**
1 in 5 credit reports contain errors. Pull your reports and look for:
- Accounts that aren't yours
- Late payments that were actually on time
- Incorrect balances
- Closed accounts showing as open

Dispute errors directly with the credit bureau online. Errors can add or remove 50+ points.

**3. Request a credit limit increase**
If your card issuer increases your limit without a hard inquiry, your utilization ratio drops instantly — even without paying down debt.

### Medium-Term Impact (1-6 months)

**4. Never miss a payment — ever**
Set autopay for minimums on all accounts. Even one 30-day late payment can drop your score 60-110 points.

**5. Become an authorized user**
Ask a family member with excellent credit to add you to their old, well-managed card. You don't even need to use the card — their positive history can boost your score.

### Long-Term Building (6+ months)

**6. Open a secured credit card (if rebuilding)**
If your score is under 580, a secured card (you deposit collateral) reports to credit bureaus and builds history.

**7. Keep old accounts open**
Length of history matters. A 10-year-old card you don't use still helps your score.

## The Credit Score Myths

**Myth: Checking your own score hurts it.**
False. Soft inquiries (your own checks, pre-approvals) don't affect your score. Only hard inquiries do.

**Myth: Carrying a small balance helps your score.**
False. Utilization below 10% gives the best score impact. Carrying a balance just costs you interest.

**Myth: Closing a card improves your score.**
Usually false. Closing cards reduces available credit (raises utilization) and can lower your average account age.

**Myth: You have one credit score.**
False. You have multiple scores — different bureaus, different FICO versions, VantageScore. They're usually within 20-30 points of each other, but not identical.

## A Realistic Timeline for Improvement

| Starting Score | Target | Realistic Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| 580 → 670 | Good | 6-12 months |
| 670 → 740 | Very Good | 12-24 months |
| 740 → 800 | Exceptional | 2-4 years |

Credit improvement is a marathon, not a sprint. But the right actions today will have compounding effects for years.
