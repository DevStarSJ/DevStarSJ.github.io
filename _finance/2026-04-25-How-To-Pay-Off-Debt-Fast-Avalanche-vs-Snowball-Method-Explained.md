Debt is the single biggest obstacle to building wealth. While you're paying 20% interest on a credit card balance, no investment on earth can consistently beat that cost.

The good news: there are proven, systematic methods to eliminate debt faster than you think. Here's how to choose the right one.

## The Two Proven Debt Payoff Methods

### Method 1: The Debt Avalanche (Math Wins)

**How it works:**
1. List all debts by interest rate, highest to lowest
2. Pay minimums on everything
3. Throw every extra dollar at the highest-rate debt
4. When it's gone, attack the next highest rate

**Why it works:** You minimize total interest paid. Mathematically, this is always the optimal strategy.

**Example:**
- Credit Card A: $5,000 at 24% APR
- Credit Card B: $2,000 at 18% APR  
- Car Loan: $8,000 at 7% APR
- Student Loan: $15,000 at 5% APR

With avalanche, you hammer Credit Card A first, saving thousands in interest.

**Best for:** Analytical personalities who stay motivated by seeing numbers shrink.

### Method 2: The Debt Snowball (Psychology Wins)

**How it works:**
1. List all debts by balance, smallest to largest (ignore interest rates)
2. Pay minimums on everything
3. Throw every extra dollar at the smallest balance
4. When it's wiped out, roll that payment into the next one

**Why it works:** Quick wins. Eliminating a debt completely — even a small one — releases dopamine and builds momentum.

**Research backs this:** A Harvard Business Review study found snowball users were more likely to become completely debt-free, despite paying more in total interest.

**Best for:** People who need motivation and quick psychological wins to stay on track.

## Avalanche vs Snowball: A Real Comparison

Let's say you have $500/month to put toward debt beyond minimums:

| Debt | Balance | Rate | Minimum |
|------|---------|------|---------|
| Medical bill | $800 | 0% | $50 |
| Credit card | $3,200 | 22% | $65 |
| Car loan | $9,500 | 8% | $200 |
| Student loan | $18,000 | 6% | $180 |

**Avalanche result:** Pay off in ~42 months, total interest: ~$4,100  
**Snowball result:** Pay off in ~44 months, total interest: ~$4,800

The difference is ~$700 and 2 months. For many people, the psychological win of eliminating the medical bill first is worth that cost.

## The Hybrid Approach

Can't decide? Use a hybrid:

1. **Start with one snowball win** — knock out the smallest debt for momentum
2. **Switch to avalanche** — now attack by interest rate

This gives you the psychological kickstart plus the mathematical efficiency.

## Finding Extra Money to Throw at Debt

The methods only work if you have extra cash. Here's where to find it:

**Immediate wins:**
- Cancel unused subscriptions (average American wastes $219/month)
- Sell things you don't use (eBay, Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist)
- Pause retirement contributions above any employer match (temporarily)

**Medium-term:**
- Negotiate lower interest rates (call your credit card company — it works more than you'd think)
- Balance transfer to 0% APR card (be cautious of fees and the payoff deadline)
- Refinance high-rate loans

**Income side:**
- Weekend gig work — driving, delivery, freelance
- Sell a skill — tutoring, design, writing, yard work
- Overtime at current job

## The Debt Payoff Mindset

**Track every payment.** Use a spreadsheet or app (YNAB, Debt Payoff Planner). Seeing progress is fuel.

**Celebrate milestones.** Every debt eliminated is a win. Reward yourself modestly — not in ways that create new debt.

**Don't add new debt.** While paying off debt, avoid new credit card charges you can't pay off that month. This is non-negotiable.

**Automate payments.** Set up automatic payments above minimums. Remove the decision from your brain entirely.

## Step-by-Step Action Plan

**Week 1:**
- List every debt: creditor, balance, interest rate, minimum payment
- Calculate your total monthly minimum payments
- Find your "extra" amount: income – expenses – minimums

**Week 2:**
- Choose your method (avalanche, snowball, or hybrid)
- Set up automatic minimums on all debts
- Direct extra money to your target debt

**Month 1:**
- Track your first month's progress
- Look for one spending cut to free up more money
- Note how the method feels psychologically

The math says avalanche. The psychology often says snowball. The best method is whichever one you'll actually stick with.

Choose it. Automate it. Don't look back.
