## What Is a REIT?

A Real Estate Investment Trust is a company that owns, operates, or finances income-producing real estate. Congress created REITs in 1960 to give everyday investors access to large-scale real estate — similar to how mutual funds give access to stocks.

**The legal requirement that makes REITs special:**
REITs must distribute at least **90% of their taxable income** to shareholders as dividends. This is why REITs typically offer higher dividend yields than most stocks.

## How REITs Make Money

REITs generate income through:
- **Rent collection** from tenants in properties they own
- **Mortgage interest** from real estate loans they finance
- **Property appreciation** when they sell holdings
- **Development profits** from building new properties

## Types of REITs

### Equity REITs (Most Common)
Own and operate income-producing real estate directly.

**Major subcategories:**
- **Retail REITs** — Shopping malls, strip malls (Simon Property Group)
- **Residential REITs** — Apartments, single-family rentals (AvalonBay, Invitation Homes)
- **Office REITs** — Office buildings (Boston Properties)
- **Industrial REITs** — Warehouses, distribution centers (Prologis — Amazon's landlord)
- **Healthcare REITs** — Hospitals, medical offices, senior housing (Welltower)
- **Data Center REITs** — Server farms (Equinix, Digital Realty)
- **Cell Tower REITs** — Wireless infrastructure (American Tower, Crown Castle)
- **Self-Storage REITs** — Storage facilities (Public Storage, Extra Space)

### Mortgage REITs (mREITs)
Finance real estate by originating or buying mortgages and mortgage-backed securities. Higher yields but more complex and sensitive to interest rates.

### Hybrid REITs
Combine both equity and mortgage strategies.

## REIT Valuation: Key Metrics

Unlike regular stocks, REITs use different valuation metrics because depreciation distorts earnings.

### FFO (Funds From Operations)
The most important REIT metric. Think of it as earnings for REITs.

`FFO = Net Income + Depreciation - Gains on Property Sales`

**Price-to-FFO** is to REITs what P/E is to regular stocks. A P/FFO of 15–20x is typical for quality REITs.

### AFFO (Adjusted FFO)
Even more refined — subtracts capital expenditures needed to maintain properties.

### Net Asset Value (NAV)
The estimated market value of all properties minus liabilities. If a REIT trades at a significant discount to NAV, it may be undervalued.

## Why REITs Belong in Your Portfolio

### 1. Inflation Hedge
Real estate values and rents typically rise with inflation. REITs provide built-in inflation protection that bonds do not.

### 2. Low Correlation to Stocks
REITs don't always move in sync with the stock market. They provide genuine diversification.

### 3. High Dividend Income
Due to the 90% distribution requirement, REIT yields are typically 3–6%, well above the S&P 500's ~1.5% average yield.

### 4. Professional Management
You get exposure to billion-dollar real estate portfolios managed by professionals — no plumbers, leases, or vacancy headaches.

### 5. Liquidity
Unlike actual real estate, publicly traded REITs can be bought or sold in seconds during market hours.

## Risks to Understand

### Interest Rate Sensitivity
Rising interest rates hurt REITs in two ways:
1. Higher borrowing costs for the REIT
2. Competing yields from bonds make REITs less attractive

### Sector-Specific Risk
- Office REITs suffered from remote work trends
- Retail REITs faced e-commerce disruption
- Always understand what sectors you're buying

### Leverage Risk
REITs use significant debt. In a recession, overleveraged REITs can cut dividends or face distress.

---

## Tax Considerations

REIT dividends are mostly classified as **ordinary income** (not qualified dividends), meaning they're taxed at your marginal rate. For this reason, REITs are often best held in **tax-advantaged accounts** (IRA, 401k) where you avoid annual taxation on distributions.

## How to Start

1. **Open a brokerage account** (Fidelity, Schwab, or Vanguard)
2. **Start with an ETF** (VNQ or SCHH for simplicity)
3. **Invest consistently** — DCA (dollar-cost averaging) works well for volatile assets
4. **Reinvest dividends** to compound growth
5. **Consider tax placement** — hold in IRA when possible

## Key Takeaways

✅ REITs offer real estate exposure without buying property

✅ Equity REITs own properties; mortgage REITs own loans
