Real estate has made more millionaires than almost any other asset class. The problem? Buying property requires a large down payment, mortgage debt, maintenance headaches, and dealing with tenants. Most people can't or don't want to do that.

Enter **REITs — Real Estate Investment Trusts** — the way to own real estate without owning property.

## What Is a REIT?

A Real Estate Investment Trust (REIT) is a company that owns, operates, or finances income-producing real estate. Think shopping malls, office buildings, apartment complexes, warehouses, hospitals, data centers, and cell towers.

By law, REITs must distribute **at least 90% of their taxable income as dividends** to shareholders. This makes them one of the most reliable sources of dividend income in the investment world.

You can buy REIT shares on the stock exchange just like buying Apple or Google stock — and you'll receive quarterly (sometimes monthly) dividend payments.

## Types of REITs

### Equity REITs (Most Common)
Own and operate physical properties. Income comes from rents.
- **Residential:** Apartment complexes, single-family rentals
- **Commercial:** Office buildings, retail centers
- **Industrial:** Warehouses, logistics facilities
- **Healthcare:** Hospitals, senior living, medical offices
- **Data Centers:** Server farms for cloud computing
- **Cell Towers:** American Tower, Crown Castle

### Mortgage REITs (mREITs)
Don't own properties — they finance them. Income comes from interest on mortgages. Higher yields but more interest-rate sensitive.

### Hybrid REITs
Combination of equity and mortgage REIT strategies.

## Why REITs Are Compelling for Passive Income

### 1. High Dividend Yields
REITs typically yield **3% to 8%** annually, significantly higher than S&P 500 average dividends (~1.5%). Some specialty REITs yield even more.

### 2. Low Barrier to Entry
You can invest in REITs with as little as the price of one share — sometimes $10 to $50. No down payment, no mortgage, no credit check.

### 3. Diversification
A single REIT might own hundreds of properties across multiple cities. One REIT ETF might hold dozens of REITs. Instant diversification across real estate.

### 4. Liquidity
Unlike actual real estate (which takes months to sell), REIT shares can be sold in seconds during market hours.

### 5. Professional Management
You don't deal with tenants, repairs, or property management. Professionals handle everything.

## REIT ETFs: The Easy Way to Diversify

If picking individual REITs feels overwhelming, REIT ETFs give instant diversification:

- **VNQ (Vanguard Real Estate ETF)** — Most popular, low expense ratio (0.12%)
- **SCHH (Schwab U.S. REIT ETF)** — Very low cost, broad exposure
- **RQI, RNP** — Actively managed, higher yields
- **REET** — Global REIT exposure including international markets

## Tax Considerations

REIT dividends are typically taxed as **ordinary income** (not qualified dividends), which means they're taxed at your regular income tax rate. To minimize taxes:

- **Hold REITs in tax-advantaged accounts** (IRA, 401(k), Roth IRA) where dividends grow tax-deferred or tax-free
- If held in taxable accounts, a portion may qualify for the 20% pass-through deduction (Section 199A)

## Risks to Understand

- **Interest rate sensitivity** — Rising rates can reduce REIT valuations and make their dividends less attractive relative to bonds
- **Sector concentration** — Retail REITs faced pressure from e-commerce; office REITs face work-from-home headwinds
- **Economic cycles** — Recessions can reduce occupancy rates and rent income
- **Not a guaranteed return** — Dividends can be cut, especially with mREITs

## How to Start Investing in REITs

1. **Open a brokerage account** — Fidelity, Schwab, Vanguard, or M1 Finance
2. **Consider a tax-advantaged account** — Roth IRA is ideal for REITs
3. **Start with a REIT ETF** — VNQ is a safe, diversified starting point
4. **Research individual REITs** — Look at occupancy rates, FFO (Funds From Operations), dividend history, and payout ratio
5. **Reinvest dividends** — Use DRIP (dividend reinvestment plan) to compound growth

## Building a REIT Income Stream

With $50,000 invested in REITs yielding an average of 4.5%:
- **Annual dividends:** ~$2,250
- **Monthly income:** ~$188

With $200,000:
- **Annual dividends:** ~$9,000
- **Monthly income:** ~$750

It's not "quit your job" money at first — but as you reinvest and grow, the income compounds.

## The Bottom Line

REITs are one of the most powerful tools for building passive income. They offer real estate exposure without the hassles of property ownership, deliver consistent dividends, and can be started with any budget. For investors seeking income, diversification, and long-term wealth building, REITs deserve a place in every portfolio.
