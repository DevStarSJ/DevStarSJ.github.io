## How Freelancer Taxes Work

As a freelancer, you're both employer and employee. This means you're responsible for:

- **Income tax**: Federal and state taxes on your earnings
- **Self-employment tax**: Social Security and Medicare (15.3%)
- **Quarterly estimated taxes**: Payments due four times a year
- **Business expenses tracking**: Deductions to lower your bill

### The Self-Employment Tax Breakdown

When you're employed, your employer pays half of Social Security and Medicare. As a freelancer, you pay both halves:

- **Social Security**: 12.4% (on first $168,600 in 2026)
- **Medicare**: 2.9% (no cap)
- **Additional Medicare**: 0.9% (income over $200K single/$250K married)

**Total**: 15.3% on top of regular income tax

## Quarterly Estimated Taxes

### Why You Must Pay Quarterly

The IRS expects taxes paid as you earn. If you owe $1,000+ at tax time, you may face penalties for not paying quarterly.

### Due Dates

| Quarter | Earning Period | Due Date |
|---------|---------------|----------|
| Q1 | Jan 1 - Mar 31 | April 15 |
| Q2 | Apr 1 - May 31 | June 15 |
| Q3 | Jun 1 - Aug 31 | September 15 |
| Q4 | Sep 1 - Dec 31 | January 15 |

### How Much to Pay

**Safe Harbor Method**: Pay 100% of last year's tax liability (110% if income over $150K) divided by 4.

**Current Year Method**: Estimate this year's income and pay 25% of expected tax each quarter.

## Essential Tax Deductions

### Home Office Deduction

If you work from home regularly, you can deduct:

**Simplified Method**:
- $5 per square foot
- Maximum 300 sq ft = $1,500

**Regular Method**:
- Calculate percentage of home used for business
- Deduct that percentage of rent/mortgage, utilities, insurance

**Requirements**:
- Space used regularly and exclusively for business
- Principal place of business

### Equipment and Technology

Deductible items:
- Computer and monitors
- Software subscriptions
- Phone and phone plan (business portion)
- Printer, scanner, office equipment
- Website and hosting costs

### Professional Services

- Accountant and bookkeeper fees
- Legal services
- Business coaching
- Professional memberships

### Marketing and Advertising

- Website design
- Business cards
- Social media ads
- Content marketing tools
- Photography for business use

### Education and Training

- Courses related to your work
- Books and publications
- Conferences and workshops
- Certification programs

### Travel and Transportation

**Business Travel**:
- Flights and hotels for business trips
- 50% of meals during travel
- Transportation costs

**Local Transportation**:
- Mileage (67 cents per mile in 2026)
- Parking and tolls for business
- Public transit for business purposes

### Health Insurance

Self-employed individuals can deduct 100% of health insurance premiums for:
- Yourself
- Spouse
- Dependents

This is an "above the line" deduction—you get it even if you don't itemize.

### Retirement Contributions

**SEP-IRA**: Up to 25% of net earnings, max $69,000

**Solo 401(k)**: Up to $23,000 employee + 25% employer, max $69,000

**Traditional IRA**: Up to $7,000 ($8,000 if over 50)

## Tracking Income and Expenses

### Best Practices

1. **Separate bank account**: Keep business finances distinct
2. **Track everything**: Use apps like QuickBooks, FreshBooks, or Wave
3. **Save receipts**: Digital copies are fine
4. **Categorize as you go**: Don't wait until tax time
5. **Review monthly**: Catch errors and missing items

### What to Track

**Income**:
- Client payments
- Platform earnings (Upwork, Fiverr, etc.)
- Product sales
- Affiliate income
- Interest on business accounts

**Expenses by Category**:
- Office expenses
- Professional services
- Marketing
- Travel
- Meals (50% deductible)
- Education
- Insurance
- Subscriptions

## Common Freelancer Tax Mistakes

### 1. Not Saving for Taxes

**The Problem**: Spending all your income and facing a huge tax bill

**The Solution**: Set aside 25-30% of every payment in a separate savings account

### 2. Missing Quarterly Payments

**The Problem**: Penalties and a massive year-end payment

**The Solution**: Mark due dates on your calendar, set up automatic savings

### 3. Not Tracking Expenses

**The Problem**: Missing legitimate deductions, paying more than necessary

**The Solution**: Track expenses weekly, use accounting software

### 4. Mixing Personal and Business

**The Problem**: Audit risk, lost deductions, messy books

**The Solution**: Separate bank accounts and credit cards

### 5. Overlooking the Home Office Deduction

**The Problem**: Leaving money on the table

**The Solution**: Measure your workspace, calculate the deduction

### 6. Ignoring 1099s

**The Problem**: Underreporting income (the IRS has copies too)

**The Solution**: Report all income, even if you don't receive a 1099

## Business Structure Options

### Sole Proprietorship

- Default structure for freelancers
- Simple and no formation required
- Personal liability for business debts
- Self-employment tax on all profits

### LLC (Limited Liability Company)

- Liability protection
- Still taxed as sole proprietor by default
- Can elect S-corp taxation
- State filing fees apply

### S-Corporation

- Can reduce self-employment tax
- Must pay yourself "reasonable salary"
- More complex administration
- Best when earning $60K+ in profit

**Example**: $100K profit as sole proprietor = $15,300 SE tax

As S-corp with $60K salary: SE tax only on salary = $9,180

Savings: $6,120

## Tax Software vs. Accountant

### DIY with Software

Good for:
- Simple freelance income
- Few clients
- Straightforward deductions
- Comfortable with taxes

Popular options:
- TurboTax Self-Employed
- H&R Block Self-Employed
- TaxAct

### Hire an Accountant

Worth it when:
- Multiple income streams
- Business structure questions
- Audit concerns
- Want to maximize deductions
- Have complex situations

Typical cost: $300-1,000 for annual filing

## Year-End Tax Planning

### November-December Checklist

1. **Review estimated tax situation**: Do you need a Q4 catch-up payment?
2. **Accelerate expenses**: Buy needed equipment before year-end
3. **Defer income**: If possible, delay December invoices to January
4. **Max retirement contributions**: SEP-IRA can be funded until tax deadline
5. **Review health insurance**: Confirm premiums are deductible
6. **Organize records**: Start gathering documents now

### Record Keeping

Keep these documents for 7 years:
- Bank and credit card statements
- Receipts for all expenses
- 1099 forms received
- Mileage logs
- Home office measurements and calculations
- Contract and invoice copies

## Final Thoughts

Freelancer taxes are more complex than employee taxes, but they also offer more opportunities for deductions. The key is staying organized throughout the year rather than scrambling at tax time.

**Key takeaways**:
- Set aside 25-30% of income for taxes
- Pay quarterly to avoid penalties
- Track every business expense
- Keep business and personal finances separate
- Consider your business structure as you grow
- Get professional help if needed

With proper planning, you can minimize your tax burden and keep more of your hard-earned freelance income.
