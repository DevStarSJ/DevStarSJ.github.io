What if your home paid for itself?

That's the promise of house hacking — and it's not a fantasy. Thousands of people are doing it right now, living for free or nearly free while building equity and generating passive income.

It's one of the most powerful wealth-building strategies available to regular people, and it requires no special connections or large amounts of capital to start.

## What Is House Hacking?

House hacking is the strategy of **buying a property, living in part of it, and renting out the rest** to offset (or eliminate) your housing costs.

Classic house hacking scenarios:

- **Duplex/triplex/fourplex:** Buy a multi-unit building, live in one unit, rent the others
- **Single-family with extra rooms:** Rent out spare bedrooms to roommates
- **ADU (Accessory Dwelling Unit):** Build or rent out a garage apartment, basement apartment, or backyard cottage
- **Short-term rental:** Rent your home on Airbnb/VRBO when you travel, or rent a room short-term

## The Math That Makes It Work

Let's look at a real duplex example:

**Purchase price:** $350,000  
**Down payment (5% FHA loan):** $17,500  
**Monthly mortgage (PITI):** $2,200

**Your unit:** You live here — no rent to you  
**Tenant unit:** Rents for $1,400/month

**Your effective housing cost:** $2,200 − $1,400 = **$800/month**

Compare that to renting a similar apartment for $1,800. You're paying less *and* building equity *and* acquiring an asset that appreciates over time.

At scale, as rents rise with inflation and your mortgage stays fixed, that $800 gap could shrink to zero — or flip positive.

## How to Get Started

### Step 1: Choose the Right Property Type

**Duplexes and small multifamily (2–4 units)** are ideal for beginners. You can finance them with FHA loans (3.5% down), conventional loans (5–25% down), or VA loans (0% down for veterans). Banks treat them as residential — not commercial — which means better rates and easier qualification.

**Single-family with ADU** is another strong option, especially in suburban areas. An existing basement apartment or guest house can generate rent immediately.

### Step 2: Understand Owner-Occupant Financing Advantages

Because you're living in the property, you qualify for:
- **FHA loans** — As low as 3.5% down, even for multi-unit properties up to 4 units
- **VA loans** — Zero down for veterans on 1–4 unit properties
- **Conventional owner-occupant rates** — Typically 0.5–1% lower than investor rates

This financing advantage is why house hacking beats straight investment properties for wealth building when you're starting out.

### Step 3: Run the Numbers Before You Buy

The key metric: **Cash-on-cash return**

```
Annual net income ÷ Total cash invested = Cash-on-cash return
```

Also calculate:
- Gross rent multiplier
- Cap rate
- Expected appreciation

Use conservative assumptions: vacancy rate of 5–10%, maintenance at 1% of property value annually, and 10% property management (even if you self-manage, model it in).

### Step 4: Manage Your Tenants Well

Being a landlord while living next door is unique. Screen tenants carefully — run credit checks, verify income (aim for 3x rent), and check references. A good tenant relationship makes house hacking pleasant; a bad one makes it miserable.

## The House Hacking Wealth Path

Many successful real estate investors follow this path:

1. **Year 1–2:** Buy first house hack (duplex or rooms). Build equity, reduce housing costs.
2. **Year 3–5:** Move to second property, rent out first entirely (now a full cash-flowing rental).
3. **Repeat:** Each move adds a new income-producing asset.

After 10–15 years, you could own 3–5 properties, all financed with owner-occupant rates, generating thousands per month in net income.

## Tax Advantages

- **Depreciation:** Deduct the depreciation of the rental portion each year
- **Expenses:** Mortgage interest, property taxes, repairs, utilities (proportional to rental space) are deductible
- **Capital gains exclusion:** If you live there 2 of 5 years before selling, the gain on *your* unit may be excluded from capital gains tax

## Honest Challenges

**It's not passive initially.** Dealing with repairs, tenant calls, and vacancies takes time and effort — especially at first.

**You live with or next to your tenants.** Privacy trade-offs are real. Some people love the community; others find it stressful.

**Capital is still required.** Even with FHA at 3.5% down, on a $300,000 property that's $10,500 — plus closing costs.

**Regulations vary.** Some cities restrict short-term rentals or ADUs. Research local zoning laws first.

## Is House Hacking Right for You?

House hacking makes sense if you:
- Want to enter real estate investing with relatively low capital
- Are comfortable with the landlord role (at least short-term)
- Live in a market where multi-unit properties cash flow reasonably
- Are flexible about your living situation

It's not for everyone — but for those willing to try it, it's one of the fastest paths to financial independence available.

## Bottom Line

House hacking is the original life hack for real estate. It turns your biggest expense (housing) into an income-generating asset. Done right, you could live for free for years while building a portfolio that funds your retirement.

The best time to start is when you're young, flexible, and ready to be a little uncomfortable for a lot of long-term gain.
