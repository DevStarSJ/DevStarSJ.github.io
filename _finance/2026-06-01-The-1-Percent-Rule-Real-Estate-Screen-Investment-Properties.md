Real estate investing can seem complicated — cap rates, NOI, cash-on-cash returns, debt service coverage ratios. But before you crunch all those numbers, smart investors use one simple filter to eliminate 80% of bad deals in 30 seconds.

It's called **the 1% Rule**.

---

## What Is the 1% Rule?

The 1% Rule states:

> **Monthly rent should be at least 1% of the property's purchase price.**

If a property costs $200,000, it should rent for at least $2,000/month.

That's it. One simple calculation.

---

## Why Does the 1% Rule Work?

![Real estate investment property analysis](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1582407947304-fd86f28f3e47?w=1000&auto=format&fit=crop)

*Photo by Scott Graham on Unsplash*

The rule works because it gives a rough estimate of whether a property can generate positive cash flow after expenses.

**Rough expense breakdown for rental properties:**
- Mortgage (principal + interest): ~40-45%
- Property taxes: ~10-15%
- Insurance: ~5%
- Maintenance/repairs: ~10%
- Vacancy: ~5-8%
- Property management: ~8-10%

Total expenses typically run **70-80% of gross rents**. At the 1% rule, that still leaves 20-30% cash flow.

### Example:
- Purchase price: $200,000
- Down payment (25%): $50,000
- Monthly rent: $2,000
- Estimated monthly expenses: ~$1,400-1,600
- **Monthly cash flow: $400-600** ✅

## The 1% Rule in Action: Pass vs. Fail

| Property | Price | Rent | Ratio | Result |
|----------|-------|------|-------|--------|
| Condo A | $300,000 | $1,800 | 0.6% | ❌ Fail |
| House B | $150,000 | $1,600 | 1.07% | ✅ Pass |
| Duplex C | $250,000 | $2,800 | 1.12% | ✅ Pass |
| Townhouse D | $400,000 | $2,200 | 0.55% | ❌ Fail |

Properties that fail the 1% rule may still be good investments for appreciation, but they're unlikely to cash flow positively.

---

## When the 1% Rule Doesn't Apply

The 1% Rule is a screening tool, not a final decision. Here's when to adjust expectations:

### Markets Where 1% Is Nearly Impossible:
- **San Francisco, NYC, LA, Seattle:** Typical ratios are 0.3-0.5%
- These markets trade on appreciation, not cash flow
- Still can build wealth, but you're betting on price growth

### Markets Where 1%+ Is Achievable:
- **Midwest cities** (Cleveland, Detroit, Kansas City)
- **Southern cities** (Memphis, Birmingham, Little Rock)
- **Secondary markets** with strong rental demand

### When to Still Buy Below 1%:
- You expect significant appreciation
- The property is in a high-demand, low-supply area
- You're buying for equity, not cash flow
- You can add value (BRRRR strategy)

## Beyond the 1% Rule: Next Steps

Once a property passes the 1% screening, do deeper analysis:

**1. Calculate the Cap Rate**
Cap Rate = Net Operating Income / Purchase Price
A good cap rate is typically 5-10% depending on market.

**2. Calculate Cash-on-Cash Return**
Cash-on-Cash = Annual Cash Flow / Total Cash Invested
Target: 8-12%+ for most investors.

**3. Run a Full Cash Flow Analysis**
Account for every expense: mortgage, taxes, insurance, maintenance, vacancy, management, HOA if applicable.

**4. Research the Market**
- Population growth trends
- Employment diversity
- Rental vacancy rates
- Rent growth history

## The 0.7% Rule for High-Cost Markets

If you invest in expensive markets, some investors use a modified **0.7% Rule**:
- Monthly rent ≥ 0.7% of purchase price
- Combined with expected appreciation and equity build

This won't give you cash flow, but the overall return can still be attractive in appreciating markets.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The **1% Rule = monthly rent ÷ purchase price ≥ 1%**
- Use it as a **30-second screen** to eliminate bad deals quickly
- Properties passing the 1% rule are likely to **cash flow positively**
- It's a starting point — always do full analysis before buying
- In expensive markets, focus on appreciation or look in secondary markets
- **Cash flow is king** for long-term rental property sustainability

The 1% Rule won't make you a real estate expert, but it will save you hours of analysis on deals that were never going to work.
