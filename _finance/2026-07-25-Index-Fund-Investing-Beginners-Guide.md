Here's everything you need to know to get started.

## What Is an Index Fund?

An index fund is a type of investment fund that tracks a specific market index — like the **S&P 500** (top 500 US companies) or the **Total World Stock Market** — rather than trying to beat it.

Instead of a fund manager picking stocks and hoping to outperform, an index fund simply *owns all the stocks* in the index in proportion to their weight. When the market goes up, you go up. When it drops, you drop — but so does everyone else.

**Two main formats:**
- **Mutual funds** — priced once per day, can be set up for automatic investments
- **ETFs (Exchange-Traded Funds)** — trade throughout the day like stocks, lower minimums

## Why Index Funds Win

### The Math Problem with Active Management

Research consistently shows that **80–90% of actively managed funds underperform their index benchmark** over a 10–20 year period. Why?

1. **Fees**: Active funds charge 0.5–1.5% annually. Index funds charge 0.03–0.20%. That difference compounds enormously over decades.
2. **Trading costs**: Active managers trade frequently, incurring costs and tax liabilities.
3. **The efficiency of markets**: It's very hard to consistently pick stocks better than the collective wisdom of millions of investors.

> A 1% annual fee difference on $100,000 over 30 years at 7% returns = over $100,000 in lost wealth.

### Instant Diversification
One S&P 500 index fund gives you exposure to 500 companies across 11 sectors. If one company fails, it barely moves the needle.

### Tax Efficiency
Index funds have low turnover, which means fewer taxable events. ETF versions are especially tax-efficient.

## How to Build Your Portfolio

### The Simple 3-Fund Portfolio
The **Three-Fund Portfolio** — popularized by Bogleheads — covers the entire investable market:
1. **US Total Market** (e.g., VTI)
2. **International Total Market** (e.g., VXUS)
3. **US Bonds** (e.g., BND)

A simple allocation based on age: **Your age in bonds** (e.g., age 30 → 30% bonds, 70% stocks). Adjust based on risk tolerance.

### Even Simpler: One-Fund Portfolio
**Target-Date Funds** (e.g., Vanguard Target Retirement 2055) automatically shift from stocks toward bonds as you approach retirement. Zero maintenance required.

## Where to Buy Index Funds

All major US brokerages offer excellent options:
- **Fidelity** — No-minimum index funds, some with 0% expense ratio
- **Vanguard** — The OG of index investing; strong for mutual fund investors
- **Schwab** — Low minimums, strong ETF options
- **M1 Finance** — Automatic investing with pie-based portfolios

For tax-advantaged accounts: max your **401(k)** (up to $23,500 in 2025) and **Roth IRA** (up to $7,000) before taxable accounts.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Trying to time the market**: Research shows missing just the 10 best market days in a decade can cut your returns in half. Stay invested.

**Checking too often**: Seeing daily fluctuations triggers emotional decisions. Check quarterly at most.

**Overcomplicating**: 3–5 index funds is plenty. More funds ≠ better diversification.

**Stopping during downturns**: Bear markets are when you're buying shares at a discount. Keep investing consistently (dollar-cost averaging).

Time in market beats timing the market. Starting early matters more than investing more.

## Final Thoughts

Index fund investing isn't exciting — and that's the point. There's no hot tip to chase, no manager to trust, no news to obsess over. You buy the whole market, keep costs low, reinvest dividends, and wait.

It's boring. It works. And it's within reach of anyone with $1 and a brokerage account.

**The best investment strategy is the one you can stick with for decades. For most people, that's index funds.**
