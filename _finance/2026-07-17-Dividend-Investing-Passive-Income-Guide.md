## What Are Dividends?

When a profitable company decides to share some of its earnings with shareholders, that payment is called a **dividend**. Companies like Johnson & Johnson, Coca-Cola, and Realty Income have paid and *increased* their dividends for decades.

Key metrics to understand:

- **Dividend Yield**: Annual dividend ÷ Current stock price. A $100 stock paying $4/year = 4% yield
- **Dividend Per Share (DPS)**: The actual dollar amount paid per share you own
- **Payout Ratio**: Percentage of earnings paid as dividends. Under 60% is generally sustainable
- **Ex-Dividend Date**: You must own the stock *before* this date to receive the next dividend

## Types of Dividend Investments

### Individual Dividend Stocks

**Dividend Aristocrats**: S&P 500 companies that have increased dividends for 25+ consecutive years. Examples: Procter & Gamble (68 years), Coca-Cola (62 years), Johnson & Johnson (61 years).

**Dividend Kings**: Even more elite — 50+ consecutive years of increases.

**High-Yield Dividend Stocks**: Yields above 5%. Higher yield often signals higher risk — always investigate *why* the yield is high.

### Dividend ETFs

Perfect for beginners — instant diversification across dozens or hundreds of dividend payers:

| ETF | Yield (approx) | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| VYM (Vanguard High Dividend Yield) | ~3.0% | Large-cap high yield |
| SCHD (Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity) | ~3.5% | Quality dividend growth |
| DVY (iShares Select Dividend) | ~4.5% | Highest yielders |
| DGRO (iShares Dividend Growth) | ~2.5% | Growing dividends |

### REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts)

REITs are required by law to pay out 90% of taxable income as dividends. This creates reliably high yields (often 4–8%). Popular REITs: Realty Income (O), VICI Properties, Prologis.

## The Power of Dividend Growth

Here's what makes dividend investing extraordinary: **compounding + dividend reinvestment (DRIP)**.

If you invest $10,000 in a stock yielding 3% with 7% annual dividend growth:

| Year | Shares Value | Annual Dividend |
|---|---|---|
| Year 1 | $10,700 | $300 |
| Year 5 | $14,025 | $421 |
| Year 10 | $19,671 | $590 |
| Year 20 | $38,697 | $1,161 |
| Year 30 | $76,122 | $2,284 |

With dividend reinvestment (buying more shares with each dividend), those numbers accelerate dramatically.

## How to Build a Dividend Portfolio

### Step 1: Define Your Goal
- **Income now**: High-yield focus (REITs, utilities, energy)
- **Income later**: Dividend growth focus (Aristocrats, quality growth companies)
- **Both**: Barbell approach — 60% growth + 40% high yield

### Step 2: Evaluate Individual Stocks

Before buying any dividend stock, check:

✅ **Payout ratio under 70%** (under 50% is ideal)  
✅ **5+ years of consecutive dividend increases**  
✅ **Free cash flow > dividends paid**  
✅ **Debt-to-equity under 1.0** (for non-REITs)  
✅ **Business with durable competitive advantage (moat)**

### Step 3: Diversify Across Sectors

Don't concentrate in one sector. A balanced dividend portfolio might include:
- Consumer staples (Procter & Gamble, PepsiCo)
- Healthcare (Johnson & Johnson, AbbVie)
- Utilities (NextEra Energy, Realty Income)
- Technology (Microsoft, Apple — yes, they pay dividends)
- Energy (Chevron, ExxonMobil)
- Financials (JPMorgan, Aflac)

### Step 4: Reinvest Dividends Automatically

Enable DRIP (Dividend Reinvestment Program) on your broker. Every dividend payment automatically buys more shares — compounding your position without any action from you.

### Step 5: Monitor, Don't Obsess

Check your holdings quarterly, not daily. Watch for:
- Dividend cuts or suspensions
- Payout ratio creeping above 80%
- Declining free cash flow
- Major business model disruption

## Warning Signs: When to Sell a Dividend Stock

🚩 Dividend cut announced  
🚩 Payout ratio consistently above 90%  
🚩 Revenue/earnings declining for 3+ years  
🚩 Debt levels spiraling up  
🚩 "Yield trap" — stock dropped 50%, yield looks high but company is in trouble

## Common Dividend Investing Mistakes

**Chasing yield**: An 8% yield on a struggling company is worse than a 3% yield on a growing one. Calculate total return (price appreciation + dividends), not just yield.

**Ignoring taxes**: Dividends are taxable (qualified dividends taxed at capital gains rates; ordinary dividends at income tax rates). In taxable accounts, dividend-heavy portfolios can create tax drag. Consider holding high-dividend stocks in tax-advantaged accounts (IRA, 401k).

**Selling during downturns**: Dividend investors often earn more in downturns — lower prices mean you reinvest dividends into more shares at a discount. Hold steady.

**Forgetting inflation**: A 3% yield is meaningless if inflation is 5%. Focus on dividend growth companies that consistently raise payouts above inflation.

## Starting Small

You don't need $100,000 to start. With fractional shares available on most brokers:
- $50/month into SCHD = real diversified dividend exposure
- Increase contributions over time
- Reinvest every dividend

The best dividend portfolio is the one you start. Time is your most valuable asset in this strategy.
