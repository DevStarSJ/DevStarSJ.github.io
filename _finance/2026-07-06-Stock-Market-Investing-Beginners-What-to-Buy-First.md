# Stock Market Investing for Beginners: What to Buy First in 2026

Opening a brokerage account is easy. Knowing what to actually buy is where most beginners freeze up.

The internet is full of hot stock tips, meme stocks, and complex strategies. Most of it is noise. The good news: **the simplest approach is also one of the best-performing approaches over the long run.**

Here's exactly what to buy as a beginning investor in 2026.

## Start Here: Understand What You're Buying

When you buy a **stock**, you're buying a tiny ownership stake in a company. If the company does well, your stake grows. If it doesn't, it shrinks.

When you buy an **ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund)**, you're buying a basket of stocks in one transaction. An S&P 500 ETF, for example, gives you exposure to 500 of the largest US companies.

For beginners, **ETFs are almost always better than individual stocks.** Here's why:

1. **Instant diversification** — One ETF can hold hundreds of stocks
2. **Low cost** — Expense ratios under 0.10% are common
3. **No stock-picking required** — No need to analyze individual companies
4. **Historically strong returns** — The S&P 500 has averaged ~10% annually over decades

## The Core Portfolio: 3 ETFs That Cover Almost Everything

Most financial experts agree that the following three ETFs form a solid foundation:

### 1. VTI — Vanguard Total Stock Market ETF
- **What it holds:** Every publicly traded US company (~3,800 stocks)
- **Expense ratio:** 0.03%
- **Why it's great:** Broadest US market exposure at rock-bottom cost

### 2. VXUS — Vanguard Total International Stock ETF
- **What it holds:** ~8,500 stocks from developed and emerging markets outside the US
- **Expense ratio:** 0.07%
- **Why it's great:** US stocks are only about 60% of global market cap — international exposure matters

### 3. BND — Vanguard Total Bond Market ETF
- **What it holds:** US government and corporate bonds (~10,000 bonds)
- **Expense ratio:** 0.03%
- **Why it's great:** Reduces portfolio volatility; bonds zig when stocks zag

### Putting It Together

A simple allocation based on age:
- **In your 20s–30s:** 70% VTI / 20% VXUS / 10% BND
- **In your 40s:** 60% VTI / 20% VXUS / 20% BND
- **In your 50s:** 50% VTI / 15% VXUS / 35% BND
- **Near retirement:** 40% VTI / 10% VXUS / 50% BND

Younger investors can handle more volatility because they have time to recover.

## The Investment Process: Step by Step

### Step 1: Open and fund a brokerage account
Go to Fidelity.com or Schwab.com, open an individual brokerage account, link your bank, and deposit an initial amount.

### Step 2: Decide on account type
- **Taxable brokerage account:** No limits, no restrictions, but gains are taxed
- **Roth IRA:** Tax-free growth, $7,000/year limit — max this first if eligible
- **401(k)/employer plan:** Tax-advantaged, especially with employer match

**Order of operations:** Employer 401k match → Roth IRA → Additional taxable account

### Step 3: Buy your ETF
Search the ticker symbol (VTI, VOO, etc.), enter how many shares (or a dollar amount with fractional shares), and place a market order.

### Step 4: Set up automatic contributions
Log in monthly and buy more, or set up automatic investing if your broker offers it. The habit of investing regularly — regardless of market conditions — is called **dollar-cost averaging** and is one of the most effective strategies for ordinary investors.

## What NOT to Do as a Beginner

❌ **Don't try to time the market**
"Buy low, sell high" sounds obvious but is nearly impossible to execute consistently. Even professional fund managers fail at this. Stay invested.

❌ **Don't chase hot stocks or sectors**
Whatever was last year's winner (AI stocks, crypto, whatever) often underperforms the following year. Diversification beats concentration.

❌ **Don't check your portfolio every day**
Daily price fluctuations are noise. Looking constantly leads to emotional decisions. Check quarterly at most.

❌ **Don't invest money you need soon**
The stock market can drop 20–40% in a correction. Only invest money you don't need for at least 3–5 years.

❌ **Don't stop investing during downturns**
Market crashes feel terrifying. But they're also sales. Investors who kept buying during 2008 and 2020 did exceptionally well when markets recovered.

## Your Action Plan

1. **This week:** Open a Fidelity or Schwab account
2. **Next week:** Fund the account with whatever you can ($100, $500, $1,000)
3. **Next month:** Buy VTI or VOO — keep it simple
4. **Ongoing:** Set up automatic monthly contributions ($50–$500)
5. **Annually:** Review allocation, rebalance if needed (once a year is enough)

Investing doesn't need to be complicated. Buy diversified ETFs, invest regularly, leave it alone, let time do the work.
