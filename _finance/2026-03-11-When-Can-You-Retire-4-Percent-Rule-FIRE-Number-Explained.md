## What Is the 4% Rule?

The 4% rule comes from the **Trinity Study** (1994, updated multiple times), which analyzed historical stock and bond returns going back to 1926.

The conclusion: **If you withdraw 4% of your portfolio in year 1, then adjust for inflation each year, your portfolio will last 30+ years with 95%+ probability.**

**The magic formula:**

> **Retirement Number = Annual Expenses × 25**

That's it. If you need $50,000/year to live:
- $50,000 × 25 = **$1,250,000**

Reach $1.25 million, retire at 4% withdrawal — historically, you'd never run out of money.

---

## Why 25x? The Math Behind the Rule

If you have $1,250,000 and withdraw 4%:
- Year 1 withdrawal: $50,000
- Remaining: $1,200,000
- If portfolio earns 7–10% → grows to $1,284,000–$1,320,000
- Next year: withdraw $51,500 (inflation-adjusted), portfolio still grows

The portfolio keeps up with withdrawals because markets generally grow faster than your withdrawals.

## The Different Types of FIRE

The FIRE movement (Financial Independence, Retire Early) has several variations:

### LeanFIRE
**Target:** $750,000–$1,000,000  
**Annual spending:** $30,000–$40,000  
**Lifestyle:** Frugal, minimal, often low cost-of-living area

### RegularFIRE  
**Target:** $1,000,000–$2,000,000  
**Annual spending:** $40,000–$80,000  
**Lifestyle:** Comfortable middle-class

### FatFIRE
**Target:** $2,500,000+  
**Annual spending:** $100,000+  
**Lifestyle:** Luxurious, no compromises

### CoastFIRE
**Concept:** Invest aggressively early, then stop — let compound interest do the rest while you work a lower-stress job to cover current expenses

**Example:**
- At 30, you have $150,000 invested
- At 7% average return, $150,000 → $1,145,000 by age 60
- CoastFIRE: You've "coasted" — no more retirement saving needed
- Just work a job that covers your current bills

### BaristaFIRE
**Concept:** Have enough invested to almost retire, then work part-time (like a Starbucks barista) for employer health insurance + small income supplement

## How Long Until You Reach Your FIRE Number?

The most important variable is your **savings rate** — not your income:

| Savings Rate | Years to Retirement |
|-------------|-------------------|
| 10% | 51 years |
| 20% | 37 years |
| 30% | 28 years |
| 40% | 22 years |
| 50% | 17 years |
| 60% | 12.5 years |
| 70% | 8.5 years |
| 80% | 5.5 years |

*Assumes 5% real return (7% nominal minus 2% inflation) and current expenses*

**The shocking insight:** If you save 50% of your income, you can retire in 17 years regardless of your income level.

## The FIRE Investment Strategy

Your portfolio allocation matters enormously. The Trinity Study used a 60/40 (stocks/bonds) portfolio. Modern FIRE practitioners often use:

### Aggressive FIRE Portfolio (Under 50)
- 90% stocks / 10% bonds
- Focus: maximum growth
- Index ETFs: VTI (60%), VXUS (30%), BND (10%)

### Moderate FIRE Portfolio (50–60)
- 70% stocks / 30% bonds
- Balance: growth + stability
- Reduces sequence-of-returns risk as retirement nears

### Conservative FIRE Portfolio (60+)
- 50% stocks / 50% bonds
- Focus: preservation + income
- Standard retirement allocation

## The 4% Rule's Limitations (What Critics Say)

The 4% rule isn't perfect:

**Concern 1: 30-year rule, not 50-year**
The original study was for 30-year retirements. If you retire at 40, you need a 50-year horizon — some research suggests 3.5% or 3.3% is safer.

**Concern 2: High valuations may mean lower future returns**
The study used historical returns. If markets return 4–5% real instead of 7%, the math changes.

**Concern 3: Sequence of returns risk**
If markets crash 40% in year 1 of retirement, your portfolio may never fully recover. Solution: hold 1–2 years of expenses in cash/bonds as a buffer.

**Response from FIRE community:**
- Most retirees reduce spending in down years (flexible spending)
- Many do part-time work initially
- Social Security kicks in eventually
- Most portfolios in studies survived far longer than 30 years

**Practical adjustment:** Use 3.5% withdrawal rate for retirements over 40 years. Your FIRE number becomes 28.5x expenses instead of 25x.

## Sequence of Returns Risk: The Biggest Retirement Threat

The biggest threat to a FIRE portfolio isn't a bad average return — it's a bad return in the first 5 years.

**Example:** Two investors with $1,000,000 at 4% withdrawal ($40,000/year)

**Investor A (Lucky sequence):**
- Year 1: +25% return
- Year 2: +15% return
- Year 3-30: Average 7%
- Result: Portfolio at 30 years = $3.2M ✅

**Investor B (Unlucky sequence):**
- Year 1: -35% return (market crash)
- Year 2: -20% return
- Year 3-30: Average 7%
- Result: Portfolio exhausted at year 24 ❌

**Protection strategies:**
1. Hold 1–2 years of expenses in cash/money market
2. Reduce withdrawals 10–20% in years when portfolio drops 20%+
3. Do part-time work for first 3–5 years of retirement
4. Delay Social Security to maximize benefits

## Bottom Line

Your FIRE number = annual expenses × 25.

The path to reaching it = maximize savings rate + invest in low-cost index funds + time.

If you save 50% of your income today, you can retire in 17 years. If you save 30%, you can retire in 28 years.

The math is simple. The discipline is the hard part. But now you have the formula — use it.
