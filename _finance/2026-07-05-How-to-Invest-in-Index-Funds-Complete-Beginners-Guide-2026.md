## What Is an Index Fund?

An index fund is a type of investment that **tracks a market index** — like the S&P 500 — by holding the same stocks in the same proportions.

Instead of trying to beat the market, you simply *become* the market.

**Examples of popular indexes:**
- **S&P 500** — Top 500 US companies
- **Total Stock Market** — Nearly every US public company
- **MSCI World** — Global stocks across 23 developed countries
- **Bloomberg US Aggregate Bond** — US bond market

## Why Index Funds Beat Active Funds

The data is overwhelming:

- **Over 15 years, 92% of active fund managers underperform the S&P 500** (SPIVA report, 2025)
- Index funds have **lower fees** — typically 0.03%–0.20% vs. 0.5%–1.5% for active funds
- Less tax drag from lower turnover
- No risk of picking the wrong fund manager

**The math on fees:**
Invest $10,000 at 7% annual return over 30 years:
- 0.05% fee → **$74,872**
- 1.00% fee → **$57,435**

That 0.95% difference costs you **$17,437**. Fees matter enormously.

## Step 2: Pick the Right Account Type

Before choosing funds, choose your **account wrapper**:

- **401(k)** — Employer-sponsored, tax-deferred, often has matching
- **IRA (Roth or Traditional)** — Individual retirement account, tax advantages
- **Taxable brokerage** — No limits, no tax benefits, full flexibility

**Priority order:**
1. 401(k) up to employer match (free money!)
2. Max out Roth IRA ($7,000/year in 2026)
3. Max out 401(k) ($23,500/year)
4. Taxable brokerage

## Step 3: Choose Your Index Funds

### The Simple 3-Fund Portfolio

This covers the entire global market with just three funds:

| Fund | What It Covers | Example |
|---|---|---|
| US Total Market | All US stocks | FSKAX, VTI, SWTSX |
| International | Non-US stocks | FTIHX, VXUS, SWISX |
| US Bonds | Stability | FXNAX, BND, SWAGX |

### The Even Simpler 1-Fund Option

**Target Date Funds** do everything automatically:
- Pick your retirement year (e.g., FIPFX for 2050)
- They auto-adjust stocks/bonds as you age
- Set it and forget it

## Step 4: Set Your Asset Allocation

**Rule of thumb:** `110 - your age = % in stocks`

- Age 25 → 85% stocks, 15% bonds
- Age 40 → 70% stocks, 30% bonds
- Age 60 → 50% stocks, 50% bonds

*More aggressive investors go 100% stocks in their 20s and 30s — historically justified.*

---

## Step 5: Automate and Don't Touch It

The biggest mistake investors make is **reacting to market drops**.

**The right strategy:**
1. Set up automatic monthly contributions
2. Reinvest all dividends automatically
3. Rebalance once a year
4. **Don't check it every day**

## What About ETFs vs. Mutual Funds?

Index funds come in two flavors:

| | **ETF** | **Mutual Fund** |
|---|---|---|
| Trading | Like a stock (any time) | End of day only |
| Minimum | 1 share (~$50–$500) | Often $0–$3,000 |
| Tax efficiency | Slightly better | Slightly worse |
| Auto-invest | Harder | Easy |

**Both are great.** ETFs are more flexible; mutual funds are easier to automate. Pick what fits your workflow.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ **Waiting for the "right time" to invest** — Time in the market beats timing the market  
❌ **Buying too many funds** — 3 funds cover the world. You don't need 20.  
❌ **Ignoring fees** — Even 0.5% extra is a huge drag over decades  
❌ **Selling during crashes** — Market drops are buying opportunities, not exit signals  
❌ **Checking your portfolio daily** — It will make you anxious and irrational
