Owning rental property sounds great — until you're dealing with a broken water heater at 2 AM. **Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs)** let you earn passive income from real estate without ever being a landlord. Here's everything you need to know.

---

## What Is a REIT?

A REIT is a company that owns, operates, or finances income-producing real estate — think apartment complexes, shopping malls, office buildings, data centers, hospitals, and more.

By law, REITs must:
- **Distribute at least 90% of taxable income** as dividends to shareholders
- Have at least 75% of assets in real estate

This means REITs are **dividend machines** — they're legally required to share their profits with you.

## Types of REITs

### Equity REITs (Most Common)
Own and operate properties. Income comes from rent.

**Top examples:**
- **Prologis (PLD)** — Industrial/warehouses (Amazon, FedEx tenants)
- **American Tower (AMT)** — Cell towers, digital infrastructure
- **Realty Income (O)** — "The Monthly Dividend Company" — retail properties
- **Invitation Homes (INVH)** — Single-family rental homes

### Mortgage REITs (mREITs)
Invest in real estate mortgages rather than physical properties. Higher yields, higher risk.

**Top examples:**
- **AGNC Investment (AGNC)** — Agency mortgage-backed securities
- **Annaly Capital (NLY)** — Diversified mortgage REIT

### REIT ETFs (Best for Beginners)
Instead of picking individual REITs, buy a basket:
- **VNQ (Vanguard Real Estate ETF)** — 170+ REITs, 0.12% expense ratio
- **SCHH (Schwab US REIT ETF)** — Low-cost, diversified
- **O (Realty Income)** — A single REIT with consistent monthly dividends

## How REIT Dividends Work

REITs typically pay dividends **quarterly** (some monthly). Here's what that looks like with Realty Income (O):

- **Annual dividend yield:** ~5–6%
- **$10,000 invested** → ~$500–$600/year in passive income
- **$50,000 invested** → ~$2,500–$3,000/year
- **$200,000 invested** → ~$10,000–$12,000/year

This is **truly passive** — no maintenance calls, no tenant drama.

---

## Tax Considerations

REIT dividends are typically taxed as **ordinary income** (not at the lower qualified dividend rate). However:

- REITs qualify for the **20% pass-through deduction** (Section 199A) under U.S. tax law
- Holding REITs in a **Roth IRA or 401k** means tax-free or tax-deferred growth — optimal strategy

## How to Invest in REITs

### Option 1: REIT ETF (Recommended for Beginners)
1. Open a brokerage account (Fidelity, Schwab, Vanguard)
2. Search for **VNQ** or **SCHH**
3. Buy shares
4. Reinvest dividends automatically (DRIP)

### Option 2: Individual REITs
Research specific sectors you're bullish on:
- Bullish on e-commerce? → Prologis (industrial warehouses)
- Bullish on digital infrastructure? → American Tower or Crown Castle
- Want monthly income? → Realty Income

### Option 3: Non-Traded REITs / Crowdfunding
Platforms like **Fundrise** or **Arrived** let you invest in private real estate starting at $10–$100. Lower liquidity but potentially higher yields.

## Risks to Understand

- **Interest rate sensitivity:** REITs often fall when interest rates rise (investors move to bonds)
- **Sector concentration:** Know what your REIT owns — retail malls face different risks than data centers
- **Dividend cuts:** In recessions, some REITs reduce dividends; diversify across sectors
- **Tax drag:** In taxable accounts, dividends are taxed annually — prefer tax-advantaged accounts
