Most people assume retirement at 65 is the only option. The FIRE movement says otherwise.

FIRE — Financial Independence, Retire Early — is a growing community of people who save aggressively, invest wisely, and leave the workforce decades before the traditional retirement age.

Is it extreme? Yes. Is it for everyone? No. But the principles behind it are worth understanding — even if you just want more financial freedom, not total retirement.

---

## The Core Math of FIRE

FIRE is built on two key concepts:

### 1. The 4% Rule

Research shows that if you withdraw **4% of your portfolio each year**, it will last at least 30 years with very high probability.

This means:
- If you spend $40,000/year → You need **$1,000,000** ($40k / 0.04)
- If you spend $60,000/year → You need **$1,500,000**
- If you spend $80,000/year → You need **$2,000,000**

**Your FIRE Number = Annual Expenses × 25**

### 2. Your Savings Rate Determines Retirement Age

This is the most powerful insight in personal finance:

| Savings Rate | Years to Retire |
|---|---|
| 10% | 43 years |
| 20% | 37 years |
| 30% | 28 years |
| 40% | 22 years |
| 50% | 17 years |
| 60% | 12.5 years |
| 70% | 8.5 years |

*Assumes starting from $0, 5% real investment return*

The higher your savings rate, the **double benefit**: you save more AND you learn to live on less (lowering your FIRE number).

## Types of FIRE

Not all FIRE is the same:

### Lean FIRE
- Annual expenses: $25,000–$40,000
- FIRE number: $625,000–$1,000,000
- Lifestyle: frugal, minimalist, often geographic arbitrage (living in low-cost areas/countries)

### Regular FIRE
- Annual expenses: $50,000–$100,000
- FIRE number: $1,250,000–$2,500,000
- Lifestyle: comfortable middle-class, nothing extravagant

### Fat FIRE
- Annual expenses: $100,000+
- FIRE number: $2,500,000+
- Lifestyle: comfortable, travel, not much sacrifice

### Barista FIRE / Coast FIRE
- Work part-time to cover expenses
- Investments handle growth
- No need to draw down portfolio
- Great for people who like working but want freedom

## How People Actually Achieve FIRE

### Step 1: Know Your Numbers

1. Track current annual spending (last 12 months of expenses)
2. Calculate your FIRE number (expenses × 25)
3. Calculate your savings rate (savings / income × 100)

### Step 2: Increase Your Savings Rate

Both sides matter:

**Increase income:**
- Salary negotiations (the highest ROI skill in FIRE)
- Side income and freelancing
- Career advancement and skill building

**Reduce expenses:**
- Housing (the biggest lever — 30–40% of most budgets)
- Transportation (cars are wealth destroyers for many)
- Food (cooking at home vs. eating out)
- Subscriptions and recurring costs

### Step 3: Invest the Difference Efficiently

FIRE investors are usually boring investors:
- **Tax-advantaged accounts first** (401k match → Roth IRA → 401k → HSA)
- **Simple index funds** (total market + international + bonds)
- **No stock-picking, no timing the market**

### Step 4: Track Progress

Your FI number is the goal. Net worth statements monthly or quarterly. Celebrate milestones: 25%, 50%, 75%, 100%.

## The Reality Check: What FIRE Doesn't Tell You

### Healthcare Before 65 Is Expensive

In the US, employer health insurance stops when you retire. Private insurance at 40 can cost $400–$800/month per person. Budget for this.

**Solutions:** ACA marketplace subsidies, spouse's employer coverage, part-time work for benefits, health-sharing ministries.

### Sequence of Returns Risk

If the market crashes in your first 5 years of retirement and you're withdrawing, it can devastate a portfolio.

**Mitigation:** Keep 1–2 years of expenses in cash, flexible spending (reduce withdrawals in down years), some part-time income.

### Identity and Purpose

Many early retirees discover that work provided structure, identity, and community they didn't realize they valued.

**Plan for this:** FIRE works best when you're retiring *to* something — not just *from* work. What will you do with 50 free years?

## FIRE by the Numbers: A 35-Year-Old's Plan

**Starting point:**
- Age 35, income $100,000/year, current savings: $50,000
- Goal: Retire at 50 (15 years away)

**The math:**
- Target annual spending in retirement: $60,000
- FIRE number: $60,000 × 25 = **$1,500,000**
- Need to accumulate: $1,450,000 more

**The plan:**
- Savings rate: 50% ($50,000/year invested)
- Expected return: 7% annual
- Result: **$1,500,000 in ~14 years** ✅

Is it doable? With a six-figure income, yes — with significant lifestyle changes.

## Do You Need Full FIRE?

Even partial FIRE principles transform financial life:

- **Knowing your number** reduces money anxiety
- **Higher savings rate** accelerates any goal
- **Simple index investing** beats most professional strategies
- **Spending intentionally** on what matters = more happiness per dollar

You don't have to retire at 40. But having the *option* to? That's worth pursuing.
