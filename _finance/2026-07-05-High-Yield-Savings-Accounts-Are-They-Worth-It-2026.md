The average traditional bank savings account pays **0.06% APY** in 2026. Meanwhile, high-yield savings accounts (HYSAs) at online banks are paying **4.50–5.25% APY**.

On $10,000, that's $6 a year vs. $500 a year. The difference is absurd.

---

## What Is a High-Yield Savings Account?

A high-yield savings account (HYSA) is a savings account that pays significantly higher interest than traditional bank accounts. They're typically offered by **online banks** and **credit unions** because:

- They have lower overhead (no branches)
- They compete for deposits by offering better rates
- They're FDIC-insured just like traditional banks

**Key features:**
- FDIC insured up to $250,000 per depositor
- No investment risk (unlike stocks or bonds)
- Liquid — money accessible within 1–3 business days
- No lock-in period (unlike CDs)

## HYSA vs. Other Options

| Option | Rate | Risk | Liquidity |
|---|---|---|---|

| **HYSA** | 4.5–5.25% | None | 1–3 days |

| **I-Bonds** | ~4.5% | None | 12-month lock |

**For your emergency fund:** HYSA is the clear winner. No risk, good returns, accessible when you need it.

**For long-term investing:** Index funds still win hands-down over 5+ year horizons.

## How Much Should You Keep in a HYSA?

**Rule of thumb:**
- **Emergency fund:** 3–6 months of expenses
- **Short-term savings goals** (1–3 years): vacation, car, down payment
- **Anything you need within 2 years** and can't afford to lose

**What NOT to put there:**
- Long-term retirement funds (opportunity cost is too high)
- Money you won't need for 5+ years (stocks will beat it)

## Common Questions

**Will I owe taxes on the interest?**
Yes. Interest from savings accounts is taxable income. You'll receive a 1099-INT form if you earn more than $10.

**Is my money safe?**
Yes — up to $250,000 per depositor, per FDIC-insured bank. Online banks have the same protection as traditional banks.

**Can I have multiple HYSAs?**
Absolutely. Many people use different HYSAs for different goals (emergency fund, vacation fund, car fund).

**What's the catch?**
The main inconveniences: transfers take 1–3 days, you typically can't deposit cash, and there's no ATM card for most HYSAs.
