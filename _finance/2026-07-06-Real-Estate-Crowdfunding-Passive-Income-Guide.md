# Real Estate Crowdfunding: Passive Income Without Buying Property

Owning investment property sounds appealing — until you think about the down payment, maintenance calls at midnight, difficult tenants, and property taxes. For most people, direct real estate investment is out of reach.

**Real estate crowdfunding changes the equation.** You can now invest in commercial buildings, apartment complexes, and residential developments with as little as $10 — and collect passive income without ever being a landlord.

## How Real Estate Crowdfunding Works

Real estate crowdfunding platforms pool money from many investors to fund real estate projects. In return, investors receive:

- **Dividends** from rental income or interest payments
- **Appreciation** when properties are sold at a profit

Think of it like buying a small slice of a large property alongside thousands of other investors — except a professional team handles all the management.

### Two Main Structures

**Equity Crowdfunding**
You own a share of the property itself. You earn dividends from rental income and a portion of the profit when the property sells. Higher potential returns, but also more risk.

**Debt Crowdfunding**
You're essentially lending money to a real estate developer. You earn fixed interest payments (like a bond). Lower risk, more predictable returns, but no upside from appreciation.

## Major Platforms in 2026

### Fundrise
**Minimum:** $10 | **Type:** Equity + Debt | **Historical returns:** 7–12% annually

The most beginner-friendly platform. Invests in diversified portfolios of residential and commercial properties across the US. Available to all investors (not just accredited).

### RealtyMogul
**Minimum:** $5,000 | **Type:** Equity + Debt | **Target returns:** 6–12%

More institutional-grade deals. Access to office buildings, retail, and multifamily properties. Requires accredited investor status for some offerings.

### Crowdstreet
**Minimum:** $25,000 | **Type:** Equity | **Target returns:** 10–20%+

High-end commercial real estate for accredited investors only. Historically strong returns but requires significant capital and longer hold periods.

### Arrived
**Minimum:** $100 | **Type:** Equity | **Target returns:** 5–9%

Focuses specifically on single-family rental homes. Simpler mental model for most people — you own shares of an actual house that tenants rent.

## Pros and Cons

### ✅ Advantages

**Low barrier to entry**
Start with $10–$100 vs. $50,000–$200,000 for a down payment on actual property.

**True passive income**
No tenant calls, no maintenance coordination, no property management.

**Portfolio diversification**
Real estate has historically low correlation with stocks — it can stabilize a portfolio.

**Geographic diversification**
Invest in properties across the country (or world) without living there.

**Professional management**
Experienced teams handle acquisitions, operations, and dispositions.

### ❌ Disadvantages

**Illiquidity**
Most investments lock up your money for 3–7 years. Unlike stocks, you can't sell tomorrow.

**Platform risk**
If the platform goes under, recovering your investment can be complicated.

**Fees**
Management fees (typically 1–2.5% annually) reduce net returns.

**Less control**
You're trusting the platform's investment decisions. You can't influence which properties are chosen.

**Tax complexity**
Real estate investments generate K-1 forms and can complicate your tax return.

## Comparing to REITs

Real estate investment trusts (REITs) are the publicly-traded alternative. How do they compare?

| Feature | Crowdfunding | REITs (Public) |
|---------|------------|----------------|
| Minimum investment | $10–$25,000 | ~$10 (1 share) |
| Liquidity | Low (locked for years) | High (sell anytime) |
| Returns | 7–20%+ | 5–8% historical |
| Correlation with stocks | Low | Moderate-high |
| Tax forms | K-1 (complex) | 1099 (simple) |
| Management | Platform team | REIT management |

**Quick answer:** REITs for liquidity and simplicity. Crowdfunding for potentially higher returns and lower stock-market correlation.

## Who Is This For?

**Good fit if you:**
- Have $10,000+ you don't need for 3–5 years
- Want real estate exposure without being a landlord
- Are diversifying beyond stocks and bonds
- Can handle illiquidity

**Not a good fit if you:**
- Need the money within 1–2 years
- Are investing your emergency fund (never do this)
- Aren't comfortable with limited transparency
- Need simpler tax situations

## Getting Started

1. **Check your accreditation status** — Some platforms require accredited investor status ($200K+ income or $1M+ net worth). Fundrise and Arrived are open to everyone.

2. **Start small** — Begin with $500–$1,000 on a beginner-friendly platform to understand how it works before committing more.

3. **Read the offering documents** — Every deal comes with a Private Placement Memorandum (PPM). At minimum, understand the expected returns, fees, and hold period.

4. **Diversify across platforms** — Don't put all your crowdfunding allocation on one platform or one deal type.

5. **Plan for illiquidity** — Only invest money you genuinely won't need for the stated hold period.

---

Real estate crowdfunding isn't magic — the returns aren't guaranteed and the risks are real. But as one piece of a diversified passive income strategy, it can provide steady cash flow and exposure to an asset class that was previously inaccessible to most investors.
