What if your portfolio paid you every month — without selling a single share?

That's the promise of dividend investing. And unlike most "passive income" schemes, this one is real, proven, and accessible to anyone with a brokerage account.

---

## What Are Dividends?

A dividend is a cash payment a company makes to its shareholders — typically every quarter. It's your cut of the company's profits, just for owning shares.

**Example:**
- You own 100 shares of Johnson & Johnson (JNJ)
- JNJ pays $1.24 per share quarterly
- You receive **$124 every 3 months** without doing anything

Most companies that pay dividends are large, stable, profitable businesses: consumer staples, utilities, healthcare, financials.

## Key Dividend Metrics

Before buying any dividend stock, know these numbers:

### Dividend Yield
`Annual dividend / Stock price × 100`

- JNJ at $155 paying $4.96/year = **3.2% yield**
- Higher isn't always better — very high yields (>7%) can signal trouble

### Payout Ratio
`Annual dividends / Earnings per share × 100`

- Under 60% = healthy and sustainable
- Over 80% = risky, may be cut during downturns
- Over 100% = company is paying more than it earns — red flag 🚨

### Dividend Growth Rate
How fast has the dividend increased each year?

- 5–8% annual growth = excellent
- Dividend growing = company's profits growing

## Types of Dividend Investments

### Individual Dividend Stocks

**Dividend Aristocrats** — S&P 500 companies with 25+ consecutive years of dividend increases:
- **Coca-Cola (KO)** — 62 years of increases, 3.1% yield
- **Johnson & Johnson (JNJ)** — 62 years, 3.2% yield
- **Procter & Gamble (PG)** — 68 years, 2.4% yield
- **Realty Income (O)** — Monthly dividends, 5.4% yield

### Dividend ETFs

For instant diversification, one ETF holds hundreds of dividend payers:

| ETF | Focus | Yield | Expense Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| **VYM** | High dividend yield | 2.9% | 0.06% |
| **SCHD** | Quality dividend growth | 3.4% | 0.06% |
| **VIG** | Dividend growth (10+ yr) | 1.7% | 0.06% |
| **DGRO** | Dividend growth | 2.3% | 0.08% |
| **JEPI** | High income (covered calls) | 7.5% | 0.35% |

**Best starting point for most:** SCHD — high quality, growing dividends, rock-bottom fees.

### REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts)

REITs must distribute 90% of taxable income as dividends. They offer real estate exposure without buying property:

- **Realty Income (O)** — 5.4% yield, monthly payments
- **Vanguard Real Estate ETF (VNQ)** — 3.8% yield, diversified

## The Power of DRIP (Dividend Reinvestment)

DRIP = Dividend Reinvestment Plan — automatically reinvesting dividends to buy more shares.

**Example: $10,000 in SCHD at 3.4% yield, reinvesting dividends**

| Year | Shares | Annual Dividend |
|---|---|---|
| Year 1 | 100 | $340 |
| Year 5 | 118 | $402 |
| Year 10 | 142 | $483 |
| Year 20 | 205 | $697 |
| Year 30 | 296 | $1,006 |

*Assumes 3.4% yield and 6% annual stock appreciation*

From $10,000 to generating **$1,000/year** — without adding a single dollar.

## Building a Dividend Portfolio

### The Simple Core Strategy

1. **Core holding (50–60%):** Dividend ETF like SCHD or VYM
2. **Growth dividend stocks (20–30%):** Dividend Aristocrats you believe in
3. **High yield (10–20%):** REITs or JEPI for income boost

### Monthly Income Strategy

To receive dividends every month, combine stocks/ETFs with different payment schedules:

- **January, April, July, October:** SCHD
- **February, May, August, November:** VYM or individual stocks
- **March, June, September, December:** O (Realty Income — also pays monthly!)

## Dividend Investing Pitfalls

❌ **Chasing the highest yield** — 10%+ yields are often unsustainable or traps  
❌ **Ignoring dividend growth** — A 2% yield growing 8%/year beats a 5% yield growing 0%  
❌ **Holding in taxable accounts unnecessarily** — Dividends are taxed annually; hold in IRAs/401(k) when possible  
❌ **Overconcentrating in one sector** — Don't hold 10 utility stocks and call it diversification  
❌ **Forgetting total return** — A stock that pays 5% but drops 10% isn't a win

## Realistic Income Expectations

| Portfolio Size | At 3% Yield | At 4% Yield | At 5% Yield |
|---|---|---|---|
| $10,000 | $300/year | $400/year | $500/year |
| $50,000 | $1,500/year | $2,000/year | $2,500/year |
| $100,000 | $3,000/year | $4,000/year | $5,000/year |
| $500,000 | $15,000/year | $20,000/year | $25,000/year |

To generate **$1,000/month** from dividends alone at 4% yield, you'd need **$300,000 invested**.

This is why dividend investing works best as a long-term accumulation strategy — not a get-rich-quick scheme.
