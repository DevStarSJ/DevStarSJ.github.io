# The Infinite Buying Method: TQQQ & 3x Leveraged ETF Dollar-Cost Averaging Strategy

Most investors fear volatility. The Infinite Buying Method turns that fear into a systematic advantage — by buying more when prices drop, and locking in profits when targets are hit.

## What Is the Infinite Buying Method?

The **Infinite Buying Method** (무한매수법, *Muhanmaesupbeop* in Korean) is a disciplined, rules-based investment strategy developed by a Korean retail investor known by the pen name **"Laore"** (라오어). It combines **dollar-cost averaging (DCA)** with **3x leveraged ETFs** and a **profit-target exit mechanism**.

The name "Infinite Buying" reflects the core philosophy: **no matter how far prices fall, you keep buying**. You never give up, never stop accumulating — because lower prices simply mean you're building a larger position at better prices.

### Key Instruments

| ETF | Tracks | Leverage |
|-----|--------|----------|
| **TQQQ** | NASDAQ-100 | 3x |
| **UPRO** | S&P 500 | 3x |
| **SOXL** | Semiconductor sector | 3x |

These are ProShares/Direxion daily-reset leveraged ETFs, meaning they target **3× the daily return** of their underlying index.

## How the Strategy Works: Step by Step

The mechanics are deceptively simple:

### Step 1 — Divide Your Capital into 60 Equal Parts

Take your total allocated capital and split it into **60 equal portions**.

> **Example:** You allocate $60,000 → each daily purchase = **$1,000**

The 60-day division is the foundation. It ensures you spread risk across a meaningful time window — roughly 3 months of trading days.

### Step 2 — Buy 1/60th Every Trading Day

Each trading day, purchase exactly one portion (1/60th of your capital) regardless of price direction.

- Price went up 5% today? → Buy anyway.
- Price dropped 10% today? → Buy anyway.
- Market looks terrifying? → Buy anyway.

This mechanical discipline removes emotion from the equation.

### Step 3 — Sell Everything When Your Target Return Is Reached

When your **total position reaches +33% profit** (a common target), **sell your entire holding**.

Why +33%? Because 3x leverage theoretically needs a ~11% move in the underlying index to deliver +33%. That's achievable within a typical market cycle.

> Some practitioners use different targets (+20%, +50%) depending on risk tolerance.

### Step 4 — Reset and Repeat

After selling, you start fresh:
- Recount your remaining capital (original + profits)
- Divide into 60 new portions
- Begin daily purchases again

This sell-and-restart cycle compounds your gains over time while resetting your average cost basis with each round.

### Step 5 — Keep Buying Through Downturns

During bear markets or corrections, your average cost keeps falling as you buy at progressively lower prices. You're accumulating **more shares per dollar** during downturns — exactly when most investors panic-sell.

## Why 3x Leveraged ETFs?

### The Compounding Upside

During bull runs, 3x leverage delivers extraordinary returns:

- NASDAQ-100 gains 11% → TQQQ gains ~33%
- S&P 500 gains 10% → UPRO gains ~30%

With DCA, you're not betting everything at one price point. You're spreading risk, so when the recovery comes, even a partial rebound from your blended average cost can hit the profit target.

### Turning Volatility Into an Advantage

Normal DCA in regular ETFs is effective but slow. 3x leverage means:

1. **Faster profit target achievement** — smaller index moves trigger your exit
2. **Volatility works for you during accumulation** — price swings let you average down more aggressively
3. **Shorter holding periods** — less exposure to long-term decay risks

## The Critical Risks You Must Understand

### ⚠️ Volatility Decay (Beta Slippage)

3x leveraged ETFs suffer from a mathematical phenomenon called **volatility decay** (also called beta slippage):

- Day 1: Index +10% → TQQQ +30% (from $100 → $130)
- Day 2: Index −10% → TQQQ −30% (from $130 → $91)
- Net index change: −1%
- Net TQQQ change: **−9%**

In choppy, sideways markets, TQQQ can lose value even when the underlying index is flat. **This is why long-term buy-and-hold of TQQQ is dangerous.**

### ⚠️ Catastrophic Single-Day Drops

A single day drop of **−33% or more** would theoretically wipe out a 3x leveraged ETF. While circuit breakers prevent this in practice, extreme events (March 2020 COVID crash) saw TQQQ fall over 20% in a single session.

### ⚠️ Extended Bear Markets

In 2022, TQQQ fell **over 80%** from peak to trough as the NASDAQ entered a prolonged bear market driven by Fed rate hikes. Investors who continued buying throughout faced severe paper losses and required enormous subsequent gains to recover.

### ⚠️ The 60-Division Rule Is Non-Negotiable

Investing more than 1/60th on any single day — especially during "obvious" dips — undermines the strategy's risk distribution. The entire point is systematic, equal-portion buying.

## Historical Performance Context

### The COVID Recovery (2020–2021): Strategy at Its Best

The pandemic crash followed by massive monetary stimulus created ideal conditions:

- TQQQ fell ~70% from Feb–March 2020
- Then surged **~700%** from March 2020 lows to November 2021 highs
- Investors who kept buying through the crash and held to recovery saw **multi-bagger returns**

For Infinite Buying practitioners, the crash was a gift — cheaper shares accumulated daily, and the sharp recovery quickly hit profit targets multiple times.

### 2022 Bear Market: Strategy at Its Worst

2022 was the stress test:

- NASDAQ-100 fell ~33% for the year
- TQQQ fell **~80%** peak to trough
- Investors buying since early 2022 saw their daily purchases increasingly "underwater"
- Recovery required TQQQ to gain **+400%** just to return to 2021 highs

This exposed the strategy's core vulnerability: **a prolonged, deep bear market with no quick recovery can trap capital for years.**

## Practical Considerations

### Tax Implications
- Frequent buying and selling generates **short-term capital gains** (taxed as ordinary income in the US)
- Each "round" of buy-then-sell creates a taxable event
- Tax-advantaged accounts (IRA, 401k) are worth considering

### Currency Risk for International Investors
- TQQQ, UPRO, SOXL trade in USD
- Non-US investors face **USD/local currency exchange rate risk**
- A strong home currency eroding USD gains can significantly reduce real returns

### Brokerage Access
- Most major US brokerages (Fidelity, Schwab, TD Ameritrade) offer these ETFs
- International investors may need specific broker access
- Some platforms restrict leveraged ETF purchases without an acknowledgment of risk

## Is This Strategy Right for You?

The Infinite Buying Method is **not for everyone**. It suits investors who:

✅ Have strong emotional discipline to buy through severe downturns  
✅ Can afford to have capital "locked up" for extended periods  
✅ Understand and accept the risks of 3x leveraged products  
✅ Have a long enough time horizon to weather extended bear markets  
✅ Treat this as a **systematic, rules-based strategy** — not a get-rich-quick scheme  

It is **not suitable** for:

❌ Investors who cannot tolerate seeing -50% or worse on their position  
❌ Anyone who might need the invested capital in the near term  
❌ Investors who would deviate from the daily purchase discipline during fear

| Rule | Detail |

| Bear market behavior | Continue buying daily — never stop |

## The Bottom Line

The Infinite Buying Method is a sophisticated application of dollar-cost averaging specifically engineered for the volatility characteristics of 3x leveraged ETFs. It doesn't eliminate risk — it **systematizes how you respond to risk**.

The 2020–2021 period proved the strategy can generate extraordinary returns. The 2022 bear market proved it can also test your patience (and capital) to the extreme.

**If you follow it, follow it completely.** Half-measures — buying irregularly, holding through losses without a plan, skipping purchases during fear — destroy the mathematical edge the strategy depends on.
