If you're buried in debt, knowing **which debt to pay off first** can make a massive difference — both financially and psychologically. Two of the most popular strategies are the **Debt Snowball** and the **Debt Avalanche**. Let's break down both so you can choose the right approach for your situation.

## The Debt Snowball Method

Made famous by financial expert Dave Ramsey, the **Debt Snowball** method focuses on paying off your **smallest balances first**, regardless of interest rate.

### How It Works:
1. List all your debts from **smallest to largest balance**
2. Pay minimum payments on all debts
3. Throw every extra dollar at the **smallest debt**
4. Once that's paid off, roll that payment into the next smallest
5. Repeat until debt-free

### Example:
| Debt | Balance | Interest Rate | Minimum Payment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Credit Card A | $500 | 22% | $25 |
| Medical Bill | $1,200 | 0% | $50 |
| Car Loan | $8,000 | 6% | $200 |
| Student Loan | $15,000 | 5% | $150 |

With Snowball, you attack Credit Card A first (smallest balance), then the medical bill, then car loan, then student loan.

### Pros:
✅ Quick wins keep you **motivated**  
✅ Reduces number of debts faster  
✅ Psychologically powerful  
✅ Simple to follow  

### Cons:
❌ You may pay more interest overall  
❌ High-rate debt lingers longer  

## The Debt Avalanche Method

The **Debt Avalanche** is the mathematically optimal strategy. You prioritize debts with the **highest interest rates** first, saving the most money over time.

### How It Works:
1. List all your debts from **highest to lowest interest rate**
2. Pay minimum payments on all debts
3. Throw every extra dollar at the **highest-rate debt**
4. Once paid off, roll that payment into the next highest rate
5. Repeat until debt-free

### Using the Same Example:
With Avalanche, you attack Credit Card A first (22% rate — coincidentally also smallest), then medical bill, car loan, student loan.

But if the car loan were at 28%, you'd attack that first even though it has a larger balance.

### Pros:
✅ Minimizes total interest paid  
✅ Mathematically optimal  
✅ Saves the most money  

### Cons:
❌ Progress can feel slow (large high-rate debts take time)  
❌ Requires more discipline  
❌ Fewer "quick wins"  

## How Much Can You Save?

Let's say you have $500/month extra to put toward debt:

**Scenario: $5,000 at 22% + $20,000 at 6%**

| Strategy | Total Interest Paid | Time to Debt-Free |
|---|---|---|
| Snowball | $4,821 | 52 months |
| Avalanche | $4,190 | 50 months |

**Avalanche saves ~$631 and 2 months** in this scenario. The difference grows with larger, higher-rate debts.

## Which Strategy Should YOU Choose?

### Choose Debt Snowball if:
- You've **tried and failed** to pay off debt before
- You need **motivational wins** to stay on track
- Your debts have **similar interest rates**
- You're dealing with emotional stress around debt

### Choose Debt Avalanche if:
- You're **financially disciplined**
- Your debts have **significantly different interest rates**
- You're focused on **minimizing total cost**
- You don't need quick psychological wins

## The Hybrid Approach

Some people use a **hybrid strategy**: pay off 1–2 small debts first for quick wins (snowball), then switch to avalanche for the remaining high-rate debts. This combines motivation with math.

## The Most Important Rule

**The best debt payoff strategy is the one you'll actually stick with.**

Research shows that many people who try the avalanche method abandon it because they don't see progress fast enough. An imperfect strategy executed consistently beats a perfect strategy abandoned halfway through.

## Step-by-Step Action Plan

1. **List all debts** — balance, rate, minimum payment
2. **Choose your strategy** (snowball or avalanche)
3. **Build a $1,000 emergency fund first** (prevents new debt)
4. **Cut expenses** — find every extra dollar
5. **Attack your target debt** aggressively
6. **Celebrate milestones** — they matter for momentum
7. **Don't take on new debt** during the process

## Bottom Line

Both strategies work. Debt Avalanche saves more money; Debt Snowball keeps more people motivated. Analyze your debts, know yourself, and pick the strategy you'll follow through to the end. The finish line — being completely debt-free — is what matters.
