# Dividend Growth Investing: Build a Passive Income Stream That Grows Every Year

Imagine receiving a paycheck every month — without going to work. That's the promise of **dividend growth investing**, and for millions of investors, it's become a financial cornerstone.

Unlike speculative stocks that only pay off when you sell, dividend growth stocks put cash in your pocket regularly, *and* they increase that cash payout year after year.

## What Is Dividend Growth Investing?

Dividend growth investing (DGI) is a strategy focused on buying stocks from companies that:

1. **Pay dividends** — regular cash distributions to shareholders
2. **Grow those dividends** — consistently increasing the payout over time

A company that raises its dividend every year signals financial health, shareholder-friendly management, and sustainable earnings.

---

## Why Dividend Growth Beats Fixed Income

| | Dividend Growth Stocks | Bonds / CDs |
|---|---|---|
| Income growth | ✅ Increases over time | ❌ Fixed |
| Inflation protection | ✅ Built-in | ❌ Erodes |
| Capital appreciation | ✅ Possible | ❌ Minimal |
| Volatility | Moderate | Low |

A bond paying 4% today still pays 4% in 20 years. A dividend stock yielding 3% today that grows at 8% annually pays **over 14%** on your original investment after 20 years.

## The "Dividend Aristocrats": Companies Worth Knowing

**Dividend Aristocrats** are S&P 500 companies that have raised their dividends for **25+ consecutive years**.

Notable examples:
- **Johnson & Johnson** — 60+ years of consecutive increases
- **Procter & Gamble** — 60+ years
- **Coca-Cola** — 60+ years
- **Realty Income** — monthly dividends, 25+ years of increases

These companies survived recessions, wars, pandemics, and market crashes — and kept raising dividends through all of it.

## How to Start a Dividend Growth Portfolio

### Step 1: Define Your Income Goal

Decide how much annual dividend income you want. Example: $12,000/year = $1,000/month.

At a 3% average yield, you'd need ~$400,000 invested.
At a 4% average yield, you'd need ~$300,000 invested.

### Step 2: Choose Quality Over Yield

**High yield can be a trap.** A 10% yield often means the market expects a dividend cut. Focus on:
- Dividend yield: **2–5%** (sustainable range)
- Dividend growth rate: **5–10% annually**
- Payout ratio: **below 60%** (shows dividend is sustainable)

### Step 3: Diversify Across Sectors

Build income from multiple industries:

- **Consumer Staples**: P&G, Colgate-Palmolive
- **Healthcare**: Johnson & Johnson, AbbVie
- **Utilities**: NextEra Energy, Duke Energy
- **REITs**: Realty Income, VICI Properties
- **Financials**: JPMorgan Chase, BlackRock
- **Technology**: Microsoft, Apple (growing fast)

### Step 4: Reinvest Dividends Early On

Use **DRIP (Dividend Reinvestment Plans)** to automatically reinvest dividends. This compounds your share count and accelerates growth dramatically.

$10,000 invested in a 3% yield with 8% dividend growth:
- Without reinvestment: ~$24,000 in dividends after 20 years
- **With DRIP**: potentially **$60,000+** due to compounding

## Key Metrics to Evaluate Dividend Stocks

### 1. Dividend Yield
```
Annual Dividend Per Share ÷ Stock Price × 100
```
Target: 2–5% for sustainable income growth stocks.

### 2. Payout Ratio
```
Annual Dividend Per Share ÷ Earnings Per Share × 100
```
Target: Below 60% for most companies (REITs can be higher).

### 3. Dividend Growth Rate (DGR)
How much has the dividend increased annually over 5–10 years? Target: 5%+ consistently.

### 4. Years of Consecutive Increases
More years = more reliable. Dividend Aristocrats (25+ years) and Dividend Kings (50+ years) are the gold standard.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ **Chasing yield** — A 12% yield often precedes a dividend cut
❌ **Ignoring payout ratio** — Unsustainable dividends get cut in recessions
❌ **Over-concentration** — Don't put 40% in one sector
❌ **Selling during corrections** — Dividend stocks drop too; stay the course
❌ **Forgetting taxes** — Qualified dividends are taxed at lower rates; know your tax situation
