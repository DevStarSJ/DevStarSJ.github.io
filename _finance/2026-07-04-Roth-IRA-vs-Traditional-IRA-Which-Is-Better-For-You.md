Choosing between a Roth IRA and a Traditional IRA is one of the most important retirement decisions you'll ever make. Both offer powerful tax advantages — but they work in opposite ways. Pick the wrong one and you could leave thousands of dollars on the table.

Let's cut through the confusion.

---

## The Core Difference: When You Get Taxed

| | **Roth IRA** | **Traditional IRA** |
|---|---|---|
| Contributions | After-tax (no deduction) | Pre-tax (tax-deductible) |
| Growth | Tax-free | Tax-deferred |
| Withdrawals | Tax-free | Taxed as income |
| Required Minimum Distributions | None | Required at age 73 |

**In plain English:**
- **Roth IRA** = Pay taxes now, never again
- **Traditional IRA** = Skip taxes now, pay later

---

## 2026 Contribution Limits

- **Under 50:** $7,000/year
- **Age 50+:** $8,000/year (catch-up contribution)

These limits are the same for both account types. However, your ability to *deduct* Traditional IRA contributions or *contribute* to a Roth IRA depends on your income.

### Roth IRA Income Limits (2026)
- **Single filers:** Phase out $150,000–$165,000
- **Married filing jointly:** Phase out $236,000–$246,000

If you earn above these thresholds, you cannot contribute directly to a Roth IRA (though the "backdoor Roth" strategy may help).

## When Roth IRA Wins

**Choose Roth if you:**
1. **Are in a low tax bracket now** — pay low taxes today, withdraw tax-free in retirement when you might be in a higher bracket
2. **Are young** — more decades of tax-free compound growth
3. **Expect higher income in retirement** — Social Security, pension, or other income could push you into a high bracket
4. **Want flexibility** — Roth contributions (not earnings) can be withdrawn anytime penalty-free
5. **Want to leave money to heirs** — no RMDs means your account can keep growing

---

## When Traditional IRA Wins

**Choose Traditional if you:**
1. **Are in a high tax bracket now** — the deduction saves you more today than the future tax cost
2. **Expect lower income in retirement** — you'll pay less tax when you withdraw
3. **Need to reduce taxable income this year** — deductible contributions lower your AGI
4. **Are over 50 and behind on savings** — the immediate tax break makes it easier to save more

---

## The Tax Bracket Math

Let's say you're deciding between Roth and Traditional, putting in $7,000:

**Scenario: Currently in 22% bracket, expect 12% bracket in retirement**
- Traditional: Save $1,540 in taxes now, pay $840 later → **Net win: $700 for Traditional**

**Scenario: Currently in 12% bracket, expect 22% bracket in retirement**
- Roth: Pay $840 now, save $1,540 later → **Net win: $700 for Roth**

The math favors Roth when your future tax rate will be *higher* than your current rate.

---

## Can You Have Both?

Yes! Many investors contribute to both in the same year, splitting the $7,000 limit between accounts. This "tax diversification" strategy hedges against future tax law changes.

Common split:
- Maximize 401(k) first (if employer match available)
- Then split IRA contributions between Roth and Traditional

---

## Key Withdrawal Rules

### Roth IRA
- Contributions: Withdraw anytime, no penalty, no tax
- Earnings: Penalty-free after age 59½ AND account is 5+ years old
- No RMDs during your lifetime

### Traditional IRA
- Withdrawals: Taxed as ordinary income
- Early withdrawal (before 59½): 10% penalty + income tax
- RMDs: Must start at age 73

---

## The Backdoor Roth Strategy

If your income exceeds the Roth IRA limits, you can still access Roth benefits:

1. Contribute $7,000 to a Traditional IRA (non-deductible)
2. Convert it to a Roth IRA immediately
3. Pay taxes only on any growth (usually minimal if done quickly)

This legal workaround lets high earners enjoy tax-free growth.

---

## Bottom Line

**Default recommendation for most people:**
- **Under 30 / early career:** Roth IRA — you're likely in a low bracket and have decades of tax-free growth ahead
- **Peak earning years / high income:** Traditional IRA or 401(k) — the deduction saves more now
- **Unsure?** Split contributions for tax diversification

The best IRA is the one you actually fund. Start with whichever one removes friction and gets money in the account.
