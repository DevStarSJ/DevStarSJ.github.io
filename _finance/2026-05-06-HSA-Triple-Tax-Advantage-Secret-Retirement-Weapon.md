Most people know about 401(k)s and IRAs. Far fewer know about the account that arguably beats them both on tax efficiency. The **Health Savings Account (HSA)** is arguably the most powerful tax-advantaged vehicle available to American workers — and most people are using it wrong, or not using it at all.

## What Is an HSA?

An HSA is a savings account for medical expenses — but with a unique triple tax advantage:

1. **Contributions are tax-deductible** (reduces taxable income)
2. **Growth is tax-free** (investments inside the HSA grow without tax)
3. **Withdrawals are tax-free** (when used for qualified medical expenses)

No other account in the US tax code offers all three. Not a 401(k), not a Roth IRA. HSAs are uniquely powerful.

## Who Can Contribute?

To open and fund an HSA, you must be enrolled in a **High-Deductible Health Plan (HDHP)**:
- 2025 minimum deductibles: $1,650 (individual) / $3,300 (family)
- 2025 out-of-pocket maximums: $8,300 (individual) / $16,600 (family)

**2025 HSA Contribution Limits:**
- Individual: $4,300
- Family: $8,550
- Age 55+ catch-up: additional $1,000

You cannot contribute if you're enrolled in Medicare, claimed as a dependent, or have other non-HDHP coverage.

## The Standard Use (And Why Most People Stop Here)

The typical HSA strategy: contribute pre-tax dollars, use them to pay current medical bills tax-free. This is fine. It's saving money on healthcare. But it leaves the most powerful feature on the table.

## The Power Move: Invest Your HSA

Here's what most people don't know: **most HSA providers let you invest your balance in mutual funds and ETFs**, just like a brokerage account. And that investment growth is completely tax-free.

This transforms your HSA from a medical expense account into a **tax-sheltered investment account**.

### The Strategy: Pay Out of Pocket Now, Withdraw Later

1. **Contribute the maximum to your HSA every year**
2. **Invest it in index funds** (not just leaving it in a savings account)
3. **Pay current medical expenses out of pocket** (use your regular checking account)
4. **Save every medical receipt** — there's no time limit for reimbursement
5. **Let the HSA grow tax-free for decades**
6. **At any point** (even 30 years from now), submit your saved receipts and withdraw the equivalent amount tax-free

There's no deadline on reimbursements. A $200 doctor's bill from 2026 can justify a $200 withdrawal in 2056 — and the difference in growth between $200 invested at 10% for 30 years is $3,490 in tax-free money.

## HSA as Retirement Account

At age 65, HSAs become functionally equivalent to a Traditional IRA:
- Withdrawals for **any reason** (not just medical) are subject to ordinary income tax only
- No penalty for non-medical withdrawals (unlike before 65, which has a 20% penalty)

But for medical expenses in retirement — which are substantial — HSA withdrawals remain **completely tax-free**. The average retired couple spends over $300,000 on healthcare in retirement. Having a tax-free source for that is enormous.

### The Priority Order (Maximizing Retirement Accounts)

For many people, the optimal contribution order is:
1. **401(k) to get employer match** (free money, always first)
2. **Max HSA** ($4,300 individual / $8,550 family)
3. **Max Roth IRA** ($7,000 / $8,000 if 50+)
4. **Return to 401(k)** to reach the $23,500 limit
5. **Taxable brokerage** if more to invest

## HSA vs. FSA — Don't Confuse Them

| Feature | HSA | FSA |
|---------|-----|-----|
| Requires HDHP | Yes | No |
| Rolls over | Yes, indefinitely | Use-it-or-lose-it (mostly) |
| Investment options | Yes | No |
| Portable | Yes (your account) | Employer-tied |
| 2025 max contribution | $4,300 individual | $3,300 |

FSAs are useful but cannot be used as investment vehicles. HSAs are the superior long-term tool.

## Choosing an HSA Provider

If your employer offers an HSA, you may be stuck with their provider for contribution purposes — but you can transfer funds annually to a better provider.

**Best HSA providers for investing:**
- **Fidelity HSA** — No fees, excellent investment options
- **Lively** — Clean UI, free, integrates with TD Ameritrade
- **HSA Bank** — Widely available, good investment options

Look for: no monthly fees, no minimum balance to invest, access to low-cost index funds.

## Practical Steps to Start

1. Verify you're on an HDHP
2. Open an HSA (with Fidelity if your employer doesn't offer one, or transfer funds there)
3. Set up automatic contributions up to the annual maximum
4. Move contributions above the cash minimum into index funds
5. Create a folder (physical or digital) to store all medical receipts
6. Pay medical expenses from your checking account
7. Watch your HSA grow

## Bottom Line

The HSA is the most tax-efficient account available to Americans who qualify for it. A 30-year-old who maxes their HSA every year and invests the balance could accumulate over $500,000 by retirement — entirely tax-free for medical expenses that will definitely happen. Most people use it as a glorified debit card for copays. Don't be most people.

If you have an HDHP and you're not maximizing your HSA, you're leaving one of the best tax deals in the US tax code on the table.
