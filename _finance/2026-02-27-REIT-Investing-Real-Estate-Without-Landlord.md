# REIT Investing: Own Real Estate and Earn Passive Income Without Being a Landlord

Real estate has made more millionaires than almost any other asset class. But traditional real estate investing requires:
- Hundreds of thousands of dollars for a down payment
- A mortgage and debt
- Finding and managing tenants
- 2 AM maintenance calls
- Property taxes, insurance, repairs

What if you could own a piece of the Marriott in Times Square, a portfolio of Amazon fulfillment centers, and a collection of cell towers — all for $500 — and collect rent checks every month?

That's exactly what REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts) offer.

## What Is a REIT?

A Real Estate Investment Trust (REIT) is a company that **owns, operates, or finances income-producing real estate**. By law, REITs must:

1. **Invest at least 75% of total assets in real estate**
2. **Distribute at least 90% of taxable income as dividends** to shareholders
3. Have at least 100 shareholders
4. Be structured as a corporation

That 90% distribution requirement is why REITs typically offer **dividend yields of 3-8%** — much higher than typical stocks. They're required to pass most profits directly to investors.

In exchange for this pass-through structure, REITs pay no corporate income tax — meaning more of each dollar earned reaches shareholders.

## Types of REITs

Not all REITs are the same. Understanding the categories helps you diversify intelligently.

### Equity REITs (Most Common)
Own and operate physical properties, collecting rent:
- **Residential:** Apartments, single-family rentals (AvalonBay, Equity Residential)
- **Retail:** Shopping malls, strip centers (Simon Property Group, Regency Centers)
- **Office:** Office buildings and complexes
- **Industrial:** Warehouses, fulfillment centers, logistics (Prologis, STAG Industrial)
- **Healthcare:** Hospitals, senior housing, medical offices (Welltower, Healthpeak)
- **Hotel/Hospitality:** Hotels and resorts (Host Hotels & Resorts)
- **Data Centers:** Server facilities (Equinix, Digital Realty)
- **Cell Towers:** Communications infrastructure (American Tower, Crown Castle)
- **Specialty:** Casinos, prisons, billboards, farmland (VICI Properties, Farmland Partners)

### Mortgage REITs (mREITs)
Don't own properties — they finance real estate through mortgages and mortgage-backed securities. Higher yields but significantly higher risk. Interest rate sensitivity is extreme.

Examples: AGNC Investment, Annaly Capital Management

**Warning:** mREITs often look attractive with 10-15% yields but have a history of dividend cuts and price volatility. Approach with caution.

### Hybrid REITs
Combine equity and mortgage REIT strategies. Less common.

### REIT ETFs
Funds holding multiple REITs for instant diversification. Often the best approach for most investors.

## Why REITs Belong in Your Portfolio

### 1. High Current Income
The mandated 90% distribution means REITs typically yield 3-8% — far above the S&P 500 average of ~1.5%. For income investors, this is significant.

### 2. Inflation Hedge
Real estate rents and property values historically rise with inflation. Many REIT leases include annual rent escalators. During inflationary periods, real estate tends to outperform.

### 3. Portfolio Diversification
REITs have a relatively low correlation to stocks and bonds. Adding 10-20% REIT exposure to a traditional stock/bond portfolio historically improves risk-adjusted returns.

### 4. Liquidity
Unlike physical real estate (which can take months to sell), publicly traded REITs can be bought and sold in seconds on a stock exchange.

### 5. Professional Management
No property management headaches. Professional teams handle acquisitions, management, and capital allocation. You just collect dividends.

### 6. Accessible Entry Point
You can buy one share of many REITs for $20-100. Compare that to a 20% down payment on an investment property.

## Standout REITs Worth Knowing

### Realty Income Corporation (O)
- **Sector:** Net lease retail
- **Yield:** ~5-6%
- **Track record:** Monthly dividends paid since 1969, 125+ consecutive quarterly increases
- Known as "The Monthly Dividend Company"
- Tenants include Walgreens, Dollar General, 7-Eleven

### Prologis (PLD)
- **Sector:** Industrial/logistics warehouses
- **Yield:** ~2.5-3.5%
- **Why notable:** World's largest industrial REIT, Amazon's biggest landlord
- Beneficiary of e-commerce growth; demand for warehouse space remains robust

### VICI Properties (VICI)
- **Sector:** Gaming/experiential
- **Yield:** ~5-6%
- **Why notable:** Owns physical property of MGM Grand, Caesars Palace, Venetian
- 100% of rent is net lease — tenants pay taxes, insurance, maintenance
- Has never missed or cut its dividend since going public

- **Sector:** Senior housing and healthcare facilities
- **Yield:** ~2-3%
- **Why notable:** Strong demographic tailwind from aging Baby Boomers
- Owns senior housing, post-acute care facilities, outpatient medical

### American Tower (AMT)
- **Sector:** Cell towers
- **Yield:** ~3-4%
- **Why notable:** 220,000+ communication towers globally
- Essential infrastructure; 5G rollout driving long-term demand
- Internationally diversified revenue

### STAG Industrial (STAG)
- **Sector:** Industrial
- **Yield:** ~3.5-4%
- **Why notable:** Monthly dividend payer, single-tenant industrial properties
- More concentrated than Prologis but solid track record

## How Much REIT Exposure Should You Have?

Most portfolio allocation frameworks suggest **5-15% in REITs** for diversification benefit:

- **Conservative/income focus:** 15-20% REITs
- **Balanced portfolio:** 10-15% REITs
- **Growth focus:** 5-10% REITs

More than 20-25% REIT concentration can increase portfolio volatility due to interest rate sensitivity.

## The Tax Consideration

REIT dividends are mostly classified as **ordinary income** (not qualified dividends), meaning they're taxed at your regular income tax rate — not the lower 15-20% capital gains rate.

**Practical implication:** Hold REITs in **tax-advantaged accounts** (IRA, 401k) whenever possible to defer or eliminate tax on dividends.

- Roth IRA: Dividend growth is completely tax-free forever
- Traditional IRA/401k: Tax is deferred until retirement withdrawal
- Taxable brokerage: Dividends taxed as ordinary income annually

The difference between holding REITs in a taxable vs. Roth IRA can add up to hundreds of thousands of dollars over 30 years.

## REIT Metrics to Understand

### FFO (Funds From Operations)
The key earnings metric for REITs. Standard EPS is misleading because REITs depreciate buildings that are actually appreciating in value.

**FFO = Net Income + Depreciation – Property Sales Gains**

This gives a truer picture of operating cash flow.

### AFFO (Adjusted Funds From Operations)
FFO minus maintenance capital expenditures. Even more accurate picture of distributable cash flow.

**Payout ratio for REITs:** Look at dividends as a % of AFFO. Under 80% is generally sustainable.

### NAV (Net Asset Value)
The estimated value of a REIT's properties minus its debts. Comparing market price to NAV shows if a REIT trades at a premium or discount to its underlying assets.

### Debt Metrics
REITs use significant leverage. Watch:
- **Debt-to-EBITDA:** Under 6x is generally conservative
- **Fixed charge coverage ratio:** Higher is safer
- **Debt maturity schedule:** REITs with near-term debt maturities face refinancing risk

## Risks to Understand

### Interest Rate Risk
When interest rates rise, REIT prices typically fall — both because the cost of financing increases and because bonds become more attractive alternatives. The 2022-2023 rate hike cycle hit REITs hard.

However: long-term, quality REITs have recovered from rate cycles and delivered strong total returns. Don't abandon REITs at the first rate increase.

### Sector-Specific Risk
- Retail REITs face e-commerce headwinds
- Office REITs face work-from-home structural changes
- Hotel REITs are highly cyclical

Diversify across sectors to mitigate this.

### Dividend Cuts
Unlike traditional stocks, a REIT cutting its dividend is a serious red flag — it often signals fundamental problems. Screen for conservative payout ratios and strong balance sheets.

### Liquidity (Private REITs)
Publicly traded REITs are highly liquid. Non-traded and private REITs often lock up your capital for years. Stick to publicly traded REITs unless you're a sophisticated investor.

## Getting Started

1. Open a brokerage account (if you don't have one)
2. Start with VNQ for instant diversification
3. As portfolio grows, add individual REITs in sectors you understand
4. Hold REITs in tax-advantaged accounts when possible
5. Reinvest dividends until you need the income

## Bottom Line

REITs democratize real estate investing. The same wealth-building properties of real estate — income, inflation protection, appreciation — without the capital requirements, illiquidity, or management headaches.

For most investors, a 10-15% REIT allocation through diversified ETFs like VNQ, supplemented by a few individual REITs for higher income, represents an excellent building block for a complete investment portfolio.

Real estate has built generational wealth for centuries. With REITs, you don't need to be a landlord to participate.
