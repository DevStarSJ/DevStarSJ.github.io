## What Is Real Estate Crowdfunding?

Real estate crowdfunding pools money from many investors to fund real estate projects—residential, commercial, industrial, or mixed-use. Investors receive returns in the form of:

- **Dividends** from rental income
- **Appreciation** when properties are sold
- **Interest payments** on debt-based investments

Instead of buying a whole property, you buy a slice—sometimes as small as $10–$500—of many properties across different markets.

## Types of Real Estate Crowdfunding

### 1. Equity Crowdfunding
You own a share of the actual property. Returns come from:
- Rental income distributions (quarterly or monthly)
- Capital gains when the property sells (typically 3–7 year hold)

**Risk/Return:** Higher potential returns (8–15%+), higher risk

### 2. Debt Crowdfunding
You lend money to real estate developers. Returns come from:
- Fixed interest payments
- Short-term loans (6–24 months)

**Risk/Return:** Lower risk, predictable returns (6–12%)

### 3. REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts)
Publicly traded funds that hold real estate assets. Available on any brokerage. By law, must distribute 90%+ of taxable income to shareholders.

**Risk/Return:** Most liquid option, lower minimum, 3–8% dividend yields

## Risks to Understand

### Illiquidity
Most crowdfunding investments lock up your money for **3–7 years**. This is not emergency-fund money. Only invest what you won't need.

### Platform Risk
If the platform goes bankrupt, you may face complications recovering your investment. Stick to established platforms with track records.

### Real Estate Market Risk
Property values fluctuate. Commercial real estate (office, retail) faces structural headwinds. Research specific deal types.

### Developer Risk (Debt Deals)
If the developer defaults, recovery can be complex and slow, even with collateral.

## Tax Considerations

Real estate crowdfunding generates:
- **Ordinary income** (rental distributions) — taxed at your marginal rate
- **Capital gains** (property sales) — taxed at 0%, 15%, or 20% depending on holding period
- **Depreciation benefits** — some platforms pass through depreciation deductions

Keep good records. Most platforms provide 1099 forms but consult a tax professional for complex portfolios.
