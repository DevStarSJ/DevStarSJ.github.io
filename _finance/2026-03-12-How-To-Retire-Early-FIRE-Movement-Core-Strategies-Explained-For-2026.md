## What Is FIRE?

FIRE stands for **Financial Independence, Retire Early**. The core idea: accumulate enough invested assets that you can live off investment returns indefinitely — without needing to work.

Financial independence doesn't necessarily mean you stop working forever. Many FIRE achievers continue to work — but on their own terms, doing work they choose, not work they're forced to do.

## The 4% Rule: The Math Behind FIRE

The foundation of FIRE planning is the **4% Rule**, derived from the famous Trinity Study. It states:

> **If you withdraw 4% of your portfolio in year 1 and adjust for inflation each year, your portfolio has historically lasted 30+ years.**

### Calculating Your FIRE Number

**Annual expenses × 25 = FIRE Number**

| Annual Expenses | FIRE Number |
|---|---|
| $25,000/year | $625,000 |
| $40,000/year | $1,000,000 |
| $60,000/year | $1,500,000 |
| $80,000/year | $2,000,000 |
| $100,000/year | $2,500,000 |

The path to FIRE, therefore, has two levers: **reduce annual expenses** and/or **increase the portfolio size**.

## The Savings Rate: The Single Most Important Variable

Your savings rate determines how fast you reach FIRE more than almost anything else. Here's why:

| Savings Rate | Years to FIRE (assuming 7% real return) |
|---|---|
| 10% | ~43 years |
| 25% | ~32 years |
| 50% | ~17 years |
| 65% | ~11 years |
| 75% | ~8 years |

A 50% savings rate doesn't require an enormous income — it requires intentional spending. A person earning $60,000 and saving $30,000/year is building wealth faster than someone earning $150,000 and spending $145,000.

## The FIRE Flavors: Which Type Fits You?

The FIRE movement has evolved into several distinct approaches:

### Lean FIRE
- Live extremely frugally, both before and after retirement
- FIRE number typically under $750,000
- Annual spending: $20,000–$30,000
- Often involves geographic arbitrage (retiring in a lower-cost country)

### Fat FIRE
- Maintain a comfortable to luxurious lifestyle in retirement
- FIRE number: $2.5M–$5M+
- Annual spending: $100,000+
- Requires higher income or longer accumulation period

### Barista FIRE
- Semi-retire — reach partial financial independence
- Continue part-time work for health insurance and supplemental income
- Lower FIRE number needed (~$600K–$800K)
- Hugely popular because it's achievable much faster than full FIRE

### Coast FIRE
- Invest enough early that compound growth will reach your full FIRE number by traditional retirement age
- Once "Coast FIRE" is reached, you only need to earn enough to cover current expenses
- The investment portfolio "coasts" to full FIRE without additional contributions

## The FIRE Investment Strategy

Most FIRE practitioners use a simple, low-cost index fund approach:

**Standard FIRE portfolio:**
- 80–100% stocks (mostly index funds: VTI, VXUS, or equivalent)
- 0–20% bonds (increases toward retirement date)
- Emergency fund of 6–12 months expenses in cash

**The logic:** During the accumulation phase, maximize equity exposure for growth. Approaching and in early retirement, maintain enough bonds/cash to avoid selling stocks during downturns (sequence-of-returns risk).

### Sequence of Returns Risk: The Early Retiree's Main Challenge

The biggest risk in FIRE is retiring into a major market downturn. If you retire with $1M and the market drops 40%, you now have $600K — and withdrawing 4% from $600K only covers $24,000/year, potentially not enough.

**Mitigation strategies:**
1. **Cash cushion:** 1–2 years of expenses in cash — don't sell stocks during crashes
2. **Flexible withdrawal rate:** Reduce spending temporarily during downturns
3. **Variable spending:** Spend less in bad years, more in good years
4. **Part-time work option:** "One more year" of income helps substantially

## Practical Steps to Start Your FIRE Journey

### Step 1: Calculate Your FIRE Number
- Track all annual expenses for 3 months
- Multiply by 4 to annualize (or multiply annual total by 25)
- This is your target portfolio

### Step 2: Maximize Tax-Advantaged Accounts First
- **401(k):** Up to $23,500/year in 2026 (pre-tax or Roth)
- **IRA:** Up to $7,000/year
- **HSA (Health Savings Account):** $4,300/year if on HDHP — triple tax-advantaged
- **Roth conversions:** Strategic for FIRE due to Roth ladder for early access

### Step 3: Invest the Rest in Taxable Brokerage
- After maxing tax-advantaged accounts, invest in a standard brokerage
- Index funds with low turnover minimize taxable events

### Step 4: Track Your Progress
- **FIRECalc.com** — historical simulation of your portfolio survival rate
- **Personal Capital / Empower** — free net worth and investment tracking
- **Spreadsheet** — many FIRE community members swear by simple tracking sheets

## Common FIRE Objections Answered

**"I need healthcare — I can't retire early"**
In the U.S., pre-Medicare healthcare is the biggest FIRE obstacle. Solutions:
- ACA marketplace insurance (subsidies available based on income)
- Barista FIRE — part-time job with health benefits
- Healthcare sharing ministries (lower cost, higher risk)
- Geographic arbitrage — retire in a country with universal healthcare

**"What if I get bored?"**
Most FIRE achievers don't stop doing meaningful things — they start doing them on their own terms: travel, passion projects, entrepreneurship, volunteering, more time with family.

**"FIRE is only for high earners"**
While a high income accelerates FIRE, it's fundamentally about savings rate and intentional spending. There are FIRE success stories across many income levels.
