# Roth IRA vs Traditional IRA: Which One Is Right for You?

Choosing between a Roth IRA and a Traditional IRA is one of the most important retirement planning decisions you'll make. Get it right, and you could save tens of thousands of dollars in taxes over your lifetime.

The core difference is simple: **when do you pay taxes?**

## The Core Difference

**Traditional IRA: Pay taxes LATER**
- Contributions may be tax-deductible now
- Money grows tax-deferred
- Pay taxes when you withdraw in retirement

**Roth IRA: Pay taxes NOW**
- Contributions with after-tax money (no deduction)
- Money grows tax-free forever
- Withdrawals in retirement are completely tax-free

Both have a **$7,000 annual contribution limit** in 2026 ($8,000 if you're 50+).

## Traditional IRA: When It Wins

### Tax Deduction Today
If you're eligible for the deduction, a Traditional IRA reduces your taxable income right now. In a 24% tax bracket, a $7,000 contribution saves you $1,680 in taxes immediately.

### You Expect Lower Tax Rate in Retirement
If you're currently in a high tax bracket (32%+) and expect to be in a lower bracket (22% or below) in retirement, pay taxes later.

### No Income Limits for Contributions
Anyone with earned income can contribute to a Traditional IRA, though the deductibility phases out at higher incomes if you have a workplace plan.

**Deductibility phase-out (2026) if covered by workplace plan:**
- Single: $77,000–$87,000
- Married filing jointly: $123,000–$143,000

### Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs)
Starting at age 73, you must withdraw a minimum amount each year. This can actually be a drawback if you don't need the income.

## Roth IRA: When It Wins

### Tax-Free Growth Forever
The real magic of a Roth IRA is tax-free compounding. Watch what happens to $7,000 invested at 30 vs. 50:

| Age Started | Decades Growing | At Age 65 (10% avg) |
|------------|----------------|---------------------|
| 30 | 35 years | $197,472 |
| 40 | 25 years | $75,937 |
| 50 | 15 years | $29,240 |

All of that is tax-free income in retirement.

### You're Young (Low Tax Bracket Now)
In your 20s and 30s, you're likely in lower tax brackets (10–22%). Paying taxes now at 22% beats paying them later at potentially 32%+.

### No Required Minimum Distributions
Roth IRAs have no RMDs during your lifetime. This makes them excellent for:
- Leaving to heirs (tax-free inheritance)
- Keeping investment flexibility in retirement
- Estate planning

### Income Limits
Roth IRA has income limits — high earners can't contribute directly:

| Filing Status | Full Contribution | Partial Contribution | No Contribution |
|--------------|-------------------|---------------------|-----------------|
| Single | Under $150,000 | $150,000–$165,000 | Over $165,000 |
| Married Filing Jointly | Under $236,000 | $236,000–$246,000 | Over $246,000 |

*Note: A "backdoor Roth IRA" strategy allows high earners to contribute indirectly.*

## The Decision Framework

### Use Roth IRA if:
- ✅ You're in the 22% tax bracket or lower
- ✅ You're young (decades of tax-free growth ahead)
- ✅ You expect higher income (and taxes) in the future
- ✅ You want no RMDs
- ✅ You're early in your career

### Use Traditional IRA if:
- ✅ You're in the 32% bracket or higher
- ✅ You expect significantly lower income in retirement
- ✅ You need the tax deduction now to afford contributions
- ✅ You'll be in a lower tax state in retirement

### Do Both (If Possible):
Many financial advisors recommend **tax diversification** — having both Roth and Traditional accounts gives you flexibility to manage taxes strategically in retirement.

## The Backdoor Roth IRA

If you earn too much for direct Roth contributions, the backdoor Roth strategy lets you still contribute:

1. Make a non-deductible contribution to a Traditional IRA ($7,000)
2. Immediately convert it to a Roth IRA
3. Repeat annually

There's minimal tax owed on the conversion if done properly. Consult a tax advisor for your specific situation.

## What About the 401(k)?

The same Roth vs. Traditional logic applies to 401(k) accounts:

**Priority order for most people:**
1. Traditional 401(k) to get employer match (free money first!)
2. Roth IRA (flexible, better investment options)
3. Back to 401(k) for remaining contribution room

## Real Numbers: The Impact Over 30 Years

Assume $7,000/year invested at 10% average return, 30 years:
- Final portfolio: **~$1,265,000**

**Traditional:** Withdraw $63,250/year (5%). At 22% tax rate: **$49,335 net**
**Roth:** Withdraw $63,250/year (5%). Tax-free: **$63,250 net**

Annual difference: $13,915 — or over $417,000 extra tax-free income over 30 retirement years.

## Key Considerations for 2026

- Contribution limit: $7,000/year ($8,000 age 50+)
- You can contribute to both Roth and Traditional IRA, but combined limit is still $7,000
- Contributions for 2026 can be made until **April 15, 2027**
- Self-employed? Consider a Solo 401(k) or SEP-IRA for much higher limits

## Bottom Line

**For most young people (under 40):** Start with a Roth IRA. Tax-free growth over decades is extraordinarily powerful.

**For high earners (32%+ bracket):** Traditional IRA's deduction makes more sense, or consider a backdoor Roth for tax diversification.

**The most important thing:** Open an account and start contributing. The perfect choice between Roth and Traditional matters far less than actually investing consistently. A "suboptimal" IRA beats no IRA every single time.
