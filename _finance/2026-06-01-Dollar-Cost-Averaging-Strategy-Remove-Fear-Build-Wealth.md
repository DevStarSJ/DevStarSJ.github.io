Wall Street professionals with billion-dollar research budgets can't consistently time the market. What makes you think you can?

The answer: **you don't have to.** Dollar-cost averaging (DCA) is the strategy that turns market volatility from your enemy into your ally.

---

## What Is Dollar-Cost Averaging?

Dollar-cost averaging means **investing a fixed amount of money at regular intervals** — regardless of market conditions.

Instead of investing $12,000 all at once, you invest:
- $1,000 every month for 12 months

That's it. The strategy is that simple. The results are surprisingly powerful.

## How DCA Works in Practice

Let's say you invest $500/month in an S&P 500 index fund:

| Month | Price Per Share | Shares Bought |
|-------|----------------|---------------|
| Jan | $100 | 5.0 |
| Feb | $80 | 6.25 |
| Mar | $60 | 8.33 |
| Apr | $70 | 7.14 |
| May | $90 | 5.56 |
| Jun | $100 | 5.0 |

**Total invested:** $3,000  
**Total shares:** 37.28  
**Average price paid:** $80.47  
**Current price:** $100  
**Current value:** $3,728 ✅

If you had invested all $3,000 in January at $100/share, you'd have exactly 30 shares worth $3,000. DCA bought you **7.28 more shares** for the same money.

## The Psychology Behind DCA

The biggest enemy of investing isn't the market — it's **your own emotions**.

Studies show that individual investors consistently:
- Buy high (when markets are exciting)
- Sell low (when markets are scary)
- Miss the best days (by trying to time the market)

**DCA removes the emotional decision entirely.** You invest automatically, every month, no matter what the market does. When prices fall, you buy more shares. When prices rise, your existing shares are worth more.

> "The stock market is a device for transferring money from the impatient to the patient." — Warren Buffett

---

## DCA vs. Lump Sum: Which Is Better?

Studies show that **lump sum investing outperforms DCA about 67% of the time** — because markets go up more than they go down over time.

But here's the reality:
- Most people don't have a lump sum to invest
- Markets may drop right after a lump sum investment
- The psychological pain of a big loss early can cause you to quit investing entirely

**DCA wins on a behavioral level.** The best investment strategy is one you'll actually stick to.

## How to Start Dollar-Cost Averaging Today

### Step 1: Choose Your Investment
- **S&P 500 index fund** (e.g., VFIAX, VOO, SPY)
- **Total market index fund** (e.g., VTI)
- **Target-date retirement fund** (simplest option)

### Step 2: Set Your Amount
Start with whatever you can afford — even $50/month matters. The habit is more important than the amount.

### Step 3: Automate It
- Use your brokerage's automatic investment feature
- Schedule it for payday so you invest before you can spend it
- Never touch it during market downturns

### Step 4: Increase Over Time
As your income grows, increase your monthly contribution by even $25-50 at a time.
