# How to Retire Early: The FIRE Movement Explained

What if you didn't have to work until 65? What if, through disciplined saving and investing, you could retire in your 30s or 40s — or simply reach a point where work becomes optional?

That's the promise of the **FIRE movement**: Financial Independence, Retire Early.

## What Is FIRE?

FIRE stands for **Financial Independence, Retire Early**. At its core, FIRE is about:

1. **Aggressively saving** — typically 50-70% of income (vs. the average 5%)
2. **Investing in low-cost index funds** — mainly stocks, with some bonds
3. **Reaching a "number"** — enough invested so your returns cover your expenses forever
4. **Optional work** — you *can* keep working, but you don't *have* to

FIRE is less about never working again and more about **having the choice**. Many FIRE achievers continue working — but on their own terms, doing work they actually love.

## The Math Behind FIRE: The 4% Rule

The FIRE movement is built on the **4% rule**, a finding from the Trinity Study (1998) that a portfolio of 60% stocks / 40% bonds historically survives 30+ years of withdrawals at 4% annually.

**Your FIRE Number = Annual Expenses × 25**

If you need $40,000/year to live:
- **FIRE Number:** $40,000 × 25 = **$1,000,000**

When you have $1,000,000 invested, you can withdraw 4% ($40,000) per year and the portfolio historically sustains itself indefinitely (based on historical stock market returns of ~7% inflation-adjusted).

**The inverse: Savings Rate Determines Retirement Age**

| Savings Rate | Years to Retirement |
|---|---|
| 10% | 43 years |
| 20% | 37 years |
| 30% | 28 years |
| 40% | 22 years |
| 50% | 17 years |
| 60% | 12.5 years |
| 70% | 8.5 years |

*Assumes 5% real investment return and expenses = income × (1 - savings rate)*

## Types of FIRE

**Lean FIRE:** Retire on minimal expenses (under $25,000/year). Requires extreme frugality but a smaller number ($625,000). Often achieved in late 30s.

**Regular FIRE:** The "standard" — retire comfortably on $40,000-60,000/year. Number: $1M-$1.5M.

**Fat FIRE:** Retire with a high-income lifestyle ($100,000+/year). Requires $2.5M+. For those who don't want to give up luxuries.

**Barista FIRE / CoastFIRE:** Partially retire — quit the high-stress career but do part-time or enjoyable work that covers daily expenses, while investments grow untouched. The "middle path."

## The FIRE Framework: How to Get There

### Step 1: Calculate Your Annual Expenses
Track 12 months of spending. Be honest — include irregular expenses (car repair, medical, etc.). This is your baseline.

### Step 2: Calculate Your FIRE Number
Annual Expenses × 25 = Your FIRE Number

**Example:**
- Current spending: $50,000/year
- FIRE Number: $1,250,000

### Step 3: Maximize Income
FIRE is accelerated by income. Many FIRE pursuers:
- Advance careers aggressively in their 20s-30s
- Build side businesses
- Develop high-income skills (tech, finance, medicine)
- Avoid lifestyle inflation (keep expenses stable as income rises)

### Step 4: Aggressively Save and Invest
**Tax-advantaged accounts first:**
1. 401(k) to employer match
2. HSA (triple tax advantage if eligible)
3. Roth IRA (up to $7,000/year in 2026)
4. Back-door Roth if above income limits
5. 401(k) maximum ($23,500 in 2026)

**Taxable brokerage** for amounts above tax-advantaged limits

**Investment allocation:**
- Classic FIRE: 90-100% stocks (higher growth, higher volatility)
- Popular choice: VTI (total US market) + VXUS (international)

### Step 5: Optimize Expenses
The two biggest expenses are usually housing and transportation:

**Housing:** House hacking (rent out rooms), moving to lower cost-of-living areas, or downsizing

**Transportation:** One reliable used car instead of two new cars; biking; public transit

**The FIRE calculus:** Every $1 you cut from monthly expenses:
1. Saves $1/month in current spending
2. **Reduces your FIRE number by $300** (you need 25x less annually)
3. Has a **double compounding benefit**

## FIRE Withdrawal Strategy

Once you reach your FIRE number, the classic strategy is:

- **4% rule:** Withdraw 4% of portfolio value annually
- **Asset allocation:** 60-70% stocks / 30-40% bonds at retirement
- **Roth conversion ladder:** Convert 401k to Roth IRA annually during early retirement (access after 5-year wait)
- **Flexibility:** Reduce spending slightly in bad market years (variable withdrawal)

## Realistic Challenges

**Healthcare (US-specific):** Without an employer, health insurance is expensive. Budget $12,000-18,000/year until Medicare eligibility at 65. ACA subsidies can help at lower income.

**Early withdrawal penalties:** 401k and IRAs have 10% penalties before age 59½. Workarounds: Roth conversion ladder, 72(t) distributions, Rule of 55.

**Sequence of returns risk:** Retiring into a market crash can permanently damage your portfolio. Solution: keep 1-2 years of expenses in cash; stay flexible with spending.

**Inflation:** 4% rule accounts for historical inflation, but unexpected high inflation periods are a risk.

**Longevity:** If you retire at 35, you need your portfolio to last 50+ years. Consider a 3.5% withdrawal rate for extra safety.

## Is FIRE Right for You?

FIRE requires genuine sacrifice — giving up spending today for freedom tomorrow. It's not for everyone.

**You might love FIRE if:**
- You value freedom and flexibility over consumption
- You're motivated by long-term goals
- Your career feels like a treadmill, not fulfillment
- You're willing to optimize spending without feeling deprived

**FIRE might not be for you if:**
- You genuinely love your work and can't imagine stopping
- Your lifestyle requires high spending (family size, health needs, location)
- You prefer steady accumulation without extreme frugality

## The Real Goal: Financial Independence

Even if "retire at 40" isn't your dream, the underlying goal — **having enough money that work becomes a choice** — is one of the most powerful financial targets you can have.

Financial independence gives you leverage in every negotiation, resilience in every crisis, and freedom to make decisions from strength rather than necessity.

Start the math. Know your number. Build toward it.
