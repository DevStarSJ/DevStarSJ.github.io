There's a retirement account that lets you invest after-tax money, watch it grow completely tax-free for decades, and withdraw it in retirement without owing a single cent in taxes.

It's called a Roth IRA — and if you're in your 20s or 30s, it may be the most powerful wealth-building tool available to you right now.

Here's why starting early makes an almost unfair difference.

## What Is a Roth IRA?

A Roth IRA (Individual Retirement Account) is a retirement savings account where:
- You contribute **after-tax** money (no deduction now)
- Your money grows **completely tax-free**
- You withdraw in retirement **tax-free**
- There are **no required minimum distributions** (unlike Traditional IRAs)

The key benefit: you pay taxes on the seed, not the harvest. If you invest $7,000 today and it grows to $70,000 over 30 years, you owe zero taxes on that $63,000 gain when you withdraw.

## 2026 Roth IRA Rules

**Contribution Limits:**
- Under 50: $7,000/year
- 50 and over: $8,000/year (catch-up contribution)

**Income Limits (2026 estimates):**
- Single filers: Full contribution up to ~$146,000 MAGI; phases out up to ~$161,000
- Married filing jointly: Full up to ~$230,000; phases out up to ~$240,000

**Above the limit?** Use the Backdoor Roth IRA strategy (contribute to Traditional IRA, then convert).

**Withdrawal rules:**
- Contributions (not earnings) can be withdrawn anytime, penalty-free
- Earnings: tax-free and penalty-free after age 59½ AND after 5-year holding period

## The Compound Math: Why Age 25 Beats Age 35

Let's run the numbers with a simple comparison:

**Person A** opens a Roth IRA at 25 and contributes $7,000/year until 35 — then stops. Total invested: $70,000.

**Person B** opens at 35 and contributes $7,000/year until 65. Total invested: $210,000.

Assuming 7% average annual return (conservative historical average):

| | Person A | Person B |
|--|---------|---------|
| Invested | $70,000 | $210,000 |
| Balance at 65 | ~$1,068,000 | ~$735,000 |

Person A invested **$140,000 less** and ends up with **$333,000 more**.

That's the power of compounding. The early decade of growth that Person B missed can never be fully replaced, no matter how much they invest later.

## Roth IRA vs. Traditional IRA vs. 401(k)

| Feature | Roth IRA | Traditional IRA | 401(k) |
|---------|---------|----------------|-------|
| Tax on contribution | After-tax | Pre-tax (deductible) | Pre-tax |
| Tax on withdrawal | Tax-free | Taxed as income | Taxed as income |
| Contribution limit | $7,000 | $7,000 | $23,000 |
| Income limit | Yes | Yes (for deduction) | No |
| RMDs at 73 | No | Yes | Yes |
| Early withdrawal (contributions) | Penalty-free | 10% penalty | 10% penalty |

**General rule for your 20s-30s:** Roth > Traditional because you're likely in a lower tax bracket now than you'll be at retirement. Pay low taxes now, avoid taxes on decades of growth later.

## How to Actually Invest Your Roth IRA

Opening a Roth IRA is easy (Fidelity, Vanguard, Schwab all offer free accounts). The crucial question is: **what do you invest in?**

A Roth IRA is not a savings account. The account itself doesn't earn anything — you have to invest the money inside it.

### For Most People in Their 20s–30s: Target-Date Fund or Three-Fund Portfolio

**Option 1: Target-Date Fund (Simplest)**
Pick a fund matching your approximate retirement year, like VTTSX (Vanguard Target Retirement 2060). It automatically adjusts from aggressive (stocks) to conservative (bonds) as you age. Set it, forget it.

**Option 2: Three-Fund Portfolio (Slight More Control)**
- 70-80% US Total Stock Market (e.g., FZROX at Fidelity — 0% expense ratio)
- 15-20% International Stocks (e.g., FZILX)
- 5-10% US Bonds (e.g., FXNAX)

Rebalance once a year. That's it.

**Option 3: S&P 500 Index Fund**
If you want even simpler: put everything in an S&P 500 index fund (VOO, FXAIX, IVV). Historically ~10% average annual return over long periods.

## The "Priority Order" for Retirement Saving

Where should your money go first? Here's the widely recommended order:

1. **401(k) up to employer match** — Free money. Always get the full match first.
2. **Pay off high-interest debt** (8%+ interest) — Guaranteed return.
3. **Max Roth IRA ($7,000/year)** — Tax-free growth for decades.
4. **Max 401(k) ($23,000/year)** — More tax-advantaged space.
5. **Taxable brokerage account** — After all tax-advantaged space is used.

Don't skip the Roth IRA to max the 401(k). The tax-free nature of Roth makes it uniquely valuable, especially for long time horizons.

## Real Strategies to Fund Your Roth IRA

**The Monthly Auto-Invest Method**
$7,000/year = $583/month. Set up an automatic monthly transfer into your Roth IRA and auto-invest in your chosen fund. You'll never miss the money if it leaves before you see it.

**The Tax Refund Method**
Use your annual tax refund to fund most or all of your Roth IRA contribution. A $5,000-7,000 refund effectively funds the year in one shot.

**The Side Hustle Top-Up**
Fund the baseline from your job, and use any side income to top up to the $7,000 limit.

**Note:** You can contribute to your Roth IRA anytime between January 1 and Tax Day (April 15) of the following year. So you have until April 15, 2027 to contribute for 2026.

## Roth IRA as an Emergency Fund (The "Roth Hack")

Since you can withdraw contributions (not earnings) penalty-free at any time, your Roth IRA can double as a last-resort emergency fund in your 20s. This isn't ideal, but it means you're not sacrificing retirement investing while building your emergency fund.

**Rule:** Only do this if you have no other options, and replace what you took out as soon as possible.

## Common Roth IRA Mistakes

**Not opening one because you "can't max it"**
Contribute whatever you can. $500/year beats $0. Open the account and start.

**Leaving money in cash inside the Roth**
Your contributions sit in a money market fund until you invest them. Check your account — make sure the money is actually invested in a fund.

**Withdrawing earnings early**
Contributions: fine to withdraw. Earnings before 59½ and before the 5-year rule: 10% penalty + taxes. Know the difference.

**Waiting until "later" to start**
Every year you wait costs compounding years you can never get back. The best time was 5 years ago. The second best time is today.

## The Bottom Line

A Roth IRA is one of the few places in the US tax code that genuinely rewards long-term thinking. The government taxes you now — at your current (lower) rate — and then never touches your money again.

If you're 25 and invest $7,000 in a Roth IRA today, that money could be worth $75,000–$100,000 at retirement. Completely tax-free.

That's not magic. That's compound interest with a 40-year runway — and it only works if you start.

Open the account today.
