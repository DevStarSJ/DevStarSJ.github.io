Saving for retirement is hard. But there's actually a harder problem: **making your money last once you retire.** With life expectancy stretching well into the 80s and 90s, a 65-year-old retiree may need to fund 25–35 years of living expenses. Run out of money at 82 is a catastrophe.

This guide covers the key strategies for building a retirement income plan that won't leave you broke.

---

## The Core Challenge: Sequence of Returns Risk

The single greatest threat to a retirement portfolio isn't a low average return — it's experiencing major losses in the **early years of retirement** while you're withdrawing money.

**Example:** Two retirees each start with $1,000,000 and withdraw $50,000/year. Portfolio A experiences losses early; Portfolio B experiences losses late.

| Year | Portfolio A Returns | Portfolio A Balance | Portfolio B Returns | Portfolio B Balance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | -25% | $700,000 | +15% | $1,100,000 |
| 2 | -15% | $545,000 | +12% | $1,182,000 |
| 3 | +20% | $604,000 | +10% | $1,250,000 |
| 5 | +15% | $642,000 | -15% | $1,113,000 |
| 10 | +10% | $711,000 | -25% | $963,000 |
| 20 | (depleted) | $0 | +8% | $1,240,000 |

Same average returns, completely different outcomes. Portfolio A ran out of money at year 17. Portfolio B is thriving at year 20.

This is why **the order of returns matters enormously** in retirement.

## The 4% Rule (and Its Limitations)

The 4% rule comes from the Trinity Study (1994): if you withdraw 4% of your portfolio in year one of retirement, then adjust for inflation each year, your portfolio had a 95%+ probability of lasting 30 years based on historical US market data.

**For a $1,000,000 portfolio:** 4% = $40,000/year in withdrawals.

### Why the 4% Rule May Be Too Aggressive Today

1. **Lower bond yields** — the original study assumed higher fixed income returns
2. **Higher equity valuations** — future returns may be lower than historical averages
3. **Longer retirements** — 30-year periods may not be enough for today's retirees
4. **Sequence of returns risk** — recent high inflation and market volatility

Many financial planners now recommend **3.3–3.5%** as a safer withdrawal rate for new retirees in 2026.

**For a $1,000,000 portfolio:** 3.5% = $35,000/year.

## The Bucket Strategy

Instead of one big investment pool, divide assets into three "buckets" based on when you'll need the money:

### Bucket 1: Short-Term (Years 1–3)
**Allocation:** Cash, money market, short-term CDs
**Purpose:** Cover 2–3 years of living expenses in liquid, stable form
**Amount:** 2–3× your annual spending needs

This bucket protects you from having to sell stocks during a market crash. You live off this while the other buckets recover.

### Bucket 2: Medium-Term (Years 4–10)
**Allocation:** Bonds, bond funds, dividend stocks, conservative income-producing investments
**Purpose:** Generate income to refill Bucket 1
**Amount:** 5–7× annual spending

As Bucket 1 is depleted, Bucket 2 generates income to refill it.

### Bucket 3: Long-Term (Years 11+)
**Allocation:** Stocks, REITs, growth investments
**Purpose:** Long-term growth to beat inflation and fund the later decades
**Amount:** Remaining portfolio

Bucket 3 can weather short-term volatility because you won't need it for 10+ years.

## Retirement Income Sources: Building Your Stack

![Retirement planning](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1559526324-4b87b5e36e44?w=900&auto=format&fit=crop)
*Photo by Towfiqu barbhuiya on Unsplash*

The best retirement income plans have **multiple income streams** that reduce dependence on any single source.

### 1. Social Security
- Currently pays an average of $1,975/month for retired workers (2026)
- Full Retirement Age (FRA): 67 for people born after 1960
- **Claiming at 62:** Benefits reduced by ~30%
- **Claiming at 70:** Benefits increased by ~32% above FRA

**The delay game:** For every year you delay claiming from FRA to 70, benefits increase by 8%. This is the highest guaranteed return available to retirees.

### 2. Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs) from Tax-Deferred Accounts
- **IRA/401k:** Required withdrawals starting at age 73 (SECURE 2.0 Act)
- **Formula:** Account balance ÷ IRS life expectancy factor
- RMDs create taxable income whether you need the money or not

**Strategy:** Consider Roth conversions during low-income years before RMDs kick in.

### 3. Pension (If You Have One)
Defined benefit pensions provide guaranteed income for life. If you have one:
- Understand your payout options (single life vs. joint and survivor)
- Factor it in before deciding when to claim Social Security

### 4. Dividend Income
A portfolio focused on dividend-paying stocks and REITs can generate income without selling shares.

**Target yield:** 3–4% on your equity portfolio
- **Example:** $600,000 in dividend stocks at 3.5% yield = $21,000/year in dividends

### 5. Annuities (Use Carefully)
Income annuities convert a lump sum into guaranteed lifetime income. Useful for covering baseline expenses beyond Social Security.

- **SPIA (Single Premium Immediate Annuity):** Pay a lump sum, receive monthly income immediately
- **DIA (Deferred Income Annuity):** Cheaper option that starts payments at a future age (80+)

**Warning:** Avoid complex variable or indexed annuities with high fees. Stick to simple SPIAs or DIAs from highly-rated insurers.

## Tax Strategy in Retirement

Managing taxes in retirement can be as valuable as managing investments.

### The Tax Bracket Management Game
In retirement, you can often control your taxable income strategically:

**Goal:** Fill up the 12% or 22% bracket efficiently each year

**Tools:**
- **Roth conversions:** Convert traditional IRA to Roth during low-income years
- **Tax-loss harvesting:** Realize capital losses to offset gains
- **Asset location:** Keep tax-inefficient assets (REITs, bonds) in tax-deferred accounts; stocks in taxable accounts
- **Qualified Charitable Distributions (QCDs):** If charitable, donate directly from IRA to charity — counts against RMD but not as taxable income

### The Three-Bucket Tax Strategy
- **Taxable accounts** (brokerage): Draw from first to control income
- **Tax-deferred accounts** (traditional IRA/401k): Draw from second
- **Tax-free accounts** (Roth IRA): Draw from last; keep growing as long as possible

## Healthcare: The Wildcard Expense

Healthcare is the most unpredictable retirement expense. Key considerations:

### Medicare Coverage
- **Medicare Part A (hospital):** Free if you paid Medicare taxes
- **Medicare Part B (medical):** $185.50/month standard premium in 2026
- **Medicare Part D (prescriptions):** Varies by plan
- **Medigap/Supplement:** Covers gaps in Parts A and B

**Medicare enrollment:** Sign up within 3 months before or after turning 65 or face lifetime penalties.

### Long-Term Care Planning
One of the biggest risks retirees face:
- Average nursing home cost: ~$100,000/year (2026)
- Average home care aide: ~$60,000/year
- Probability of needing long-term care at some point: ~70% for people over 65

**Options:**
1. Self-insure with a larger portfolio
2. Long-term care insurance (buy in your 50s when premiums are manageable)
3. Hybrid life insurance/LTC policies

---

## Withdrawal Order: Which Accounts First?

Generally withdraw in this order to minimize lifetime taxes:

1. **Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs)** — mandatory, can't avoid
2. **Taxable accounts** — capital gains rates; step-up in basis at death
3. **Traditional IRA/401k** — ordinary income tax; draw to fill lower tax brackets
4. **Roth IRA** — tax-free; save for last or leave for heirs

## A Sample Retirement Income Plan (Age 67, $1M Portfolio)

| Income Source | Annual Amount |
|---|---|
| Social Security (claimed at 67) | $28,000 |
| Dividend income (3.5% on $600K) | $21,000 |
| Portfolio withdrawal (2.5% on $400K) | $10,000 |
| **Total Income** | **$59,000** |

After standard deduction ($15,000) and Social Security exclusion (partial): effective federal tax rate approximately 7–10%.

This plan:
- Keeps total portfolio withdrawal rate at sustainable levels
- Allows Roth IRA to continue growing
- Maintains Bucket 1 for 2–3 years of cash
- Can flex upward or downward based on market conditions

## Key Retirement Income Principles

1. **Delay Social Security if possible** — 8% annual increase from FRA to 70 is unbeatable
2. **Use the bucket strategy** — don't be forced to sell stocks in a downturn
3. **Plan for healthcare costs** — especially long-term care
4. **Manage taxes proactively** — Roth conversions and bracket management matter
5. **Maintain flexibility** — the best plans can adjust to market conditions
6. **Keep 1–2 years in cash** — protects against sequence of returns risk

The goal isn't to die with the most money — it's to never run out of money while living the life you planned for. With careful planning, that's an achievable goal.
