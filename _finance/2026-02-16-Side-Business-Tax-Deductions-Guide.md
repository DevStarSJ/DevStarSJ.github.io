# Side Business Tax Deductions: What You Can Write Off in 2026

Running a side business? Congratulations—you've unlocked one of the best tax advantages available to everyday Americans. But only if you know what you can deduct.

## Why Side Business Deductions Matter

When you're employed, your options are limited. But as a side business owner, you can deduct **ordinary and necessary** business expenses from your taxable income.

**The math:**
- Side business revenue: $20,000
- Deductible expenses: $8,000
- Taxable income: $12,000
- Tax savings (24% bracket): $1,920

That's nearly $2,000 back in your pocket—legally.

## Home Office Deduction

If you use part of your home exclusively and regularly for business, you can claim this deduction.

### Two Methods:

**1. Simplified Method:**
- $5 per square foot
- Maximum 300 sq ft = $1,500
- Easy, no complex calculations

**2. Actual Expense Method:**
- Calculate actual costs (rent/mortgage interest, utilities, insurance)
- Multiply by percentage of home used for business
- More paperwork, potentially bigger deduction

### Requirements:
- Space must be used **exclusively** for business
- Must be your principal place of business (or where you meet clients)
- Applies to homeowners AND renters

## Vehicle and Travel Expenses

### Two Methods for Vehicles:

**1. Standard Mileage Rate (2026):**
- 70 cents per business mile
- Simpler, requires mileage log

**2. Actual Expense Method:**
- Gas, insurance, repairs, depreciation
- Multiply by business use percentage
- Requires detailed records

### What Counts as Business Travel:
- Visiting clients
- Picking up supplies
- Attending business events
- NOT commuting to a regular job

### Out-of-Town Travel:
- Airfare, train tickets
- Hotels and lodging
- 50% of meals while traveling
- Rental cars
- Conference registration fees

## Education and Training

You can deduct education that:
- Maintains or improves skills in your current business
- Is required to keep your professional status

### Deductible:
- Online courses related to your business
- Industry conferences
- Books and publications
- Professional certifications
- Workshop and seminar fees

### NOT Deductible:
- Education that qualifies you for a new career
- Basic education requirements

## Health Insurance (Self-Employed)

If you're not eligible for employer coverage, you can deduct:
- Health insurance premiums for yourself
- Dental and vision coverage
- Coverage for spouse and dependents
- Long-term care insurance (with limits)

**Note:** This is an "above the line" deduction—you get it even if you don't itemize.

## Retirement Contributions

Self-employed retirement accounts offer huge tax advantages:

### SEP-IRA:
- Contribute up to 25% of net self-employment income
- Maximum $69,000 (2026)
- Easy to set up and manage

### Solo 401(k):
- Employee contribution: $23,500
- Employer contribution: 25% of compensation
- Total max: $70,000
- Best for high earners

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### 1. Missing Documentation
Keep receipts for everything. Use apps like Expensify or Dext to scan and organize.

### 2. Personal vs. Business Mixing
Keep separate bank accounts and credit cards. Mixed finances are audit red flags.

### 3. Overreaching
The home office must be **exclusively** for business. A corner of your living room doesn't count.

### 4. Forgetting the Obvious
- Mileage logs for every business trip
- Meal receipts with business purpose noted
- Home internet bills (don't forget!)

### 5. Not Tracking Small Expenses
$5 here, $10 there—it adds up. Track everything.

## Documentation Requirements

### What to Keep:
- Receipts (paper or digital)
- Bank and credit card statements
- Mileage logs
- Home office measurements
- Contracts and invoices
- Calendar showing business activities

### How Long to Keep:
- Generally 3 years from filing
- 6 years if you underreported income by 25%+
- Indefinitely for fraud (hopefully not you!)

## Maximizing Your Deductions

### Start of Year:

2. Choose a mileage tracking app
3. Organize your receipt system

### Throughout Year:
1. Log expenses weekly
2. Save all receipts immediately
3. Track mileage for every business trip

### End of Year:
1. Review all deductions
2. Make retirement contributions before deadline
3. Plan large purchases strategically

- Your side business earns $30,000+
- You have complex deductions
- You're unsure about hobby vs. business rules
- You've been audited before
- You want peace of mind
