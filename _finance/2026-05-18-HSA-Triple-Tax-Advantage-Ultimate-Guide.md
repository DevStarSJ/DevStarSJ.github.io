There's a financial account so good that tax experts call it the "ultimate retirement account" — yet most people either don't know it exists or massively underuse it. The **Health Savings Account (HSA)** offers a triple tax advantage that no other account in the US tax code can match.

## What Is an HSA?

An HSA is a tax-advantaged savings account available to people enrolled in a **High-Deductible Health Plan (HDHP)**. It's designed to help cover medical costs, but savvy investors use it as a powerful long-term wealth-building tool.

## The Triple Tax Advantage

Here's why the HSA is unmatched:

1. **Tax-free contributions** — Money goes in pre-tax (or you get a tax deduction)
2. **Tax-free growth** — Investments inside the HSA grow without being taxed
3. **Tax-free withdrawals** — For qualified medical expenses, withdrawals are 100% tax-free

No other account offers all three. A 401(k) gives you #1 and #2 but taxes withdrawals. A Roth IRA gives you #2 and #3 but contributions are after-tax. The HSA gives you all three — if you use it right.

## 2026 HSA Contribution Limits

| Coverage Type | 2026 Limit |
|---|---|
| Individual | $4,300 |
| Family | $8,550 |
| Age 55+ catch-up | +$1,000 |

To qualify, your health insurance plan must have:
- Deductible of at least $1,650 (individual) or $3,300 (family)
- Out-of-pocket maximum of $8,300 or less (individual) / $16,600 or less (family)

## The Strategy Most People Miss: Invest Your HSA

Most people treat their HSA like a flexible spending account — money goes in, bills get paid, balance stays near zero. This is leaving enormous money on the table.

**The optimal HSA strategy:**

1. **Contribute the maximum each year**
2. **Invest every dollar** in low-cost index funds
3. **Pay medical bills out of pocket** (from your regular savings)
4. **Save all your receipts forever**
5. **Let the HSA grow untouched for decades**
6. **Reimburse yourself years later** (no time limit on reimbursements!)

This is legal and IRS-approved. You can pay a medical bill in 2026, save the receipt, and reimburse yourself tax-free from your HSA in 2036 — after a decade of tax-free growth.

## HSA as a Retirement Account

At age 65, the HSA becomes functionally equivalent to a **Traditional IRA**:
- Withdrawals for non-medical expenses are taxed as ordinary income (just like a 401k)
- No penalty for non-medical withdrawals (unlike before 65, when there's a 20% penalty)

This means if you never need the money for medical expenses, you still have a solid retirement account. If you DO use it for medical expenses (and retirees have a lot of those), every withdrawal is tax-free.

**Expected lifetime medical costs in retirement:** The average couple retiring today will spend **$300,000+ on healthcare in retirement**. Your HSA can cover this tax-free.

## HSA vs. Other Accounts: Which to Fund First?

Here's the optimal contribution order for most people:

1. **401(k)** up to employer match (free money, always first)
2. **HSA** maximum (triple tax advantage, use it)
3. **Roth IRA** maximum (tax-free growth)
4. **401(k)** beyond the match
5. **Taxable brokerage** (no tax advantages, but no limits)

Many financial experts argue the HSA should actually be **#1 or #2** due to the triple tax benefit — even before maxing out retirement accounts.

## What Counts as a Qualified Medical Expense?

The list is broad:
- Doctor visits and copays
- Prescription medications
- Dental and vision care
- Mental health services
- Chiropractor, acupuncture
- Hospital bills
- LASIK eye surgery
- Sunscreen (SPF 15+, yes really)
- Over-the-counter medications (since 2020)

## How to Open and Maximize Your HSA

**Step 1:** Confirm your health plan qualifies as an HDHP

**Step 2:** Open an HSA with a provider that allows investing:
- **Fidelity HSA** — Best overall: no fees, great investment options
- **Lively** — No account fees, links to TD Ameritrade for investing
- **HealthEquity** — Common employer-sponsored option

**Step 3:** Contribute the maximum (automate it if possible)

**Step 4:** Move to the investment option (most HSAs separate cash and investment portions)

**Step 5:** Invest in low-cost index funds — FSKAX, VTSAX, or an S&P 500 fund

**Step 6:** Organize medical receipts digitally (use an app or folder in Google Drive)

## The Numbers: What Maximum HSA Contributions Become

Starting at age 35, maxing out a family HSA ($8,550/year) with 7% average annual return:

| Age | HSA Value |
|---|---|
| 45 | ~$118,000 |
| 55 | ~$353,000 |
| 65 | ~$881,000 |

Nearly **$900,000** available for tax-free medical expenses in retirement — or tax-deferred withdrawals for anything else. All from contributing $8,550/year.

## The Catch

The main limitation: you can only contribute to an HSA while enrolled in an HDHP. If you switch to a PPO or HMO, contributions stop (though existing funds remain and keep growing).

For young, healthy people who rarely use medical care, an HDHP plus HSA is almost always the financially superior choice — even if the premiums seem higher on paper, the tax savings and HSA benefit usually more than compensate.

## Start Now

If your employer offers an HDHP option and you're not maximizing your HSA, you're missing one of the best tax advantages in the US tax code. Set it up, invest every dollar, pay your medical bills from other funds, and let it compound for decades.

Your future self will thank you — especially when the medical bills start rolling in at 70.
