Real estate is one of the most proven wealth-building vehicles in history. But between the massive down payments, mortgage stress, problematic tenants, and 2 AM maintenance calls — most people can't or don't want to be landlords.

Real estate crowdfunding changes that equation entirely.

---

## What Is Real Estate Crowdfunding?

Real estate crowdfunding pools money from many investors to fund real estate projects — commercial buildings, apartment complexes, rental properties, and more.

You invest **as little as $10–$500**, earn **passive income** (dividends/distributions), and benefit from **property appreciation** — all without managing a single tenant.

---

## Types of Real Estate Crowdfunding

### 1. REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts)

The most liquid option — REITs trade like stocks on major exchanges. They're required by law to distribute **90% of taxable income** to shareholders.

**Pros:** High liquidity, low minimums ($1–10), diversified, regulated
**Cons:** Less control, subject to stock market volatility

**Popular REITs:** Realty Income (O), Prologis (PLD), American Tower (AMT)

## How Returns Work

### Equity Investments
You own a share of the property. Returns come from:
- **Rental income** (quarterly or monthly distributions)
- **Appreciation** (profit when property sells)
- Typical total return: **8–15% annually**

### Debt Investments
You're the lender. The developer pays you interest.
- Lower risk than equity (senior secured)
- Returns: **6–10% annually**
- Fixed payments, more predictable

## Risks to Understand

**1. Illiquidity:** Most crowdfunded real estate is locked for 3–10 years. Don't invest money you'll need soon.

**2. Platform risk:** You're dependent on the platform staying solvent. Stick to established platforms.

**3. Project risk:** Individual projects can fail. Diversify across multiple investments.

**4. No guaranteed returns:** Historical returns don't guarantee future results.

**5. Accreditation requirements:** Some platforms require being an "accredited investor" ($200K+ income or $1M+ net worth).

## Who Should Consider This?

**Good fit if you:**
- Want real estate exposure without being a landlord
- Have $100–$10,000 to invest long-term
- Understand you can't access the money quickly
- Want income-producing assets in your portfolio

**Not a good fit if:**
- You need the money within 2–3 years
- You're just getting started with an emergency fund
- You're not willing to accept possible total loss of investment

## Getting Started: A Simple Path

1. **Start with REITs** — buy $200 of Realty Income (O) to see how it feels
2. **Open a Fundrise account** — invest $500, explore the platform
3. **Reinvest all dividends** for 3–5 years
4. **Graduate to private platforms** as your portfolio grows

Real estate crowdfunding won't make you rich overnight. But with $5,000–$10,000 invested consistently, you can be earning **$400–$1,000 in passive income per year** within a few years — without ever fixing a leaky faucet.
