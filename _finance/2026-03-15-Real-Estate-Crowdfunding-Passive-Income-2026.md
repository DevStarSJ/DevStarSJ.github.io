## What Is Real Estate Crowdfunding?

Real estate crowdfunding pools money from many investors to fund real estate projects — residential, commercial, industrial, or mixed-use. Instead of needing $200,000+ for a down payment on a rental property, you contribute $500 or $5,000 alongside hundreds of other investors.

The platform manages everything: property acquisition, management, and eventual sale. You receive:
- **Dividend income** (from rents): quarterly or monthly distributions
- **Appreciation income**: your share of profits when properties are sold

Returns typically range from **7–12% annually** depending on the platform and investment type.

---

## How It Works: The Two Main Structures

### 1. Equity Crowdfunding
You own a fractional share of the actual property. When the property appreciates and is sold, you receive a proportional share of the profit.

- **Higher potential returns** (10–15% in good markets)
- **Longer time horizon** (3–10 years typically)
- **Less liquidity** — your money is locked up

### 2. Debt Crowdfunding
You act as the lender, funding real estate loans. The property owner pays interest, which flows to you as income.

- **More predictable returns** (7–10%)
- **Fixed term** (6–24 months typically)
- **Higher in capital structure** — you're paid before equity investors if something goes wrong
- **Less upside** — you don't benefit if the property value skyrockets

## Real Estate Crowdfunding vs. REITs: Which Is Better?

Both let you invest in real estate without owning property. Key differences:

| Feature | Crowdfunding | Public REITs |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| **Liquidity** | Low (months to years) | High (sell anytime in market) |
| **Minimum** | $10–$25,000 | $50–$500 (1 share) |
| **Returns** | 7–15% (historical) | 4–8% (dividend + price) |
| **Diversification** | Specific projects | Broad exposure |
| **Market volatility** | Low (private, not daily-priced) | High (correlates with stock market) |
| **Transparency** | High (see specific properties) | Low (large portfolios) |

**Best approach**: Use REITs (ETFs like VNQ, SCHH) for liquid, diversified real estate exposure. Use crowdfunding for higher returns and lower correlation to stock market.

## The Risks You Need to Understand

Real estate crowdfunding is **not risk-free**. Key risks:

**Illiquidity Risk**: Most platforms lock your money for 3–7 years. If you need cash during that time, you may not be able to access it.

**Platform Risk**: If the crowdfunding platform itself fails (bankruptcy), your investments could be at risk even if the underlying properties are performing. Stick to established platforms with track records.

**Market Risk**: Real estate values can decline. Overbuilt markets, rising interest rates, and economic downturns affect returns.

**Project Risk**: Individual deals can underperform projections. Developers may face cost overruns, permitting delays, or tenant vacancies.

**Manager Risk**: The people managing the properties make decisions that affect your returns. Poor management = poor returns.

**Mitigation**: Diversify across platforms and deal types. Only invest money you won't need for 3–5+ years.

## Tax Treatment of Real Estate Crowdfunding Returns

- **Dividend income**: Generally taxed as ordinary income (like REIT dividends), though some platforms offer pass-through deductions
- **Capital gains on sale**: Long-term capital gains rates if held 1+ year
- **Depreciation**: Equity investments may pass through depreciation deductions, reducing your taxable income
- **1099s**: Platforms issue 1099-DIV or 1099-INT; tax reporting is generally straightforward

Consult a CPA for specific tax treatment — real estate tax law is complex and platform-specific.
