## What Is Dividend Investing?

When a company earns profits, it can either reinvest them or distribute them to shareholders. These cash distributions are called **dividends**. As an investor, you receive these payments just for holding shares — no work required.

**Example:** If you own 100 shares of a stock paying a $2/share annual dividend, you receive **$200/year** in passive income.

---

## Why Dividend Stocks Are Powerful

### 1. Regular Cash Flow
Unlike growth stocks, dividend stocks pay you on a schedule — monthly, quarterly, or annually. This creates predictable passive income.

### 2. Compounding Through Reinvestment
Use a **DRIP (Dividend Reinvestment Plan)** to automatically reinvest dividends into more shares. Over time, you own more shares, which pay more dividends, which buy more shares. This is compounding on autopilot.

### 3. Lower Volatility
Companies that pay consistent dividends tend to be mature, stable businesses (utilities, consumer staples, healthcare). They typically hold up better during market downturns.

### 4. Inflation Hedge
Many dividend companies raise their payments every year. Stocks with 10+ consecutive years of dividend increases are called **Dividend Aristocrats** — and they often beat inflation.

## Key Metrics to Evaluate Dividend Stocks

### Dividend Yield
**Formula:** Annual Dividend ÷ Stock Price × 100

| Yield | What It Means |
|---|---|
| 0–2% | Low yield, often high-growth stocks |
| 2–4% | Sweet spot — balanced income + stability |
| 4–6% | Higher income, check payout ratio |
| 6%+ | Potentially unsustainable — proceed cautiously |

> ⚠️ A very high yield (8–12%+) is often a red flag — it can signal a company in trouble.

### Dividend Payout Ratio
**Formula:** Annual Dividend ÷ Earnings Per Share × 100

A payout ratio under **60%** is generally healthy. It means the company earns much more than it pays out, so dividends are sustainable.

### Dividend Growth Rate
Companies that grow their dividends consistently are more valuable than those with a static payout. Look for stocks with 5%+ annual dividend growth.

## Top Sectors for Dividend Income

- **Utilities** (NextEra Energy, Duke Energy) — stable, regulated income
- **REITs** (Realty Income, VICI Properties) — required by law to pay 90%+ of taxable income as dividends
- **Consumer Staples** (Procter & Gamble, Coca-Cola) — recession-resistant brands
- **Healthcare** (Johnson & Johnson, AbbVie) — aging population drives demand
- **Financial Services** (JPMorgan Chase, Visa) — consistent dividend growers

## Getting Started: A Simple Portfolio Strategy

### The Core-Satellite Approach
- **Core (60–70%):** Dividend ETFs like **VYM**, **SCHD**, or **DVY** — instant diversification
- **Satellite (30–40%):** Individual dividend stocks you research and believe in

### Dollar-Cost Average Into Positions
Don't try to time the market. Invest a fixed amount monthly, regardless of price. This smooths out volatility and lowers your average cost over time.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Chasing yield:** A 12% yield sounds amazing — until the dividend gets cut
2. **Ignoring diversification:** Don't put 50% in one stock or sector
3. **Not reinvesting:** Spending dividends early kills the compounding effect
4. **Panic selling during downturns:** Dividend stocks are long-term holds

---

## The Bottom Line

Dividend investing is one of the most accessible ways to build genuine passive income. You don't need to be a Wall Street expert — you need patience, consistent investment, and time.

Start small, reinvest everything, and let compounding work its magic. The investors who build real wealth from dividends didn't start rich — they started **early**.
