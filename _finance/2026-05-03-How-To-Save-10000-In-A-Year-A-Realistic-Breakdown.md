Saving $10,000 in a year sounds daunting. But when you break it down, it's $833/month, $192/week, or $27/day. Suddenly it's not a mountain — it's a math problem. Here's how to solve it.

## Step 1: Know Your Starting Point

Before you can save more, you need to know where your money is going now.

Spend 15 minutes pulling last month's bank and credit card statements. Categorize your spending into:
1. **Housing** (rent, utilities)
2. **Transportation** (car, gas, transit)
3. **Food** (groceries, dining out, coffee)
4. **Entertainment** (subscriptions, hobbies, shopping)
5. **Other** (everything else)

Most people are shocked by categories 3 and 4. Those are where the easy wins hide.

## Step 2: Find Your $833/Month

You need to save $833 every month. This can come from three sources:

### Source A: Cutting Spending

**Big wins (each worth $100-300/month):**
- Negotiate rent or find a cheaper option (-$200/month)
- Refinance car insurance with a different carrier (-$100/month)
- Cancel subscriptions you forgot about (-$50-100/month)
- Cook at home 3 more nights per week (-$150/month)
- Cut one premium habit (daily coffee shop, frequent dining out) (-$100-200/month)

**Small wins ($20-50/month each):**
- Switch to a no-fee bank account
- Drop unused gym membership
- Lower your phone plan
- Use cashback credit cards for regular spending

**Total realistic monthly savings from cuts: $400-600/month**

### Source B: Increasing Income

**Easiest options:**
- Ask for a raise (if it's been 12+ months since your last one)
- Sell unused items (clothes, electronics, furniture) — one-time boost of $200-1,000
- Pick up freelance work in your current skill set ($500-2,000/month)
- Deliver for DoorDash/Uber Eats evenings or weekends ($500-1,500/month)
- Rent out your car on Turo, or a room on Airbnb

**Target:** An extra $200-500/month from income increases

### Source C: Automation

The biggest savings mistake is trying to save what's "left over" at the end of the month. There's never anything left over.

**Instead: Pay yourself first.**
Set up an automatic transfer to a separate savings account on payday. Start with $200, increase by $50 each month.

## Step 3: Optimize Your Savings Account

Your $833/month should sit somewhere earning interest, not in a checking account.

**Options by interest rate:**
- **High-Yield Savings Account (HYSA):** 4-5% APY — best for easy access
- **Money Market Account:** Similar to HYSA with slightly different features
- **Treasury Bills (T-bills):** 4.5-5.5% — slightly higher yield, 4-week to 1-year maturity
- **CDs (Certificates of Deposit):** Lock in a rate for 6-12 months

At 5% APY, your $10,000 goal earns an extra **$275** just in interest if you average $5,000 in the account over the year. Free money.

## Common Obstacles and How to Beat Them

**"I can't cut anything — I'm already tight"**
Track your spending for 30 days first. Most people discover $200-400/month in "invisible" spending they don't consciously value.

**"I tried before and gave up"**
Start smaller: commit to saving just $100/month. Win the habit, then scale up. The psychological momentum matters more than the initial amount.

**"An emergency wiped out my savings"**
That's why we save. Rebuild to $1,000 first (mini emergency fund), then resume the $10k goal. Don't let setbacks reset your motivation.

**"I got the money but then spent it"**
Keep the savings in a separate account at a different bank. Out of sight, out of mind. Add friction: no debit card for that account, require 2-3 business days to transfer.

## The Mindset Shift

Saving $10,000 isn't really about having more money to save. For most people earning a decent income, the money exists — it's just being redirected elsewhere.

The goal is to redirect it intentionally, before it disappears.

**Three rules that make this work:**
1. **Automate before you rationalize.** Set the transfer before you decide you need the money.
2. **Track weekly, not monthly.** Monthly reviews catch problems too late.
3. **Celebrate milestones.** At $2,500, $5,000, and $7,500 — acknowledge progress. Small rewards keep motivation alive.
