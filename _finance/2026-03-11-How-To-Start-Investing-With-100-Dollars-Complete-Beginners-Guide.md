## Why $100 Is Enough to Start

Thanks to fractional shares, robo-advisors, and commission-free trading, the barrier to investing has never been lower. The most important thing is **starting** — not the amount.

> "The best time to plant a tree was 20 years ago. The second best time is now." — Chinese Proverb

A single $100 investment growing at 10% annually becomes:
- **$259** in 10 years
- **$673** in 20 years
- **$1,745** in 30 years

Now imagine adding $100 every month. You'd have **over $200,000** in 30 years.

## Step 1: Build a $500–$1,000 Emergency Fund First

Before investing a single dollar, make sure you have 1–3 months of expenses in a high-yield savings account (HYSA). Why?

- Markets can drop 30–50% temporarily
- Without an emergency fund, you'd be forced to sell at a loss
- HYSAs currently pay 4–5% — that's a guaranteed return!

**Where to keep it:** Marcus by Goldman Sachs, Ally Bank, or SoFi (all offering ~4.5% APY in 2026).

---

## Step 2: Take Free Money First (401k Match)

If your employer offers a 401(k) match, **this is a 50–100% instant return on your investment**. No investment beats free money.

- Contribute at least enough to get the full employer match
- Even $50/month gets you the match if your employer matches 100%

## Step 4: Automate It

The secret to wealth-building isn't timing the market — it's **time in the market**, consistently.

Set up automatic investments:
1. Open a brokerage account (Fidelity, Schwab, or Robinhood)
2. Link your bank account
3. Set a recurring weekly or monthly auto-buy
4. Start with $25/week = $100/month

This is called **dollar-cost averaging (DCA)** — you automatically buy more shares when prices are low and fewer when prices are high.

## Common Beginner Mistakes to Avoid

**❌ Waiting for the "right time" to invest**
There's never a perfect moment. Time in the market beats timing the market.

**❌ Investing in individual stocks with $100**
With limited capital, a single company failure could wipe you out. Stick to diversified ETFs.

**❌ Checking your portfolio daily**
Markets fluctuate. Checking daily causes emotional decisions. Check quarterly at most.

**❌ Selling during market downturns**
Downturns are sales, not disasters. The S&P 500 has recovered from every single crash in history.

**❌ Ignoring fees**
A 1% fee vs 0.03% (Vanguard ETFs) costs you tens of thousands over 30 years.

## Tax-Advantaged Accounts: Multiply Your Returns

Once you're comfortable, maximize these accounts **before** taxable investing:

### Roth IRA (Best for Most Young Investors)
- 2026 contribution limit: $7,000/year ($8,000 if 50+)
- Tax-free growth AND tax-free withdrawals in retirement
- Can withdraw contributions (not earnings) penalty-free anytime

### Traditional IRA
- Tax deduction now, pay taxes in retirement
- Same contribution limits as Roth
- Better if you expect lower income in retirement

### 401(k)
- 2026 limit: $23,500 ($31,000 if 50+)
- Pre-tax contributions reduce your taxable income today
- Always get the employer match first
