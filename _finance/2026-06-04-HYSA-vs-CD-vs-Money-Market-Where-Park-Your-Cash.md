For years, keeping money in savings felt pointless — 0.01% APY on a typical bank account barely covered inflation. That changed when interest rates rose. Now your cash can actually *work* for you.

But where should you put it? Let's break down the three main options.

## The Three Options at a Glance

| Feature | HYSA | CD | Money Market |
|---------|------|-----|-------------|
| Current APY | 4.5–5.0% | 4.5–5.5% | 4.5–5.0% |
| Liquidity | High (anytime) | Low (penalty to break) | High (anytime) |
| Rate changes | Variable | Fixed (locked in) | Variable |
| Minimum deposit | Usually $0–$1 | Varies ($0–$1,000+) | Often $1,000–$10,000 |
| FDIC insured | Yes | Yes | Usually (check) |
| Best for | Emergency fund | Known future expense | Large cash holdings |

## High-Yield Savings Accounts (HYSA)

**What it is:** A savings account that pays significantly more than traditional banks. Almost exclusively offered by online banks.

**Best current rates (2024):**
- SoFi: 4.60% APY
- Marcus by Goldman Sachs: 4.40% APY
- Ally Bank: 4.25% APY
- American Express HYSA: 4.30% APY

**Pros:**
- Full liquidity — transfer money anytime
- No penalty for withdrawals
- FDIC insured up to $250,000
- Rates adjust with Fed rate changes

**Cons:**
- Rates are variable — can drop when Fed cuts rates
- No physical branches
- Transfer times (1–3 business days to your main bank)

**Best for:** Emergency fund, short-term savings goals (vacation, car, etc.)

## Certificates of Deposit (CDs)

**What it is:** You deposit money for a fixed term (3 months to 5 years) and receive a fixed interest rate. Breaking early means a penalty (typically 3–6 months of interest).

**Current CD rates:**
- 6-month CD: ~5.2% APY
- 1-year CD: ~5.0% APY
- 2-year CD: ~4.5% APY
- 5-year CD: ~4.2% APY

**Pros:**
- Rate is **locked in** — won't drop if Fed cuts rates
- Slightly higher rates than HYSAs for most terms
- FDIC insured
- Forces you not to touch the money

**Cons:**
- Early withdrawal penalties
- Money is illiquid for the term
- You miss out if rates rise after locking in

### CD Ladder Strategy

Instead of putting all money into one CD, spread it across multiple terms:

| Amount | Term | Purpose |
|--------|------|---------|
| $5,000 | 3-month CD | Short-term buffer |
| $5,000 | 6-month CD | Medium buffer |
| $5,000 | 12-month CD | Best rate |
| $5,000 | 24-month CD | Locked-in rate |

When the 3-month CD matures, roll it into a new 24-month CD. Repeat. You always have money maturing while maximizing returns.

## Money Market Accounts (MMA)

**What it is:** A hybrid between checking and savings — higher rates than standard savings, with check-writing and debit card features.

Not to be confused with **money market funds** (investment products, not FDIC insured).

**Current MMA rates:**
- Discover Money Market: 4.25% APY
- Sallie Mae MMA: 4.65% APY
- UFB Direct: 5.15% APY

**Pros:**
- High liquidity with check/debit access
- Often higher rates than traditional banks
- FDIC insured
- Good for large cash balances

**Cons:**
- Often require higher minimum balances to earn top rates
- Variable rates like HYSAs
- Fewer options than the HYSA market

**Best for:** Large cash reserves, business accounts, or those who want occasional check-writing access

## How to Choose

### Use a HYSA if:
- You want simple, fully liquid savings
- You're building an emergency fund
- You're saving for something within 1–2 years

### Use a CD if:
- You know you won't need the money for a specific period
- You want to lock in today's rates before potential rate cuts
- You're saving for a known future expense (house down payment in 12 months, etc.)

### Use a Money Market Account if:
- You have a large cash balance ($10,000+)
- You want the ability to write checks occasionally
- You're a business owner managing operating cash

## Don't Leave Money in Traditional Savings

The national average savings rate at traditional banks: **0.46% APY**. The best HYSAs offer **10× more**.

On $20,000:
- Traditional savings at 0.46%: $92/year
- HYSA at 4.6%: $920/year

That's **$828/year of free money** you're leaving on the table. Moving your emergency fund to a HYSA is one of the fastest, easiest money moves you can make today.
