What if your investment account sent you a paycheck every month — without you selling anything? That's what dividend investing offers, and it's one of the most accessible forms of passive income available.

## What Are Dividends?

When a company earns profits, it can either reinvest that money or distribute it to shareholders. Dividends are those distributions — usually paid quarterly, sometimes monthly.

Example: Own 100 shares of a stock paying $2/share annually = **$200/year in dividends**.

## Why Dividend Investing Works

1. **Passive income** — money arrives without active effort
2. **Compounding** — reinvest dividends to buy more shares = more dividends
3. **Stability** — dividend-paying companies tend to be mature and stable
4. **Inflation hedge** — many companies raise dividends over time
5. **Lower stress** — you don't need to time the market

## Key Metrics Every Dividend Investor Should Know

### Dividend Yield
Annual dividend ÷ Stock price × 100

Example: $2 dividend / $50 stock price = **4% yield**

A "good" yield depends on context. 3–5% is generally healthy. Above 7–8% can signal risk.

### Dividend Payout Ratio
Annual dividend ÷ Earnings per share × 100

A payout ratio below 60% usually means the dividend is sustainable. Above 80% may be a warning sign.

### Dividend Growth Rate
How fast is the dividend increasing year over year? Companies that grow dividends consistently are called **Dividend Aristocrats** (S&P 500 companies that have raised dividends 25+ years straight).

## Best Types of Dividend Investments

### 1. Dividend ETFs (Best for Beginners)
Instant diversification across dozens or hundreds of dividend stocks.

- **SCHD** – Schwab US Dividend Equity ETF (~3.5% yield, strong growth)
- **VYM** – Vanguard High Dividend Yield (~3% yield)
- **DGRO** – iShares Dividend Growth (~2.5% yield, focus on growing dividends)

### 2. Dividend Stocks
Direct ownership of individual companies.

Well-known dividend payers:
- Johnson & Johnson (JNJ)
- Realty Income (O) — pays **monthly**
- Coca-Cola (KO) — 60+ years of consecutive dividend growth
- Procter & Gamble (PG)

### 3. REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts)
Required by law to distribute 90% of taxable income. Often yield 4–8%.

Popular REITs:
- **Realty Income (O)** — "The Monthly Dividend Company"
- **STAG Industrial**
- **Vanguard Real Estate ETF (VNQ)**

### 4. Dividend-Focused Mutual Funds
Good for retirement accounts, managed for income.

## How to Build a Starter Dividend Portfolio

**Starting with $5,000:**

| Allocation | Investment | Est. Yield |
|-----------|-----------|------------|
| 40% ($2,000) | SCHD (Dividend ETF) | 3.5% |
| 30% ($1,500) | VNQ (REIT ETF) | 4% |
| 20% ($1,000) | Individual dividend stock | 3-5% |
| 10% ($500) | Monthly dividend stock/fund | 4-6% |

**Estimated annual income:** ~$175–200
That's modest to start, but with reinvestment and regular contributions, it compounds dramatically.

## The Power of Dividend Reinvestment (DRIP)

Most brokerages offer **DRIP** (Dividend Reinvestment Program) — automatically reinvesting dividends to buy more shares.

$10,000 invested at 4% yield with 7% annual growth, reinvesting all dividends:

| Year | Portfolio Value | Annual Dividends |
|------|----------------|-----------------|
| 1 | $10,700 | $428 |
| 5 | $14,026 | $561 |
| 10 | $19,672 | $787 |
| 20 | $38,697 | $1,548 |
| 30 | $76,123 | $3,045 |

Time + reinvestment = real passive income.

## Common Dividend Investing Mistakes

**Chasing high yields:** A 12% yield often means the price has crashed or the payout isn't sustainable. Dividend cuts hurt twice — income drops AND the stock price usually falls.

**Ignoring dividend growth:** A 2% yield that grows 10% per year beats a static 5% yield over a decade.

**Not diversifying:** Don't put all your eggs in one high-dividend sector (like telecom or utilities).

**Forgetting taxes:** Qualified dividends are taxed at 0–20% (lower than income). Ordinary dividends are taxed as income. Hold in tax-advantaged accounts when possible.

## Getting Started Today

1. Open a brokerage account (Fidelity, Schwab, or Vanguard for low fees)
2. Start with a dividend ETF like SCHD
3. Enable DRIP
4. Contribute monthly and let it grow
5. Don't panic when markets dip — you're getting paid to wait
