If you've ever wondered where to start investing, the answer is almost always the same: **index funds**. They're simple, cheap, and consistently outperform most actively managed funds over the long run. Here's everything you need to know to get started.

---

## What Is an Index Fund?

An index fund is a type of investment fund that tracks a market index — like the **S&P 500**, which includes the 500 largest U.S. companies. Instead of picking individual stocks, you own a tiny slice of hundreds (or thousands) of companies at once.

## Why Index Funds Beat Most Investors

- **Low fees:** Expense ratios as low as 0.03% vs. 1%+ for active funds
- **Diversification:** One fund = hundreds of stocks
- **Consistent returns:** The S&P 500 has averaged ~10% annually over 90+ years
- **Less stress:** No need to pick winning stocks or time the market

> "Don't look for the needle in the haystack. Just buy the haystack." — John Bogle, founder of Vanguard

## How to Start in 4 Steps

### 1. Open a Brokerage Account
Choose a platform like **Fidelity**, **Vanguard**, or **Schwab** — all offer commission-free index fund trading.

### 2. Fund Your Account
Start with whatever you can afford. Even **$50/month** compounded over 30 years grows significantly.

### 3. Pick Your Fund
For most beginners, a **single total market fund** (like VTI) or an S&P 500 fund (like VOO) is all you need.

### 4. Automate Contributions
Set up automatic monthly investments. This is called **dollar-cost averaging** — you buy more shares when prices are low and fewer when high, smoothing out volatility.

Here's what investing $300/month in an S&P 500 index fund can do:

| Years | Total Invested | Value (at 10% avg) |
|-------|---------------|-------------------|
| 10 | $36,000 | ~$61,000 |
| 20 | $72,000 | ~$204,000 |
| 30 | $108,000 | ~$592,000 |

The longer you stay invested, the more dramatic the compounding effect.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Timing the market:** Nobody consistently predicts market highs and lows. Stay the course.
- **Checking daily:** Short-term volatility is noise. Think in decades.
- **Too many funds:** One or two diversified index funds is enough. Over-diversifying into 20 similar funds adds complexity without benefit.
- **Stopping during downturns:** Market dips are buying opportunities in disguise.

---

## Tax-Advantaged Accounts First

Always max out tax-advantaged accounts before taxable brokerage:
1. **401(k)** — especially if your employer matches (free money!)
2. **IRA (Roth or Traditional)** — $7,000/year limit (2026)
3. **Taxable brokerage** — no limits, but no tax benefits
