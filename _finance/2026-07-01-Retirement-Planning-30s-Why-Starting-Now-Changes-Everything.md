If you're in your 30s and haven't started saving for retirement, you're not alone — but you are leaving enormous amounts of money on the table. The good news: you still have time, and the math strongly favors those who start now.

If you *have* started, this guide will help you optimize what you're already doing.

## Why Your 30s Are the Critical Decade

Compound interest is often called the **eighth wonder of the world** — and the math justifies the hype.

**$10,000 invested at age 30 (7% average return):**
- At age 50: $38,697
- At age 60: $76,122
- At age 65: $106,766

**The same $10,000 invested at age 40:**
- At age 65: $54,274

Starting at 30 vs. 40 means **$52,000 more from a single $10,000 investment** — without adding another penny.

If you invest $500/month from age 30 to 65 at 7%:
- **Total contributions:** $210,000
- **Final value:** ~$905,000

If you wait until 40 to start the same $500/month:
- **Total contributions:** $150,000
- **Final value:** ~$461,000

Ten years of delay costs you **$444,000** despite only $60,000 less in contributions.

## Step 1: Know Your Numbers

**Target retirement savings by age (rough benchmarks):**
| Age | Target Savings (as multiple of salary) |
|---|---|
| 30 | 1x your annual salary |
| 35 | 2x |
| 40 | 3x |
| 45 | 4x |
| 50 | 6x |
| 55 | 7x |
| 60 | 8x |
| 65 | 10–12x |

If you earn $70,000/year, you should have ~$70,000 saved by 30 and ~$700,000–$840,000 saved by retirement.

**The 4% Rule:** In retirement, you can safely withdraw 4% of your portfolio annually, adjusted for inflation, without depleting your principal over a 30-year retirement.

$1,000,000 × 4% = $40,000/year ($3,333/month)

**Your target nest egg:** Annual retirement expenses × 25

If you need $60,000/year in retirement: $60,000 × 25 = **$1,500,000 target**

## Step 2: Use All Tax-Advantaged Accounts First

**Priority order:**

**1. 401(k) up to employer match (ALWAYS)**
If your employer matches 3%, contribute at least 3%. This is an **instant 50%–100% return** on your money. No investment beats it.

**2. Health Savings Account (HSA) if eligible**
The triple tax advantage: contributions are pre-tax, growth is tax-free, withdrawals for medical expenses are tax-free. After 65, withdraws for any purpose taxed like a traditional IRA.
- 2026 contribution limit: $4,300 (individual), $8,550 (family)

**3. Roth IRA (max it out)**
- 2026 contribution limit: $7,000 ($8,000 if 50+)
- Income limit: phase-out begins at $150,000 (single), $236,000 (married)
- Tax-free growth and tax-free withdrawals in retirement

**4. Max out 401(k)**
- 2026 limit: $23,500
- Pre-tax contributions reduce your taxable income now

**5. Taxable brokerage account**
After maxing tax-advantaged accounts, invest in low-cost index ETFs.

## Step 3: Asset Allocation for Your 30s

**General rule:** 100 minus your age = percentage in stocks
- Age 30: 70% stocks, 30% bonds

**But many financial planners now suggest more aggressive allocations in your 30s:**
- Age 30: 90% stocks, 10% bonds
- Age 40: 80% stocks, 20% bonds

**Rationale:** With 30+ years until retirement, short-term volatility is irrelevant. The real risk is not having enough money, not losing some of it temporarily.

**Simple 30-something portfolio:**
- 60% US total market (VTI)
- 30% International (VXUS)
- 10% Bonds (BND)

Or even simpler: a **Target Date Fund** (2055 or 2060) that automatically adjusts allocation as you age.

## Step 4: How Much to Save

**Minimum to stay on track:** 15% of gross income (including employer match)

**Aggressive wealth-building:** 20%–25%

**FIRE (Financial Independence, Retire Early) path:** 40%–60%

## Step 5: Protect Your Retirement Savings

**Don't raid your 401(k):**
- 10% early withdrawal penalty (before 59½)
- Income taxes on the amount withdrawn
- Lost compound growth

**Consider these protections:**
- **Term life insurance:** If others depend on your income, protect them (20–30 year term)
- **Disability insurance:** 70% of workers will experience a disability before retirement that affects their work
- **Estate planning:** Basic will, beneficiary designations on all accounts

## Common Mistakes in Your 30s

**1. Waiting for the "right time" to start**
The right time was yesterday. The second-best time is today.

**2. Cashing out 401(k) when changing jobs**
Roll it over to your new employer's plan or an IRA. Never take the cash.

**3. Being too conservative with investments**
A 35-year-old putting retirement savings in bonds is systematically destroying their future wealth through under-growth and inflation.

**4. Ignoring fees**
A fund with 1% annual fee vs. 0.05% can cost you $200,000+ over 30 years. Check every fund's expense ratio.

**5. Not adjusting contributions as income rises**
Every raise should include a contribution increase. Try "save half of every raise."

## The Mental Framework

Retirement saving in your 30s isn't about sacrifice — it's about **paying your future self first**.

Every dollar you invest at 30 works for you for 35 years. Every dollar you invest at 50 only works for 15 years. The leverage is incredible.

Your 30s are the decade where the choices you make will feel relatively small today but compound into life-defining outcomes by your 60s. The cost of action now is tiny. The cost of inaction is enormous.

Start. Automate. Increase with each raise. Don't touch it.
