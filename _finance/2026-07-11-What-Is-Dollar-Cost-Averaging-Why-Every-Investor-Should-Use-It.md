If you've ever hesitated to invest because you weren't sure if "now is a good time," dollar-cost averaging (DCA) is your answer.

It's not flashy. It's not complicated. It's not even exciting. But it's one of the most **consistently effective investment strategies** ever studied — and it's perfectly suited for everyday investors who don't have the time or expertise to time the market.

## What Is Dollar-Cost Averaging?

Dollar-cost averaging means **investing a fixed amount of money at regular intervals, regardless of price**.

Instead of trying to invest $12,000 at the perfect moment once a year, you invest **$1,000 every month** — whether the market is up, down, or sideways.

That's it. Seriously.

## Why DCA Works: The Math Behind It

When prices are **low**, your fixed $1,000 buys **more shares**.  
When prices are **high**, your fixed $1,000 buys **fewer shares**.

Over time, this naturally lowers your average cost per share compared to investing a lump sum at a potentially bad time.

### Example:

| Month | Share Price | Shares Bought with $1,000 |
|---|---|---|
| January | $50 | 20 shares |
| February | $40 | 25 shares |
| March | $25 | 40 shares |
| April | $40 | 25 shares |
| May | $50 | 20 shares |
| **Total** | **avg $41** | **130 shares for $5,000** |

Average cost per share: **$38.46**  
Average market price over the period: **$41.00**

DCA bought shares at **$2.54 less than the average price** simply by staying consistent during the dip in March.

## DCA vs. Lump Sum: Which Is Better?

Research actually shows that **lump sum investing beats DCA about 2/3 of the time** over long periods, because markets trend upward.

But here's the thing: **most people don't have a lump sum waiting**. They have a paycheck arriving every two weeks.

DCA is the natural strategy for salaried workers investing regularly from income. And for those who *do* have a lump sum (inheritance, bonus, sale of an asset), DCA over 6–12 months reduces the psychological risk of investing everything right before a crash.

## The Real Superpower of DCA: Removing Emotion

Markets drop 10–20% every few years. When they do, most investors panic and sell — locking in losses and missing the recovery.

DCA removes this temptation. **You invest the same amount no matter what.** During the 2020 COVID crash, DCA investors who kept buying at the bottom made extraordinary returns when the market recovered.

The hardest part of investing isn't knowing what to buy. It's not selling when fear tells you to. DCA builds the habit of consistency that protects you from your own instincts.

## How to Set Up DCA in 10 Minutes

1. **Choose your account:** 401(k), IRA, or a regular brokerage account
2. **Choose your investment:** A broad market index fund (S&P 500, total market ETF)
3. **Choose your amount:** Whatever you can consistently invest each month
4. **Set up automatic investing:** Almost every brokerage allows automatic monthly purchases
5. **Don't touch it:** Review once a year, rebalance if needed, otherwise leave it alone

### Recommended ETFs for DCA:

- **VTI** (Vanguard Total Stock Market ETF) — 0.03% expense ratio
- **SCHB** (Schwab US Broad Market ETF) — 0.03% expense ratio

## DCA for Different Life Stages

**In your 20s:** Invest aggressively into stock index funds. Time is your greatest asset — small amounts now grow enormously over decades.

**In your 30s-40s:** Maintain stock-heavy DCA, begin adding some bond exposure as responsibilities grow.

**Near retirement (50s-60s):** Gradually shift DCA toward more conservative allocations — but don't stop investing.

**The key:** Never completely stop. Even $100/month sustained for 30 years, at 10% average returns, becomes **$226,000**.

## Common DCA Mistakes

❌ **Stopping during market downturns** — this is when DCA is most valuable  
❌ **Not automating** — manual investing leads to missed months  
❌ **Checking your portfolio daily** — causes anxiety and bad decisions  
❌ **Picking individual stocks** — defeats the purpose; stick to broad index funds  
❌ **Waiting until you have "enough" to start** — start with whatever you have  

## The Bottom Line

Dollar-cost averaging won't make you rich overnight. Nothing will. But it is the **closest thing to a guaranteed path** to building wealth over time — available to anyone with a bank account and a few hundred dollars a month.

The secret isn't which ETF you pick or what the market will do next month. The secret is **starting and never stopping**.
