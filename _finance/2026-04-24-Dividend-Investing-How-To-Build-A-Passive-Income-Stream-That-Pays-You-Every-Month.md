That's the power of dividend investing — and it's more accessible than most people think.

---

## What Is a Dividend?

A dividend is a **cash payment a company sends to its shareholders**, usually every quarter. When you own shares of a dividend-paying company, you get paid just for holding those shares.

Think of it like rent. When you own a rental property, tenants pay you monthly. When you own dividend stocks, corporations pay you quarterly (or monthly) — no tenants, no maintenance calls, no drama.

**Simple example:**
- You own 100 shares of a company
- The company pays a $2.00 annual dividend per share
- You receive **$200 per year**, automatically, deposited to your brokerage account

The more shares you own, the more income you receive.

## Why Dividend Investing Works

### 1. You Get Paid to Wait

Stock prices bounce up and down. But dividends? They're cash in hand. Even when the market drops 20%, many dividends keep arriving like clockwork.

### 2. Dividends Grow Over Time

The best dividend companies don't just maintain their payouts — they **increase them every year**. Companies like Johnson & Johnson, Coca-Cola, and Procter & Gamble have raised dividends for 25, 50, even 60+ consecutive years.

A share that pays $1 today might pay $2 in ten years. Your original investment gets a raise — automatically.

### 3. Compounding Accelerates Your Wealth

Reinvest your dividends to buy more shares, and those shares generate more dividends, which buy even more shares. This is called a **DRIP (Dividend Reinvestment Plan)** — and it turns a modest investment into a compounding machine over decades.

## Key Metrics to Understand

### Dividend Yield

Yield = Annual Dividend ÷ Stock Price

If a stock trades at $100 and pays $4/year in dividends, the yield is **4%**.

A higher yield seems better, but beware: a very high yield (8%+) often signals the company is struggling and the dividend may be cut.

**Sweet spot:** 2–5% yield from financially healthy companies.

### Payout Ratio

Payout Ratio = Dividends Paid ÷ Net Income

This tells you what percentage of profits the company pays out as dividends.

- **Under 60%:** Healthy — the company retains earnings to grow
- **60–80%:** Acceptable, but watch closely
- **Over 80%:** Risky — dividend may be unsustainable

### Dividend Growth Rate

How fast has the dividend increased over 5–10 years? A company growing its dividend 7–10% annually will double your income in roughly 7–10 years — without you buying a single additional share.

## Types of Dividend Investments

### Dividend Growth Stocks

Companies with long histories of increasing dividends. Often called **Dividend Aristocrats** (25+ years of consecutive increases) or **Dividend Kings** (50+ years).

Examples: Coca-Cola (KO), Realty Income (O), Johnson & Johnson (JNJ), Procter & Gamble (PG)

### High-Yield Stocks

Pay above-average dividends, often 4–7%. Utilities, consumer staples, and telecoms often fall here.

Examples: AT&T (T), Altria (MO), Duke Energy (DUK)

### REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts)

Required by law to distribute at least 90% of taxable income as dividends. Often pay 4–8% yields.

Examples: Realty Income (O), VICI Properties (VICI), American Tower (AMT)

### Dividend ETFs

Own hundreds of dividend stocks in a single fund. Lower risk than individual stocks, instant diversification.

Examples: VYM, SCHD, DVY, DGRO

## Building a Monthly Income Portfolio

Most companies pay dividends quarterly. With smart stock selection, you can **engineer monthly income**.

**Strategy — stagger your holdings:**
- January/April/July/October payers
- February/May/August/November payers
- March/June/September/December payers

Or simply buy **monthly dividend payers** like Realty Income (O) or certain covered call ETFs.

## How to Start With $1,000

You don't need a fortune to start. Here's a beginner approach:

1. **Open a brokerage account** — Fidelity, Schwab, or Vanguard (all commission-free)
2. **Start with a dividend ETF** like SCHD (Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF) — broad diversification, 3%+ yield
3. **Enable DRIP** — automatically reinvest all dividends
4. **Add consistently** — even $100/month compounds significantly over time
5. **Gradually add individual stocks** as you learn more

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Chasing the highest yield.** A 12% yield sounds amazing until the company cuts it in half. Focus on sustainable, growing dividends.

**Ignoring dividend growth.** A 2% yield that grows 10%/year will beat a 5% yield with no growth — in just 10–12 years.

**Not accounting for taxes.** Dividends are taxable (qualified dividends taxed at 0–20% for most). Hold dividend stocks in tax-advantaged accounts like IRAs when possible.

**Selling during downturns.** Dividend stocks often drop in market crashes. If the dividend is safe, the drop is a gift — you're buying income at a discount.
