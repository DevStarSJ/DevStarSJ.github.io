## What Is Dollar-Cost Averaging (DCA)?

Dollar-cost averaging means **investing a fixed dollar amount at regular intervals** — regardless of what the market is doing. Instead of trying to time the market ("buy low, sell high"), you invest the same amount every week or month, no matter the price.

**Example:**
- You invest $500/month into an S&P 500 index fund
- When the market is up, your $500 buys fewer shares
- When the market is down, your $500 buys more shares
- Over time, your average cost per share smooths out

This eliminates the paralysis of waiting for "the right time" — because there is no perfect time, and the best time to invest is almost always *right now*.

## What Are Index Funds?

An index fund is a fund that simply tracks a market index — like the S&P 500 (the 500 largest US companies) or the Total Stock Market. Instead of a manager trying to pick winners, the fund automatically holds every stock in the index.

**The key advantages:**
- **Instant diversification** — You own hundreds or thousands of companies with one fund
- **Ultra-low costs** — Expense ratios as low as 0.03%
- **Proven performance** — The S&P 500 has averaged ~10% annually over the past 90+ years
- **Tax efficiency** — Less trading means fewer taxable events

## Why DCA Into Index Funds Is So Powerful

### 1. It Removes Emotion From Investing

The biggest enemy of investment returns isn't market volatility — it's your brain. Fear makes you sell at the bottom. Greed makes you buy at the top. Studies show the average investor dramatically underperforms the market because of emotional decisions.

DCA automates the process. You set it up once, and the money flows in automatically. No decisions to make. No emotions to fight.

### 2. It Works in All Market Conditions

When markets crash (and they will), DCA investors *keep buying*. Those who bought during the 2008 crash, the 2020 COVID crash, and the 2022 bear market generated some of the best long-term returns.

> **The secret: bear markets are sales events for long-term investors.**

### 3. It Captures the Full Power of Compounding

This is why **starting early matters more than anything else**. Time is the most valuable asset in investing.

## How to Start DCA Into Index Funds

### Step 1: Open a Brokerage Account
- **Fidelity** — Best for beginners; fractional shares, no minimums
- **Vanguard** — Best for long-term, fund-focused investors
- **Charles Schwab** — Great platform, no minimums

### Step 2: Fund the Account
Link your checking account and make an initial deposit. You can start with as little as $1 at Fidelity with fractional shares.

### Step 3: Buy Your Index Fund
Search for your chosen fund (e.g., FXAIX, VOO, VTI) and purchase shares.

### Step 4: Automate It
Set up automatic investments — daily, weekly, or monthly. Most brokerages offer automatic investment scheduling. This is the "set it and forget it" step that makes DCA work.

### Step 5: Reinvest Dividends
Turn on automatic dividend reinvestment (DRIP). This compounds your returns by buying more shares with every dividend payment.

## DCA vs. Lump-Sum Investing

Research (including a famous Vanguard study) shows that **lump-sum investing beats DCA about 2/3 of the time** — because markets go up more than they go down.

However, DCA wins in important real-world scenarios:
- You don't have a lump sum available (most people invest from income)
- You're psychologically unable to invest a large sum at once without fear
- The market has been rising sharply and you're concerned about a correction

**Bottom line:** If you have a lump sum, invest it now. If you don't, DCA monthly from your income. Both are excellent strategies.

## Common DCA Mistakes to Avoid

**❌ Stopping during market crashes**
This is the worst possible time to stop. You're buying shares at a discount. Stay the course.

**❌ Checking your portfolio too often**
Daily check-ins lead to emotional decisions. Check quarterly at most.

**❌ Switching funds constantly**
Pick your index fund(s) and stick with them for years. Every switch is a potential taxable event and costs you compounding time.

**❌ Waiting until you have "more" to invest**
$50/month started now beats $500/month started in 5 years. Start small. Start now.
