One of the most common questions in personal finance: **Roth IRA or Traditional IRA?** Both are powerful retirement accounts, but they work in fundamentally different ways. The right choice depends on where you are now — and where you expect to be in retirement.

## The Core Difference: When You Pay Taxes

The single biggest distinction between these two accounts is **when** you get your tax break.

- **Traditional IRA:** You contribute pre-tax dollars (tax deduction now), and pay taxes when you withdraw in retirement.
- **Roth IRA:** You contribute after-tax dollars (no deduction now), but qualified withdrawals in retirement are **completely tax-free**.

## 2026 Contribution Limits

For tax year 2026, the IRS allows:
- **$7,000/year** for individuals under 50
- **$8,000/year** for individuals 50 and older (catch-up contribution)

This limit applies to the **combined total** across all your IRAs — you can't contribute $7,000 to each.

## Roth IRA Income Limits

Not everyone can contribute to a Roth IRA directly. For 2026:

| Filing Status | Full Contribution | Phase-Out | No Contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single | Under $146,000 | $146K–$161K | Over $161,000 |
| Married Filing Jointly | Under $230,000 | $230K–$240K | Over $240,000 |

Traditional IRAs have no income limits for contributions (though deductibility phases out if you have a workplace plan).

## When Roth IRA Wins

**Choose Roth if you:**
1. **Expect to be in a higher tax bracket in retirement** — pay taxes now at a lower rate
2. **Are young and early in your career** — more years for tax-free growth
3. **Want flexibility** — Roth contributions (not earnings) can be withdrawn anytime penalty-free
4. **Want to avoid Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs)** — Roth IRAs have no RMDs during your lifetime

The math on Roth is compelling. If you invest $7,000 today in a Roth IRA and it grows to $70,000 over 30 years, you pay zero taxes on that $63,000 of growth.

## When Traditional IRA Wins

**Choose Traditional if you:**
1. **Are in your peak earning years** (high tax bracket now) — get the deduction while it's worth more
2. **Expect lower income in retirement** — pay taxes at a lower future rate
3. **Need the tax deduction now** to make the contribution affordable
4. **Are over 50** and catching up — the immediate deduction helps

## The Roth Conversion Strategy

Already have a Traditional IRA? You can **convert** it to a Roth IRA at any time — you'll owe income taxes on the converted amount, but then all future growth is tax-free. This works especially well in:
- Low-income years (career gap, early retirement)
- Years when the market is down (convert more shares for less tax)

## The Simple Decision Framework

| Your Situation | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Under 35, early career | Roth IRA |
| High earner, peak years | Traditional IRA |
| Unsure about future taxes | Split between both |
| Over income limit for Roth | Backdoor Roth IRA |
| Need the tax break now | Traditional IRA |

## The Backdoor Roth IRA (For High Earners)

If your income exceeds the Roth IRA limits, you're not locked out. The **backdoor Roth** strategy works like this:
1. Contribute to a non-deductible Traditional IRA
2. Convert it to a Roth IRA immediately

This is legal and widely used. Note: consult a tax advisor if you have existing Traditional IRA balances due to the "pro-rata rule."

## Don't Overthink It — Start Now

The honest truth? The difference between Roth and Traditional is significant, but **the biggest mistake is not contributing at all**. If you're agonizing over which to choose:

- If you're under 40: default to Roth
- If you're over 50 in a high tax bracket: default to Traditional
- If still unsure: split your contribution 50/50

Both accounts are infinitely better than a taxable brokerage account for retirement savings. Pick one, start contributing, and optimize later.

## Action Steps

1. **Open an IRA today** — Fidelity, Vanguard, and Schwab all offer free accounts with no minimums
2. **Set up automatic monthly contributions** — divide your annual limit by 12
3. **Invest in low-cost index funds** — don't let cash just sit in the account
4. **Review your choice annually** — as income changes, so might the optimal strategy

The best retirement account is the one you actually use. Start today.
