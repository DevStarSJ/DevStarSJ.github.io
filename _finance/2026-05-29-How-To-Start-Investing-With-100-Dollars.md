"I'll start investing when I have more money" is the most expensive sentence in personal finance. The truth is brutal: waiting costs you far more than starting small.

Here's exactly how to start investing with just $100.

## Why $100 Is Enough to Start

The math proves that starting early — even with tiny amounts — beats starting later with more money:

| Scenario | Start | Monthly | Years | Final Value |
|---------|-------|---------|-------|-------------|
| A | Age 25, $100/mo | $100 | 40 | ~$310,000 |
| B | Age 35, $200/mo | $200 | 30 | ~$243,000 |
| C | Age 45, $500/mo | $500 | 20 | ~$275,000 |

Scenario A wins *despite putting in the least money*. Time + compounding is the unfair advantage.

## Step 2: Choose What to Buy

With $100, you have several solid options:

### Option A: One Index ETF (Simplest)
**VOO (Vanguard S&P 500)** or **VTI (Total Market)** — put the entire $100 here. Done.

You now own tiny pieces of America's 500 largest companies.

### Option B: Three-Fund Portfolio
- $50 → VTI (US stocks)
- $30 → VXUS (International)
- $20 → BND (Bonds)

Instant global diversification with $100.

### Option C: Target Date Fund
Pick a fund matching your retirement year (e.g., "Target Date 2055 Fund"). It automatically adjusts allocation as you age. Pure autopilot.

**Avoid for now:**
- Individual stocks (too concentrated for small amounts)
- Crypto (high volatility, not for core investing)
- Penny stocks (very high risk, often scams)

## Step 3: Set Up Automatic Investing

This is the most important step most beginners skip.

Go to your brokerage → find "Automatic Investment" or "Recurring Buy" → set $50 or $100/month.

**Why automating matters:**
- Removes the need to remember
- Eliminates emotional timing decisions
- Buys both dips and peaks (dollar-cost averaging)
- Turns investing into a background habit

## Step 4: Ignore the Noise

Once you're invested, your job is to mostly *ignore* your portfolio.

You will see red. Multiple times. Deeply red. Markets drop 10%, 20%, even 50% periodically. This is normal.

**The investors who panic-sell during downturns permanently lock in losses. The investors who hold (or buy more) end up ahead.**

Delete the app if you can't stop checking it. Seriously.

## Common Beginner Mistakes to Avoid

**Waiting for the "right time"** — there is no right time. The best time was yesterday; the second best is today.

**Checking the portfolio daily** — this causes anxiety and bad decisions. Monthly at most.

**Diversifying with 20 funds** — one good index fund beats 20 mediocre funds. Simplicity wins.

**Stopping during market dips** — this is when you should continue (or increase). You're buying on sale.

**Investing money you need soon** — only invest money you won't need for at least 5 years.

## Building the Habit: The $100 Challenge

**Month 1:** Open account, invest $100 in VOO or VTI. Turn on $50/month automatic investing.

**Month 3:** Review — not to make changes, but to watch your investments grow and build the habit.

**Month 6:** Increase automatic investment to $100/month if possible.

**Year 1:** You've invested ~$700 and started a habit that will make you wealthy.

## What About Taxes?

**Use tax-advantaged accounts first:**
1. 401k (up to employer match) — employer match is free money
2. Roth IRA ($7,000/year limit) — tax-free growth forever
3. HSA if you have one — triple tax advantage

Only after maxing these should you use a regular taxable brokerage for investments.

If you're just starting with $100, a Roth IRA is the ideal home for your first investment.

## The Real Barrier Isn't Money

It's inertia. It's the login process. It's the form fields. It's the decision paralysis.

The investors who build wealth aren't smarter or higher-earning — they're the ones who actually started. Today.

Open the account. Put in $100. Set up automatic monthly investing. That's it. You're an investor now.
