In a world of volatile stocks and uncertain markets, U.S. Treasury securities offer a safe haven for your money. This guide explains everything you need to know about Treasury bonds, I-Bonds, and how to use them in your portfolio.

## What Are Treasury Securities?

Treasury securities are debt instruments issued by the U.S. government. When you buy a Treasury, you're essentially lending money to the government in exchange for interest payments.

### Why They're Considered Safe

- **Backed by the U.S. government**: Historically the safest investment
- **No credit risk**: The government can always pay its debts
- **Highly liquid**: Easy to buy and sell
- **Exempt from state/local taxes**: Only federal tax applies

## Types of Treasury Securities

### Treasury Bills (T-Bills)

- **Term**: 4 weeks to 1 year
- **Interest**: Sold at a discount, mature at face value
- **Minimum**: $100
- **Best for**: Short-term savings, cash parking

### Treasury Notes (T-Notes)

- **Term**: 2, 3, 5, 7, or 10 years
- **Interest**: Fixed rate, paid every 6 months
- **Minimum**: $100
- **Best for**: Medium-term goals, income generation

### Treasury Bonds (T-Bonds)

- **Term**: 20 or 30 years
- **Interest**: Fixed rate, paid every 6 months
- **Minimum**: $100
- **Best for**: Long-term investing, retirement portfolios

### TIPS (Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities)

- **Term**: 5, 10, or 30 years
- **Interest**: Fixed rate + inflation adjustment
- **Principal adjusts with CPI**
- **Best for**: Protecting against inflation

## I-Bonds: The Inflation Fighter

### What Makes I-Bonds Special

I-Bonds (Series I Savings Bonds) are designed specifically to protect against inflation:

- **Composite rate**: Fixed rate + inflation rate
- **Inflation adjustment**: Changes every 6 months
- **Tax advantages**: Federal tax only, can defer until redemption
- **Purchase limit**: $10,000 per person per year (electronic)

### Current I-Bond Rates (2026)

I-Bond rates are announced in May and November:
- **Fixed rate**: Set at purchase, never changes
- **Inflation rate**: Adjusts with CPI every 6 months
- **Composite rate**: Fixed + (2 × inflation) + (fixed × inflation)

### I-Bond Rules

**Holding Period**:
- Must hold minimum 1 year
- If redeemed before 5 years, lose 3 months of interest
- After 5 years, no penalty

**Purchase Limits**:
- $10,000 electronic per SSN per year
- $5,000 paper (with tax refund only)
- Additional via trust, business, or gift

**Redemption**:
- After 1 year minimum
- Any time after that
- Through TreasuryDirect.gov

## How to Buy Treasury Securities

### TreasuryDirect.gov

The official government platform:

1. Create an account at TreasuryDirect.gov
2. Link your bank account
3. Purchase directly at auction
4. No fees or commissions

### Through a Broker

Buy on the secondary market:
- Fidelity, Schwab, Vanguard, etc.
- May pay slight premium or discount
- More flexibility in buying/selling
- Can use in IRA accounts

### Treasury Auctions

New Treasuries are sold at auction:
- **Competitive bid**: Specify the rate you want
- **Non-competitive bid**: Accept the auction rate (recommended for individuals)

## Building a Treasury Ladder

A ladder strategy staggers maturity dates for flexibility and better rates.

### Example: 5-Year T-Note Ladder

| Year | Investment | Maturity |
|------|------------|----------|
| 2026 | $10,000 | 2027 |
| 2026 | $10,000 | 2028 |
| 2026 | $10,000 | 2029 |
| 2026 | $10,000 | 2030 |
| 2026 | $10,000 | 2031 |

**Benefits**:
- One note matures each year
- Reinvest at current rates
- Regular access to funds
- Reduced interest rate risk

### I-Bond Ladder

Since I-Bonds have a 1-year lockup:

- Year 1: Buy $10,000
- Year 2: Buy $10,000 (Year 1 bonds now accessible)
- Year 3: Buy $10,000 (Year 1 & 2 accessible)
- Continue annually

After 5 years, you have $50,000 in I-Bonds with no redemption penalty.

## Treasuries vs. Other Safe Investments

### Treasuries vs. CDs

| Factor | Treasuries | CDs |
|--------|-----------|-----|
| Safety | Government backed | FDIC insured |
| Liquidity | Can sell anytime | Early withdrawal penalty |
| Rates | Market determined | Bank sets rate |
| Taxes | State tax exempt | Fully taxable |
| Minimum | $100 | Varies ($500-$1000+) |

### Treasuries vs. Money Market Funds

| Factor | Treasuries | Money Market |
|--------|-----------|--------------|
| Return | Fixed or inflation-adjusted | Variable |
| Risk | No credit risk | Very low risk |
| Liquidity | Varies by type | High |
| FDIC | No (but government backed) | No |

### Treasuries vs. Corporate Bonds

| Factor | Treasuries | Corporate Bonds |
|--------|-----------|-----------------|
| Safety | Highest | Varies by rating |
| Yield | Lower | Higher |
| Default risk | Essentially zero | Possible |
| Liquidity | High | Varies |

## Tax Treatment of Treasuries

### Federal Taxes

- Interest is taxable as ordinary income
- Report on Schedule B
- Can defer I-Bond interest until redemption
- TIPS: Taxed on phantom income (inflation adjustment)

### State and Local Taxes

- **Exempt**: Treasury interest is not subject to state/local income tax
- This makes effective yield higher for high-tax state residents

### Tax-Advantaged Accounts

- Can hold in IRA/401(k) for tax deferral
- I-Bonds cannot be held in retirement accounts
- Municipal bonds may be better for taxable accounts in high brackets

## When to Use Treasuries

### Emergency Fund

I-Bonds after year 1:
- Better rates than savings accounts
- Inflation protection
- Still accessible

### Short-Term Goals (1-3 years)

T-Bills or short-term T-Notes:
- Known return
- No market risk if held to maturity
- Liquid if needed

### Retirement Portfolio

Mix of T-Notes, T-Bonds, TIPS:
- Reduce overall portfolio volatility
- Reliable income stream
- Inflation protection with TIPS

### Conservative Allocation

For risk-averse investors:
- Higher Treasury allocation (40-60% of portfolio)
- Provides stability during market downturns
- Lower but predictable returns

## Risks to Consider

### Interest Rate Risk

- Bond prices fall when rates rise
- Matters if selling before maturity
- Longer terms = more sensitive

### Inflation Risk

- Fixed-rate bonds lose purchasing power
- TIPS and I-Bonds address this
- Consider inflation expectations

### Opportunity Cost

- Lower returns than stocks long-term
- May not keep pace with inflation (except TIPS/I-Bonds)
- Balance with growth investments

## Getting Started: Action Plan

### Step 1: Open a TreasuryDirect Account

- Go to TreasuryDirect.gov
- Create account with SSN
- Link bank account
- Takes about 10 minutes

### Step 2: Buy Your First I-Bond

- Start with $100-$10,000
- Set up automatic purchases if desired
- Remember the 1-year lockup

### Step 3: Consider a Ladder

- Plan your maturity schedule
- Match to your financial goals
- Reinvest as bonds mature

### Step 4: Diversify

- Treasuries are one part of a portfolio
- Combine with stocks, other bonds
- Adjust allocation based on age and goals

## Final Thoughts

Treasury securities are the bedrock of safe investing. While they won't make you rich, they protect your capital and provide reliable income. Every investor should understand how to use them.

**Key takeaways**:
- I-Bonds offer inflation protection with no risk
- Treasury ladders provide flexibility and steady access
- State tax exemption boosts effective yield
- Perfect for emergency funds and conservative allocations
- Start at TreasuryDirect.gov—it's free and easy

In uncertain times, there's something to be said for investments backed by the full faith and credit of the United States government.
