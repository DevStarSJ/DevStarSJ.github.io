## What Is a High-Yield Savings Account?

A HYSA is a regular savings account that pays significantly more interest than traditional banks. They're offered primarily by:

- **Online banks** (no branch overhead = higher rates passed to customers)
- **Credit unions** (member-owned = better rates)
- **Fintech companies** (backed by FDIC-insured partner banks)

Key features:
- ✅ FDIC insured up to $250,000 (or NCUA for credit unions)
- ✅ No market risk — your balance never drops
- ✅ Liquid — access your money within 1–3 business days
- ✅ No lock-up period (unlike CDs)

## Should You Choose a HYSA or a CD?

**Choose a HYSA when:**
- You need flexibility to access funds anytime
- It's your emergency fund
- You're not sure of your timeline
- You want to avoid early withdrawal penalties

**Choose a CD when:**
- You won't need the money for a defined period (6 months, 1 year, 2 years)
- CD rates are higher than HYSA rates (check both)
- You want to lock in a rate before they drop

**Current best CD rates in 2026:**
- 6-month CD: ~5.0–5.2% APY
- 1-year CD: ~4.8–5.0% APY
- 2-year CD: ~4.5–4.8% APY

For emergency funds, always choose HYSA over CDs — you need that money to be accessible.

## How Much to Keep in a HYSA

The right HYSA balance depends on your situation:

**Minimum (basic safety net):** 1 month of expenses  
**Standard recommendation:** 3–6 months of expenses  
**Conservative/variable income:** 6–12 months of expenses

### How to Calculate Your Number

Monthly expenses include:
- Rent/mortgage
- Groceries and household supplies
- Utilities and internet
- Transportation
- Insurance premiums
- Minimum debt payments
- Childcare

**Example calculation:**
- Monthly expenses: $3,500
- 3-month emergency fund target: **$10,500**
- 6-month emergency fund target: **$21,000**

## The Emergency Fund Building Strategy

If you're starting from zero, here's how to build your HYSA fast:

**Step 1: Open a separate HYSA** (not at your main bank — out of sight, out of mind)

**Step 2: Set up automatic transfers**
- Transfer a fixed amount every payday (even $50 helps)
- Make it automatic so it requires effort to stop

**Step 3: Funnel windfalls**
- Tax refund → emergency fund
- Work bonus → emergency fund
- Birthday money → emergency fund

**Step 4: Temporary spending freeze**
- 30-day no-restaurant challenge → save $200–400
- Cancel unused subscriptions → save $50–150/month
- Sell unused items → $200–1,000 one-time boost

## Common HYSA Mistakes to Avoid

**❌ Keeping your emergency fund at your main bank**
Convenience makes it tempting to spend. Use a separate bank for psychological separation.

**❌ Chasing the highest rate relentlessly**
0.1% difference on $10,000 = $10/year. Not worth switching for tiny differences. Stability matters too.

**❌ Using your emergency fund for non-emergencies**
A sale at your favorite store is not an emergency. A job loss, medical bill, or car breakdown is.

**❌ Stopping contributions after reaching your goal**
Keep contributing beyond your goal — the excess becomes your investment buffer.

**❌ Forgetting to update your emergency fund as expenses grow**
If your monthly expenses go up, your 3–6 month target should too. Review annually.

## Tax Implications of HYSA Interest

Interest earned in HYSAs is taxable as ordinary income. At 4.90% APY:

| Balance | Annual Interest | Tax (24% bracket) | Net After Tax |
|---------|----------------|-------------------|---------------|
| $5,000 | $245 | $59 | $186 |
| $10,000 | $490 | $118 | $372 |
| $25,000 | $1,225 | $294 | $931 |
| $50,000 | $2,450 | $588 | $1,862 |

You'll receive a **1099-INT** from your bank at tax time. Still much better than earning 0.01%.

**Tip:** If you're in the 32%+ tax bracket, consider **I-bonds** or **Treasury bills** (state tax-exempt) for very large emergency fund balances.
