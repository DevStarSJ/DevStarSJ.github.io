## What Is the Stock Market?

The stock market is a marketplace where investors buy and sell shares of publicly traded companies. When you buy a stock, you're purchasing a small piece of ownership in that company.

### Key Terms You Need to Know

- **Stock/Share**: A unit of ownership in a company
- **Dividend**: A portion of company profits paid to shareholders
- **Portfolio**: Your collection of investments
- **Bull Market**: When stock prices are rising
- **Bear Market**: When stock prices are falling
- **Market Cap**: Total value of a company's outstanding shares

## How Does the Stock Market Work?

### The Basics

1. Companies go public through an IPO (Initial Public Offering)
2. Shares are then traded on stock exchanges
3. Prices fluctuate based on supply and demand
4. Investors profit through price appreciation and dividends

### Major Stock Exchanges

- **NYSE (New York Stock Exchange)**: Largest in the world
- **NASDAQ**: Known for tech companies
- **S&P 500**: Index of 500 largest US companies
- **Dow Jones**: Index of 30 major US companies

## Why Invest in Stocks?

### Historical Returns

Over the long term, stocks have outperformed most other investments:

- **Average annual return (S&P 500)**: ~10% since 1926
- **Bonds**: ~5-6% average return
- **Savings accounts**: 1-4% (often below inflation)

### Beat Inflation

With inflation averaging 2-3% annually, keeping money in savings accounts actually loses purchasing power. Stocks help your money grow faster than inflation.

## Types of Stocks

### By Company Size

- **Large-cap**: Companies worth $10+ billion (e.g., Apple, Microsoft)
- **Mid-cap**: Companies worth $2-10 billion
- **Small-cap**: Companies worth $250 million - $2 billion

### By Investment Style

- **Growth stocks**: Companies expected to grow faster than average
- **Value stocks**: Undervalued companies trading below their worth
- **Dividend stocks**: Companies that regularly pay dividends

### By Sector

- Technology
- Healthcare
- Financial services
- Consumer goods
- Energy
- Real estate

## How to Start Investing

### Step 1: Set Your Goals

Ask yourself:
- What am I investing for? (Retirement, house, education)
- What's my time horizon? (5, 10, 30 years)
- How much can I invest regularly?

### Step 2: Understand Your Risk Tolerance

Your risk tolerance depends on:
- Age (younger = more risk capacity)
- Income stability
- Financial goals
- Emotional comfort with volatility

### Step 3: Open a Brokerage Account

Popular options include:
- **Fidelity**: Great research tools
- **Charles Schwab**: Excellent customer service
- **Vanguard**: Low-cost index funds
- **Robinhood**: Commission-free, user-friendly

### Step 4: Start Small and Learn

- Begin with index funds or ETFs
- Invest consistently (dollar-cost averaging)
- Don't try to time the market
- Reinvest dividends

## Investment Strategies for Beginners

### 1. Buy and Hold

- Purchase quality stocks/funds
- Hold for years or decades
- Ignore short-term volatility
- Let compound growth work

### 2. Dollar-Cost Averaging

- Invest fixed amounts regularly
- Buy more shares when prices are low
- Buy fewer shares when prices are high
- Reduces timing risk

### 3. Index Investing

- Buy index funds that track the market
- Low fees (0.03-0.20%)
- Instant diversification
- Beats most active managers long-term

## Understanding Stock Prices

### What Moves Stock Prices?

- **Company earnings**: Strong profits = higher prices
- **Economic conditions**: GDP, employment, interest rates
- **Industry trends**: Sector-specific news
- **Market sentiment**: Investor emotions and expectations

### Key Metrics to Evaluate Stocks

| Metric | What It Tells You |
|--------|-------------------|
| P/E Ratio | Price relative to earnings |
| EPS | Earnings per share |
| Dividend Yield | Annual dividend as % of price |
| Market Cap | Total company value |
| 52-Week Range | Price range over past year |

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### 1. Emotional Investing

- Don't panic sell during downturns
- Don't FOMO buy during hype
- Stick to your strategy

### 2. Lack of Diversification

- Don't put all eggs in one basket
- Spread across sectors and asset classes
- Consider international exposure

### 3. Trying to Time the Market

- Nobody consistently predicts market movements
- Time in the market beats timing the market
- Stay invested through volatility

### 4. Ignoring Fees

- High fees eat into returns
- Compare expense ratios
- Avoid unnecessary trading

### 5. Not Having a Plan

- Define your goals
- Set an asset allocation
- Rebalance periodically

## Building Your First Portfolio

### A Simple Starter Portfolio

- **60%**: Total US Stock Market Index Fund
- **30%**: International Stock Index Fund
- **10%**: Bond Index Fund

### For More Aggressive Growth (Younger Investors)

- **80%**: Stock funds (US + International)
- **20%**: Bonds or stable assets

### For Conservative Investors

- **40%**: Stock funds
- **60%**: Bonds and stable assets
