Real estate has created more millionaires than almost any other asset class. Yet many people assume it's out of reach — you need hundreds of thousands of dollars, a real estate license, or years of experience. None of that is true.

Here's how to start building wealth through property, even if you're starting from scratch.

## Why Real Estate?

Real estate offers something rare: **multiple simultaneous returns**.

1. **Cash flow** — monthly rental income after expenses
2. **Appreciation** — property values rise over time
3. **Equity buildup** — tenants pay down your mortgage
4. **Tax advantages** — depreciation, deductions, 1031 exchanges
5. **Inflation hedge** — rents and values typically rise with inflation

Stocks offer appreciation. Bonds offer income. Real estate offers both — plus leverage and tax benefits.

## Option 1: REITs (Lowest Barrier to Entry)

A **Real Estate Investment Trust (REIT)** lets you invest in real estate with as little as $10.

REITs are companies that own income-producing properties — apartments, office buildings, shopping centers, warehouses. By law, they must distribute **90% of taxable income** as dividends.

**How to invest:**
- Buy REIT shares through any brokerage account
- Look for diversified REITs like Vanguard Real Estate ETF (VNQ)
- Dividend yields typically range from 3–6%

**Best for:** People who want real estate exposure without managing property.

## Option 2: House Hacking

House hacking is the gateway drug to real estate investing.

**The concept:** Buy a property, live in one unit, rent out the others. Your tenants cover your mortgage — or even pay you to live there.

**Example:**
- Buy a duplex for $300,000
- Put 5% down ($15,000) using an FHA loan
- Rent the other unit for $1,400/month
- Your mortgage: $1,600/month
- You pay only $200/month to live there

Over 2–3 years, you save money, build equity, and learn the landlord business from the safest possible position.

## Option 3: Long-Term Rental Properties

The classic wealth-building play. Buy a property, rent it out, collect monthly cash flow.

**The 1% Rule:** A good rental property generates monthly rent equal to at least 1% of its purchase price.
- $200,000 property → $2,000/month rent = solid deal
- $200,000 property → $1,000/month rent = likely cash-flow negative

**Key expenses to calculate:**
- Mortgage (principal + interest)
- Property taxes
- Insurance
- Maintenance (budget 1% of property value per year)
- Vacancy (assume 5–8% of annual rent)
- Property management (8–10% if you hire a manager)

## Option 4: Real Estate Crowdfunding

Platforms like **Fundrise**, **RealtyMogul**, and **CrowdStreet** let you invest in commercial real estate deals for as little as $500–$1,000.

You pool money with other investors to buy apartment complexes, office buildings, or development projects. Returns typically range from 7–12% annually.

**Pros:** Passive, diversified, low minimum  
**Cons:** Illiquid (money is locked up 3–7 years), less control

## The Numbers That Matter

Before any purchase, run these calculations:

**Net Operating Income (NOI):**
Annual rent – Operating expenses (excluding mortgage) = NOI

**Cap Rate:**
NOI ÷ Property value = Cap Rate
Aim for 5–8% in most markets.

**Cash-on-Cash Return:**
Annual cash flow ÷ Total cash invested
Target: 8–12%

## Common Beginner Mistakes

**Underestimating expenses.** New investors forget vacancy, repairs, and management costs. Always budget conservatively.

**Overpaying out of excitement.** The profit is made at purchase, not at sale. Don't fall in love with a property.

**Skipping due diligence.** Get a proper inspection. Review financials. Check the neighborhood's rental demand.

**Not having reserves.** Keep 3–6 months of expenses in cash per property. Roofs fail. Tenants leave. Boilers break.

## Getting Started This Week

1. **Educate yourself:** Read *The Book on Rental Property Investing* by Brandon Turner
2. **Analyze your market:** Research rental rates, vacancy rates, and price-to-rent ratios in your area
3. **Open a Fundrise account:** Start with $500 to get a feel for real estate returns
4. **Talk to a mortgage broker:** Find out what you qualify for
5. **Run 10 deals on paper:** Practice analyzing properties before buying anything

Real estate isn't passive at the start — it takes work to learn and find good deals. But once you own a well-chosen property, it can generate income for decades with minimal effort.
