## What Is the FIRE Movement?

FIRE stands for **Financial Independence, Retire Early**. It's not just about retiring young — it's about building enough wealth that *work becomes optional*.

The movement is built on two principles:
1. **Save aggressively** (often 50–70% of income)
2. **Invest in low-cost index funds** to let compound growth do the heavy lifting

When your investment portfolio is large enough to fund your lifestyle indefinitely through passive returns, you've reached **financial independence** — and can retire on your own terms.

---

## The Math Behind FIRE

### The 4% Rule
The FIRE community relies heavily on the **4% rule**, based on the landmark Trinity Study. It found that if you withdraw 4% of your portfolio per year, a well-diversified portfolio has historically lasted 30+ years.

**Your FIRE Number:**
> Annual Expenses × 25 = Your FIRE Number

**Example:**
- Annual expenses: $40,000
- FIRE number: $40,000 × 25 = **$1,000,000**

Reach $1 million invested, withdraw $40,000/year (4%), and your money theoretically lasts forever.

### Savings Rate Is Everything
The biggest factor in how quickly you reach FIRE isn't your income — it's your **savings rate**.

| Savings Rate | Years to FIRE |
|---|---|
| 10% | ~43 years |
| 25% | ~32 years |
| 50% | ~17 years |
| 65% | ~11 years |
| 75% | ~7 years |

*Assumes 7% average annual investment returns and starting from zero.*

## Types of FIRE

### Lean FIRE
Living on minimal expenses — often under $25,000/year. Requires extreme frugality but a much smaller nest egg (~$625,000).

**Best for:** People who genuinely enjoy simple, low-cost living.

### Fat FIRE
Retiring with enough to maintain a comfortable, affluent lifestyle — typically $100,000+/year in expenses. Requires $2.5M+ invested.

**Best for:** Those who don't want to sacrifice lifestyle in retirement.

### Barista FIRE
Retire from your career but work part-time (like at a coffee shop) for supplemental income and benefits. You need less saved because you're not relying 100% on investments.

**Best for:** People who want structure and social connection but don't want a demanding career.

### Coast FIRE
You've invested enough that, if you just leave it alone, compound growth will reach your FIRE number by traditional retirement age. You can "coast" — work a low-stress job to cover current expenses without saving more.

**Best for:** Younger people who front-loaded their savings early.

## How to Pursue FIRE: Step by Step

### 1. Calculate Your Current Annual Expenses
Track everything for 3 months. This is your baseline for calculating your FIRE number.

### 2. Eliminate High-Interest Debt
Credit card debt at 20% APR will torpedo any investment strategy. Pay it off aggressively first.

### 3. Max Tax-Advantaged Accounts
- **401(k):** $23,000/year limit (2024) — especially contribute enough to get employer match
- **Roth IRA:** $7,000/year — tax-free growth, perfect for FIRE
- **HSA:** $4,150/year — triple tax advantage, use as a stealth retirement account

### 4. Invest in Low-Cost Index Funds
FIRE followers typically invest in total market index funds:
- **VTI** (Vanguard Total Stock Market ETF) — expense ratio 0.03%
- **VXUS** (Vanguard Total International) — global diversification
- **BND** (Vanguard Total Bond Market) — stability as you approach FI

### 5. Increase Your Income
The fastest path to FIRE isn't extreme cutting — it's earning more. Side hustles, career advancement, and high-demand skills accelerate your timeline dramatically.

## The FIRE Lifestyle: Myths vs. Reality

| Myth | Reality |
|---|---|
| You'll be bored | Most FIRE retirees are busier than ever with projects they love |
| You need to be frugal forever | Many pursue fat FIRE with high spending |
| It's only for high earners | A 50% savings rate on $60k income beats 10% savings on $200k |
| You can't touch retirement accounts early | The Roth conversion ladder and 72(t) distributions solve this |

## The Psychological Side of FIRE

Many people who reach FIRE don't fully retire — they transition to work they genuinely love, on their own schedule. The goal isn't to never work; it's to never **need** to work.

Financial independence gives you:
- The power to say no to bad bosses
- Freedom to take career risks
- Time for family, health, and passion projects
- Peace of mind that a job loss isn't catastrophic

---

## Is FIRE Right for You?

FIRE isn't for everyone. It requires significant sacrifice in the short term for long-term freedom. But even if you don't want to retire at 35, the **principles** — high savings rate, low-cost investing, living below your means — will make you wealthier regardless.

Start with your FIRE number. Calculate your current savings rate. Then decide how aggressively you want to pursue it.

**The journey to financial independence is worth taking — even if you never fully "retire."**
