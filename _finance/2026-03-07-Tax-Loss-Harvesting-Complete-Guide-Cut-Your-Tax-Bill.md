# Tax-Loss Harvesting: The Legal Way to Cut Your Tax Bill Every Year

Here's something most investors don't know: you can use your losing investments to legally reduce what you owe in taxes — often by thousands of dollars per year.

This strategy is called tax-loss harvesting, and it's one of the most powerful (and underused) tools in personal finance. The wealthy have been using it for decades. With modern investment platforms, it's now accessible to anyone.

Here's exactly how it works.

## What Is Tax-Loss Harvesting?

Tax-loss harvesting is the practice of selling investments that have declined in value to "realize" a loss, then using that loss to offset capital gains elsewhere — reducing your taxable income.

You immediately reinvest the proceeds in a similar (but not identical) investment to maintain your market exposure.

**The result:** Your portfolio performance stays roughly the same, but your tax bill goes down.

---

## How Capital Gains Taxes Work

To understand tax-loss harvesting, you need to understand capital gains:

### Short-Term Capital Gains
- Assets held **less than 1 year**
- Taxed as **ordinary income** (10–37% depending on your bracket)
- Highest priority to offset with harvested losses

### Long-Term Capital Gains
- Assets held **more than 1 year**
- Taxed at **0%, 15%, or 20%** (depending on income)
- Most investors pay 15%

### Capital Losses Can Offset Gains
If you have $5,000 in capital gains and $3,000 in harvested losses, you only pay taxes on **$2,000 in net gains** — saving you $300–$600 in taxes at typical rates.

### The $3,000 Rule
If your losses exceed your gains, you can deduct up to **$3,000 per year** against ordinary income. Additional losses carry forward to future years indefinitely.

## A Real Example

Let's say it's December and your portfolio looks like this:

| Investment | Purchase Price | Current Value | Gain/Loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tech ETF | $10,000 | $14,000 | +$4,000 |
| Emerging Market ETF | $8,000 | $5,500 | -$2,500 |
| Real Estate REIT | $5,000 | $4,200 | -$800 |

**Without tax-loss harvesting:**
You'd owe taxes on $4,000 in gains when you sell the Tech ETF.
At 15% long-term rate: **$600 tax bill**

**With tax-loss harvesting:**
You sell both the Emerging Market ETF (-$2,500) and REIT (-$800) to harvest $3,300 in losses.

Net taxable gains: $4,000 - $3,300 = **$700**
Tax bill: $700 × 15% = **$105**

**Tax savings: $495**

You immediately buy similar (not identical) funds to replace both positions. Your portfolio allocation stays essentially the same — you just moved the tax liability to the future.

## The Wash-Sale Rule: The Critical Constraint

The IRS isn't naive. They created the **wash-sale rule** to prevent people from selling a security for a loss and immediately buying it back.

### The Rule:
If you sell a security at a loss, you **cannot buy the same or "substantially identical" security within 30 days before or after the sale** (61-day window total).

Violating the wash-sale rule disallows the loss — you lose the tax benefit.

### What Counts as "Substantially Identical"?
- The exact same stock or fund ✗
- A different fund tracking the same index from a different provider ✗ (likely)
- An ETF and mutual fund tracking the same index ✗ (probably)

### What's Generally Allowed (Similar, Not Identical):
- Selling Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO) → Buying iShares S&P 500 ETF (IVV) — Gray area
- Selling a US Total Market ETF → Buying an S&P 500 ETF — Generally OK
- Selling a tech sector ETF → Buying a different but related ETF — Generally OK
- Selling one bond ETF → Buying a similar but different duration bond ETF — Generally OK

**Important:** Consult a tax professional for your specific situation. IRS guidance on "substantially identical" can be ambiguous.

## When Tax-Loss Harvesting Makes the Most Sense

### High-Income Years
If you're in a high income bracket, your capital gains rate is higher, making each dollar of harvested loss more valuable.

### Years With Large Capital Gains
If you sold a business, received stock options, or had a large investment gain elsewhere, harvested losses can offset those gains directly.

### Market Downturns
Paradoxically, market crashes are harvesting opportunities. When broad markets drop, you can harvest losses while immediately buying back into similar positions — keeping market exposure while capturing tax benefits.

### Year-End Tax Planning (October–December)
Most investors harvest in Q4 when the full year's gains picture is clear. Starting in October gives you time to be strategic.

---

## When It Might Not Be Worth It

Tax-loss harvesting isn't always beneficial:

### Low-Income Years
If your income is low enough that you qualify for the **0% long-term capital gains rate**, harvesting losses provides little benefit — you weren't going to owe much anyway.

### Small Balances
Transaction costs, bid-ask spreads, and complexity may outweigh benefits for very small portfolios. Generally meaningful above $50,000–$100,000.

### IRA and 401(k) Accounts
Retirement accounts are tax-advantaged — there are no capital gains taxes to offset. Tax-loss harvesting only applies to **taxable brokerage accounts**.

### Short Holding Periods
If you're planning to sell soon anyway, realizing the loss now only defers the gain temporarily. The benefit is smaller.

## Automated Tax-Loss Harvesting (Robo-Advisors)

Several robo-advisors automate daily or weekly tax-loss harvesting:

- **Betterment** — offers automated TLH as a standard feature
- **Wealthfront** — known for sophisticated TLH including individual stock-level harvesting at higher balances
- **Schwab Intelligent Portfolios Premium** — includes TLH

The automation advantage: they scan your portfolio continuously, not just year-end. Small losses get captured before they potentially turn into gains.

**Trade-off:** You give up some control over your exact fund selection, and management fees apply.

## Do-It-Yourself Tax-Loss Harvesting Checklist

If you manage your own portfolio:

- [ ] Review taxable accounts each October–November
- [ ] Identify positions with unrealized losses
- [ ] Calculate whether losses exceed transaction costs
- [ ] Choose replacement securities that maintain allocation but avoid wash-sale rule
- [ ] Execute the trades within the same tax year
- [ ] Set calendar reminders for the 31-day wait before buying back the original security
- [ ] Track all harvested losses for your tax return (Form 8949)
- [ ] Consult a CPA if your situation is complex

## The Long-Term Math: Why This Matters

Tax-loss harvesting doesn't eliminate taxes — it **defers** them. When you eventually sell the replacement investment, you'll owe taxes based on the original (lower) cost basis.

But deferral has real value:

**Example:** You harvest $10,000 in losses, saving $1,500 in taxes today. You invest that $1,500 at 8% annual return for 20 years.

Future value: $1,500 × (1.08)²⁰ = **$6,991**

Even after eventually paying taxes on the deferred gains, you've likely come out significantly ahead due to the compounding effect on the deferred tax dollars.

## Tax-Loss Harvesting vs. Other Tax Strategies

| Strategy | When It Helps |
|---|---|
| Tax-loss harvesting | Ongoing — offset gains with losses |
| Tax-gain harvesting | Low-income years — realize gains at 0% rate |
| Asset location | Always — hold bonds in IRA, stocks in taxable |
| Roth conversion | Low-income years — convert at lower rates |
| Charitable giving | High-income years — donate appreciated stock |

Tax-loss harvesting is most powerful combined with other strategies. A tax advisor can help coordinate them.

## The Bottom Line

Tax-loss harvesting is one of the few strategies where you genuinely get something for "free" — you're turning market volatility into a tax advantage without fundamentally changing your investment exposure.

The wash-sale rule requires careful execution, but the mechanics aren't complicated. For anyone with a taxable investment account of meaningful size, this is worth understanding and implementing.

Start simple: review your portfolio for losses each fall. If you have gains elsewhere, harvest the losses. That one habit can easily save you hundreds to thousands of dollars per year — every year.

The market will always have volatility. You might as well get paid for it.
